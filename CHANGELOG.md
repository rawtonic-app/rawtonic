# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Colour noise reduction leaves fewer colour specks at its default (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 10, new edits and never-edited RAWs): each pixel's filtered colour is now checked against its neighbours' plain filtered colours only, no longer against the side of a colour edge its own noisy colour picked, with a margin of 1.25 instead of 1.5 times the noise. At Color 25 the coarser scales keep a little colour noise, and in flat areas the filter kept single pixels' noisy colour within a range that included their own choice; those pixels no longer stand out. On 55 high-ISO RAWs, colour specks fell from 7.5 to 5.0 per million pixels at Color 25 (Capture One's default and Camera Raw 5.4), from 4.2 to 3.5 at Color 50 (Camera Raw 4.7) and from 4.1 to 3.1 at Color 100 (Camera Raw 4.6, Capture One at 100 1.4), with more specks on at most 2 of the 55 files; colour edges stayed within 0.12 % of version 9 in every ISO band, the error against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) fell or stayed in every cell, and fine colour detail fell by about 1 % at ISO 1600–2500 and 6400. Per ISO band still above the better editor from ISO 1600 to 6400 at every Color (below both from 12800 up), and above Capture One at Color 100 overall. Saved edits render exactly as before; at Color 0 version 10 renders as version 9.
- Folders can be moved with everything in them: **Move Folder…** in a folder's menu in Folders picks where to. On the same drive it is a rename; to another drive the Library asks first, naming the folder, its photos, files and size, the drive and its free space. The whole folder, files the library does not know included, is copied next to its new place, every file synced, read back from the drive and compared byte for byte, and then the folder appears under its name at once, never replacing anything. The original folder goes to the Trash of its drive only while it still holds exactly what was copied; one that changed meanwhile, or whose drive has no Trash, stays where it is and the Library says so. The catalog, previews and albums follow, as for a renamed folder. A crash, a failure or Stop before the folder appears changes nothing; Undo copies the folder back as it is then (edits and files added since included) and Redo moves it again. Folders with links or other drives in them, folders holding a session or catalog, and moves of a folder into or out of a session are refused (ADR 0016).
- Save & Relaunch in Preferences (after changing a setting that needs a restart) restarts the app again: it quit and did not start again on every platform, because the app ends its process when asked to exit, before Tauri would start the new one. The new instance now starts once the old one has given up its single-instance claim (checked on Linux: before, the app was gone; now it comes back with the setting applied).
- Sharpening Radius below 1 and Masking work as in Camera Raw on low-contrast photos (recipe `sharpenVersion` 4, new edits and never-edited RAWs; from Radius 1 up without Masking it renders exactly as before). Below Radius 1 the Threshold now judges the finer kernel's detail as if it were Radius 1's, so low-contrast texture is no longer shut out: at Radius 0.5 and Amount 40 the fine-detail gain against Camera Raw went from 15 % low (median of 8 public sample RAWs; Canon R5 −42 %, R6 −57 %) to −3…+3 % on every file, also on the 4 files held out from tuning (R6 −57 % → 0 %), with halos still at or below Camera Raw's and flat-area noise below it on 7 of 8 files (Nikon Z6III 1.07 against 1.06); the medium band is now 7–15 % stronger than Camera Raw's at Radius 0.5–0.75 (median). Masking reads a wider neighbourhood that tells texture from noise: at Masking 50 it keeps 82–94 % of the texture sharpening on contrasty photos (before 77–88 %, Camera Raw 84–92 %) with flat-area noise sharpened less than Camera Raw does, and on the low-contrast R5 frame it keeps 7 % instead of removing all sharpening (Camera Raw 21–23 %). On Radius and Masking settings within the slider range, the settings outside ±10 % of Camera Raw's fine detail fell from 26 to 10 of 48. Masking costs about 1.2–1.7 ms more per 2304×1728 preview frame on an M4 Pro (+16–23 %; Detail 100 with Masking 80: 9.6–10.5 ms); other settings cost the same within measurement noise. Saved edits render exactly as before.
- Photos can be moved to a folder on another drive (Move to Folder, paste after cut), with their XMP, editor sidecars and virtual copies. The Library asks first, naming the drive, how much is copied and how much is free there. Each file is copied next to its new place, synced, read back from the drive and compared byte for byte with the original, then given its name without ever replacing a file; only then do the originals go to the Trash of their drive. An original that changed meanwhile, or whose drive has no Trash, stays where it is and the Library says so. A progress notice shows how far the copy is, with Stop. A crash at any point loses nothing: before the copies are all in place the move is rolled back at the next start; after, it is finished and the originals not yet in the Trash are kept. Undo copies the photos back the same way (edits made since included) and Redo moves them again. Refused for now: network drives, and exFAT/FAT drives on macOS (they cannot rename without risking replacing a file; on Linux they work) (ADR 0016).
- Luminance curve: the tone curve has a Luminance channel next to RGB, R, G and B (recipe `curves.luminance`, for the photo and in layers). It changes the brightness of each colour by the colour's luminance and keeps its saturation and hue, so an S-curve adds contrast without the extra saturation and hue shifts an RGB curve brings (Capture One renders, S-curve on saturated colours: its RGB curve raises saturation ×1.14–1.24 and turns hues by 2.4–2.7°, its Luma curve keeps saturation within ×0.95–1.01 and hue within 1°; Lightroom has no such curve). It acts after the RGB and channel curves; lifting its black end lifts black to grey. Capture One styles bring their Luma curve into it instead of onto the RGB curve; the import report says that Capture One curves come over approximately (it applies them to γ 1.8 values: a few levels off its renders). The RGB curve's tab is now labelled RGB. Recipes without the channel render byte-identically.
- Point curves act in the wide working space, as Camera Raw's do (curve `version` 3 inside the recipe's `curves`, written by Lightroom imports and by edits of neutral curves): the RGB and the red, green and blue curves are applied to the colour in ProPhoto primaries with the sRGB tone curve instead of to display values clipped to sRGB. Measured on two photos rendered by Camera Raw, that space predicts its curves pixel by pixel within 0.06 levels for a red curve (display values: 9.6 levels; a red curve 128 → 160 turns grey 128 into R 186 / G 119, which display values cannot give) and within 0.66 instead of 1.46 levels for the RGB curve on saturated colours, so Lightroom's `ToneCurvePV2012Red/Green/Blue` import 1:1. Colours beyond sRGB now go through the curves themselves instead of being carried past them. Curves saved before keep their look, also when a point of them is moved; neutral curves render as before (exports byte-identical). No measurable GPU cost (6 MP display frame 6.7 against 7.0 ms on an M4 Pro).
- Tone targets (the targeted tone and region curve) work with layer curves, LUTs, clipping warnings, soft proofing, colour-range and layer solo views, and with curves that fall or turn back: the level under the pointer is now rendered for the photo's curves (the photo without them and without what follows them, 4–10 ms per press) instead of traced back from the displayed colour, where these were declined.
- Exporting photos with several ticked recipes is faster: the recipes are queued together and each photo is loaded, lens-corrected and uploaded to the GPU once for all of them, then each recipe renders, sizes, sharpens, watermarks and encodes its own file (before, every recipe decoded and developed the photo again). Up to four photos are worked on at once (before, at most one per 4 GB of free memory), and no further photo starts while less than 2 GB of memory is free. 20 Nikon Z RAWs with a web JPEG and a 16-bit Adobe RGB print TIFF recipe (38 files) take 23.6–24.9 s instead of 31.3–34.9 s on an M5 Pro (two A/B/B/A rounds); every file is byte-identical to before, and a single photo exports in the same time (1.63–1.67 s). Lightroom Classic and Capture One take 8–9 s for this set; the GPU render, once per recipe, is now the slowest stage (about 17 s of the 24).
- Point curves with more than 16 points render whole (curve `version` 2 inside the recipe's `curves`, written by new edits, Lightroom imports and every curve edit, so it travels with the curves through copy, paste, presets and sync): curves imported from Lightroom with many points were cut after their 16th point (every tone above it held at that point's level, up to 34 levels off on a 20-point curve); the GPU now reads such curves from a table that holds the exact curve every quarter input level (on 20-point test curves within 0.01–0.11 of an output level, always under a quarter level; a jump within one input level is spread over a quarter input level). Saved edits render exactly as before, including those with more than 16-point curves (measured on an M4 Pro against the previous main: 208 of 208 full-resolution 16- and 8-bit exports of 13 public RAWs with 8 saved recipes byte-identical, among them a 20-point curve without the key; GPU preview equivalence tests pass); re-importing the Lightroom settings or touching the curve renders them whole. A 20-point curve exports in the time of a 16-point one (24 MP, 16-bit: 2.69 s against 2.83 s, median of 4).
- Lightroom tone curves import as Camera Raw draws them: Camera Raw interpolates `ToneCurvePV2012` points with a natural cubic spline on display values (measured 2026-10-21 on two photos), which overshoots between steep points where Rawtonic's curve stays flat; imports add points (on whole levels) on Lightroom's spline until Rawtonic's curve is within a quarter level of it, except next to steep transitions (a rise of many levels within a few input levels), which whole-level points cannot follow closer (2–12 levels off there in the tests; the import logs it) (an overshooting curve: 128 → 135 at input 96, Camera Raw 135). The import no longer lifts shadow points of the curve on its own.
- Targeted adjustment for the region curve: drag up or down on the photo to move the region (Shadows, Darks, Lights or Highlights) of the tone under the pointer, as Lightroom's parametric curve does; one drag is one undo step. The tone curve graph now draws the curve the renderer applies instead of straight lines between the points.
- Sharpening follows the photo's brightness, as Camera Raw's does (recipe `sharpenVersion` 3, new edits, reset photos and never-edited RAWs): it fades out towards the shadows and is up to twice as strong in the highlights, at every Amount including the default 40. Before, it sharpened all tones alike, so shadow noise was sharpened up to five times as strongly as in Camera Raw and bright detail in dark areas got dark rims. On 8 public sample RAWs measured against Camera Raw at Amount 40–150 and Detail 0–100, the settings whose halos exceeded Camera Raw's fell from 24 of 112 to 1 (at Amount up to 100, the slider's range: 9 of 64 to 1, at most 0.3 Oklab L ×100 above), and the sharpening of dark flat areas came down to Camera Raw's level (Canon R6 at Amount 80 / Detail 100: noise ×6.0 → ×2.6, Camera Raw ×3.8). On 6 further high-ISO RAWs held out from fitting, every halo is at or below Camera Raw's (before up to +2.8), and flat-area noise at Amount 80 / Detail 100 is at or below Camera Raw's on all 14 files; it is higher than before in bright flat areas of some noisy photos (ISO 12800 A7C without noise reduction ×1.6), where Camera Raw sharpens more still, and with the default noise reduction it changes by ×0.75–1.04 at the default sharpening. Texture at the default sharpening stays at Camera Raw's or above (×1.20 of it, before ×1.21); at Amount 120–150 the strongest fine detail Rawtonic reaches is lower on some photos. Same GPU cost (preview frame and full-resolution export within ±1.5 % on an M4 Pro). Saved edits render exactly as before (136 of 136 full-resolution exports byte-identical).
- Automatic correction of axial chromatic aberration for new RAW edits (recipe `fringeVersion` 4, new RAW edits and never-edited RAWs; noisy photos included): purple and blue fringes on dark branches and letters against a bright sky, and green or yellow rims on the dark side of bright edges, up to about 8 pixels wide, now take the colour of the edge's two sides mixed at the pixel's own brightness, as a blur the same in every colour would give. Real colour is kept where a channel lies beyond both sides (a coloured stroke along an edge), where the brightness rises in two steps between the sides (a band of another colour, a light leaf before the sky), and where the colour differs in a hue other than the purple-to-blue and yellow-to-green families by more than about twice the photo's noise (an orange glow round a lamp). On 41 public sample RAWs the share of bright-edge pixels with unexplained colour fell or stayed equal on every file (Canon EOS M50 II 2.10 → 0.80 %, Panasonic S1R II 5.39 → 0.48 %, Fujifilm X-H1 1.86 → 1.01 %, Nikon Z6 II 1.31 → 0.45 %), and on 21 further held-out RAWs it fell on 14 (Sony A9 2.59 → 1.01 %), stayed equal on 4 and rose on 3 (Panasonic S5 0.11 → 0.12 %, Sony A7C 3.59 → 3.61 %, and a Nikon D7200 frame with only 270 bright-edge pixels, 21 → 30 of them); purple fringe left in hand-checked fringe regions fell on all 10 (X-H1 1.57 → 0.77, S1R II 1.46 → 0.49, Panasonic G7 0.60 → 0.14), now at or under the better of Capture One and Camera Raw at their defaults on 7 of 10 (before 4). Legitimate purple and green detail at edges kept 98–100 % of its colour except where its edges were fringed (a test chart's blue patch edges 85–91 %, a crane's purple-fringed red lattice 87 %). The pass runs once per photo geometry; on an M4 Pro it adds about 0.07 s (24 MP, few edges) to 0.15–0.3 s (24–80 MP, many edges) to a whole export (5–18 % on the photos measured), about 40 % less than when it was first built, with identical results. Saved edits render exactly as before.
- The edge-fringe pass of saved edits (`fringeVersion` 1 and 2) is about a third faster with exactly the same pixels (version 2 on a 24 MP photo 0.07 s instead of 0.10 s on an M4 Pro; version 1 runs the same code).
- Colour noise reduction leaves fewer colour specks along edges (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 9, new edits and never-edited RAWs): each pixel's filtered colour is now kept within the range of its neighbourhood's filtered colours plus about the noise, so a pixel whose noisy colour the filter kept next to a brightness or colour edge (a dark pixel by a bright border, the rim of a colour edge) no longer stands out as a coloured speck; a coloured point far beyond its surroundings (a small light, real fine detail) keeps its colour. On 55 high-ISO RAWs, colour specks fell from 10.3 to 7.5 per million pixels at Color 25, from 5.8 to 4.2 at Color 50 (Camera Raw 4.7, Capture One's default 5.4) and from 5.3 to 4.1 at Color 100 (Camera Raw 4.6); colour edges stayed within 0.08 % of version 8 in every ISO band, the error against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) and the colour texture kept rose or fell in no cell by more than 0.5 %, and fine colour detail fell by at most 0.6 % (ISO 1600–2500 and 6400). Still above both editors at Color 25 (5.4) and Capture One at Color 100 (1.4); wrong-side pixels along colour edges and 2-pixel colour blocks remain. Saved edits render exactly as before; at Color 0 version 9 renders as version 8.
- Cleaner edges between two saturated colours (recipe `fringeVersion` 3, new RAW edits and never-edited RAWs): where the demosaic leaves red and blue a pixel off green, the edge between a yellow object and grey got a tinted line and the edge between cyan and magenta chart patches a light seam neither colour has. The edge-fringe pass now also moves such pixels onto the line between the two colours a blur would follow (in linear light), only in small windows that hold a strong edge and two colours, and only for colours a mix of the two could make, so a third colour (a thin stripe, a small patch on the edge, a pink flower on a green leaf) keeps its colour. On 34 low-noise public sample RAWs, Bayer and X-Trans, the share of bright-edge pixels with unexplained colour fell on every file (Nikon D100 1.97 → 0.54 %, Olympus E-400 1.74 → 0.50 %, Panasonic TZ202 0.36 → 0.09 %, Fujifilm X-H1 4.72 → 2.07 %, X-T5 2.46 → 0.94 %, Sony ZV-1 4.75 → 1.80 %); colour lost on clearly coloured pixels stayed under 0.2 % beyond 0.04 Oklab chroma, legitimate purple and green detail at edges kept 98–100 % of its colour. Noisy photos (about ISO 3200 and up) render as before (the stage found colour lines in the noise there). The pass runs once per photo geometry, not per slider change; on an M4 Pro it takes about 0.24 s for a 24–36 MP photo (whole exports 3–9 % longer than with the previous version). Saved edits render exactly as before.
- Colour noise reduction removes more lone coloured specks (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 8, new edits and never-edited RAWs): whether such a pixel is a coloured point that stands out in brightness is now judged after the luminance noise reduction, so a pixel that is darker or brighter than its neighbours only by noise loses its stray colour, while a point the luminance noise reduction keeps (a small light, real fine detail) keeps it. On 55 high-ISO RAWs, isolated colour specks fell from 14.3 to 10.3 per million pixels at Color 25 and from 8.0 to 5.8 at Color 50 (5.6 → 5.3 at 100; Capture One's default 5.4, Camera Raw at Color 50 4.7), with more specks on at most 1 of the 55 files; colour edges stayed within 0.04 % and colour detail within 0.3 % of version 7 in every ISO band, and the error against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) rose in no cell by more than 0.5 % (largest +0.007 %). Saved edits render exactly as before; at Color 0 or Luminance 0 version 8 renders as version 7.
- Three render fixes for new edits (recipe `renderVersion` 2; saved edits render as before): Clarity, Dehaze and Glow no longer leave faint seams every 2048 pixels on photos with a short side above 3456 pixels (their large blur stopped short of its radius at render-tile edges: up to 1,341 of 65,535 levels on a striped 5400-pixel test frame, now none); lens corrections and straightening keep colours beyond sRGB instead of clipping their negative channels (only where the photo has such colours); and with the Classic and AgX looks, switching Color Grading or Calibration off now turns them off.
- Lens profiles from the lens database are looked up at the photo's f-number and scaled to its body's crop factor, and keep their whole lateral chromatic aberration correction. Canon and Fujifilm RAWs were looked up at the APEX aperture value (f/8 read as f/6, so the wrong vignetting row), CR2, CR3, RAF, ORF and RW2 files were taken for full frame (their EXIF had the focal length copied into the 35 mm equivalent), and the profiles' radius-dependent CA terms were dropped. On 62 public sample RAWs, 31 newly looked-up profiles change: on APS-C and Micro Four Thirds bodies the corner vignetting correction had been evaluated far outside the lens's measured radius (up to a brightness of 0, now −0.3 to −1.6 EV), and the distortion correction was scaled for the wrong frame (E-M1X with 12–40 mm at 12 mm: −23.9 % instead of +5.1 % radial scale at 60 % of the half-diagonal); 4,026 channel profiles had CA terms worth a median 0.5 px (up to 13 px) in a 24 MP corner. The Lens tool shows the same profile a save looks up. Saved edits keep the lens parameters they were saved with; the 35 mm equivalent in exports is the camera's own (or absent) for newly read EXIF.

- Fewer isolated dark and bright single-pixel specks in noisy photos (recipe `lumaNoiseVersion` 2, new edits): the luminance noise reduction keeps a pixel within the range of its eight neighbours plus half its noise, and the sharpening no longer lifts such noise-like extremes; a point that stands far out of the noise (a light, a star) is left alone. On 55 high-ISO RAWs (paired, same settings otherwise), isolated specks fell from 20.3 to 16.4 per million pixels at Color 25 (11.4 → 10.6 at 50, 12.5 → 8.2 at 100) and isolated lightness outliers by 28 %; colour edges are unchanged, and against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) the error is lower at 0.7–2 px (−0.8 %) and in flat areas (−3.4 %). The cost: about 1 % of the finest lightness texture (texture kept 0.7–2 px −1.3 %, 2–4 px error +0.5 %). Saved edits render exactly as before, and so do photos whose measured noise is at most 0.002 (most base-ISO photos; two ISO 50/100 test frames measured just above it and changed by at most 60/65535, below one 8-bit step); a preview frame takes about 0.2 ms more on the GPU.

## 0.1.0-alpha.3 — 2026-10-20

The third alpha: about a hundred changes since alpha.2. Tethered shooting arrives (experimental), local adjustments get finer masks and a faster brush, AI Denoise gets a new model, and colour noise reduction, fringe and chromatic aberration removal, highlight recovery, Auto white balance and sharpening were reworked and measured against the leading RAW editors.

### Highlights

- **Tethered shooting (experimental).** Connect a camera on USB, set its exposure, fire it from the app (button or ⌘K / Ctrl+K), focus, and see its live view; every shot lands in the session, verified and named by your template, with your next-capture adjustments, rating and keywords. A camera fault cannot crash the app (the camera library runs in its own helper process), and nothing on a memory card is deleted. Tested on Sony a7 III and a7 IV and Fujifilm X-H2S and GFX 50R on macOS. Other Sony and Fujifilm bodies may work but are untested; **Canon cameras are not offered yet**; Fujifilm live view does not work yet; on Linux the tether tool is included but has not been tried with a real camera.
- **Local adjustments.** 16-bit layer masks (smooth gradients without banding steps), a **Refine edge** slider for hair, fur and foliage, a better Auto Mask for brush strokes, and **Solo** (H or Option/Alt-click an eye) to see one layer or mask alone. Brush strokes follow the pointer more closely and stay quick on layers with many strokes; photos with 20–32 layers stay fluid while dragging sliders, and exports with many layers need about half the memory.
- **AI Denoise** uses a new, faster model for new edits (downloaded once, on first use; photos denoised before keep their model): about 10 s instead of 27–31 s for a 24 MP photo on our test Mac, and its amount now has the effect of the same number in Camera Raw's Denoise (50 = its default).
- **Auto straighten**: one click (crop tool *Auto*, Shift+R or the photo's context menu) levels the horizon or the verticals and crops so no empty corners show. It gives no answer rather than a wrong one when a photo has no clear lines.
- **Auto white balance** rebuilt: neutral in daylight and cool light, while sunsets, golden hour and lamplight keep their mood.
- **Cleaner colour**: colour noise reduction keeps colour edges and fine colour texture and removes lone coloured specks; Defringe removes only colour the edge does not explain; Remove chromatic aberration is on by default for RAWs whose camera or lens data has no correction of its own, and Panasonic cameras' own correction data are used.
- **Tones**: recovered highlights without pink, magenta or green casts; Shadows and Highlights act more like the leading editors; sharpening keeps halos in check at high amounts.
- **Library**: redo for undone renames, moves and trash (⇧⌘Z / Ctrl+Y), emptying the session Trash, a hot folder that resumes after a restart and can watch subfolders, naming tokens from each photo's EXIF, and XMP sidecars from the card come along at import.
- **Preset import** from Lightroom and Capture One lists what it could not translate, and translates Capture One's Color Editor into Rawtonic's colour editor.
- Updated Lensfun lens database (2026-10-08).
- **Linux**: smoother slider drags on NVIDIA under Wayland, and the first photo after an update or driver change shows in about 3 s instead of 12 s. The AppImage carries update information: **AppImageUpdate** or **Gear Lever** can update it when you ask them to (Rawtonic itself never checks for updates).
- Multi view's *Edit all selected* updates every photo live again; many smaller fixes (an export the graphics card fails (e.g. out of memory) is reported as failed instead of being written as a black file, exported files are readable by other users, virtual copies export with their own edits, edits are saved before files are moved, and more).

### Changes to existing edits

New behaviour is tied to recipe version keys, so photos edited with alpha.2 render as before, with these exceptions:

- **Black last row and column fixed** (no version key, on purpose: the black edge was never intended). Lens vignetting without distortion correction, a chromatic aberration correction that found nothing, or rejected guide lines left the photo's last pixel row and column black, in exports too. They now keep their pixels; pixels near the right and bottom edge can differ very slightly where local adjustments read that line.
- **Highlight recovery**: photos with clipped highlights change in those areas (no pink, magenta or green casts); photos without clipped highlights are unchanged.
- **Text watermarks** may differ by at most 2 of 255 levels in a few pixels (new font reader).

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **AI Denoise** is about 1.5× slower than Camera Raw's Denoise (about 10 s vs 6.5 s for 24 MP on the same Mac) and leaves a higher lightness error at the same noise level (2.21 vs 1.69); on Linux it takes about 35 s per 24 MP photo on the GPU.
- **Tethering** on the GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 2.4 s after the shot, in Capture One 2.1 s (on the a7 III, a7 IV and X-H2S Rawtonic takes 1.1–1.5 s, Capture One 1.2–1.6 s). Tethering as a whole is experimental.
- **Auto straighten**: on 51 test photos its median error is lower than Lightroom's Auto (0.06° vs 0.18°), but its 90th percentile is slightly behind (0.46° vs 0.42°). Straightening several selected photos at once is not available yet.
- **Layers**: at most 32 visible layers take effect; further visible layers are ignored.
- **Colour noise reduction** still leaves more lone colour specks than the leading editors at the default amount, and fine colour detail at ISO 3200 and above is below the best of them.
- **Chromatic aberration and fringes** remain above the best reference on a few cameras (e.g. Lumix GX850, TZ202, Canon EOS M50 II).
- **Sharpening**: low-contrast detail at high Amount/Detail settings is below the leading editors.
- Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don't open yet; imports from Lightroom and Capture One don't carry every adjustment over.
- **Linux**: tested on an NVIDIA graphics card under KDE Wayland and with exports on Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora; other graphics cards and desktops may show problems. The very first Develop frame after an install or driver update takes about 3 s on NVIDIA.

### Details

- Fix: an export or thumbnail the graphics card failed to render — for example when it ran out of memory because another program was using it — was written as an all-black file and reported as done. Such a render now fails, and the export queue lists the photograph with the reason. Renders without a GPU error are unchanged.
- Fix: a geometry warp that moves no pixel (lens vignetting without a lens profile's distortion, Remove chromatic aberration that found nothing, rejected guide lines) left the last row and column of the photo black, in exports too; they now keep their pixels. This changes such exports, saved edits included, without a recipe version key (user decision 2026-10-19: the black edge was never intended): also default exports of never-edited RAWs where automatic chromatic aberration removal finds nothing to correct, and the pixels near the right and bottom edge differ slightly where local filters read the former black line. The default exports of the three benchmark RAWs (Canon CR3, Nikon NEF, Fujifilm RAF) are byte-identical.
- **Tethered shooting.** The Tether tool in the session bar connects a camera on USB, reads and sets its exposure settings (ISO, shutter, aperture, exposure compensation, white balance, drive, focus and metering modes, image quality, where photos are saved; settings fixed on the body are shown as such), fires it (button, ⌘K/Ctrl+K, also with the tool closed), focuses and shows its live view. Every photo — fired here or on the camera — is copied into the session's Capture folder with the filename template and verified, gets the next-capture adjustments, rating and keywords in the same transaction as the hot folder's, is selected in the Library and opened when Develop is showing, and the captures form a tether import batch (Previous Import; database schema 9). A camera unplugged and plugged in again is connected again by itself. The camera library (libgphoto2) runs in a separate helper process, so a camera or USB fault cannot crash the app; on macOS it talks to cameras through the system's ImageCaptureCore service. Downloads are written crash-safe, and an interrupted one is kept aside, never added. Nothing on a memory card is deleted; on cameras that keep every tethered photo in a transfer queue and stop shooting when it is full (Fujifilm in USB tether mode), a photo is freed there once it is verified in the library — the tool can turn that off. Tested on Sony a7 III and a7 IV and Fujifilm X-H2S and GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 1.1–1.5 s after the shot (Capture One 1.2–1.6 s), 2.4 s on the GFX 50R (Capture One 2.1 s); 100 of 100 (Sony) and 50 of 50 (Fujifilm) shots in a row arrived. Fujifilm live view is not working yet.
- Colour noise reduction removes more lone coloured specks (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 7, new edits and never-edited RAWs): a pixel whose colour stands far out from all its neighbours while its brightness matches theirs within the noise takes their median colour; a coloured point that is brighter or darker than its surroundings (a small light, real fine detail) keeps its colour. On 55 high-ISO RAWs, isolated colour specks fell from 26.8 to 20.3 per million pixels at Color 25 (13.7 → 11.4 at 50, unchanged at 100; ISO 1600–2500 at Color 25 15.8 → 8.3), colour edges stayed within 0.2 % of version 6 in every ISO band, and the error against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) was lower in 2 cells and higher in none. Color 0 and saved edits render exactly as before.
- Brush strokes follow the pointer more closely, also on layers that already hold many strokes: a stroke being drawn sends the recipe once and then only its new points, and the preview no longer copies or re-reads every stroke of a painted layer on each pointer move. With 120 strokes on a layer above twenty others, pointer to rendered frame (median) 34 → 13 ms, and the ~95 ms pause after each stroke (the history list relabelled every step) is gone.
