# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Preferences opens above the welcome screen, with a visible welcome entry; Windows snap-layout controls stay inactive behind modal dialogs.

- Shortcut hints preserve file and tool names containing Mac key symbols; Windows/Linux reference hints say Enter. Linux uses neutral file-manager wording, and thumbnail path aliases are cached per library list.

- Windows and Linux show platform shortcut labels, scale the top bar with the interface, keep photo dimensions beside shortened filenames, and enforce a usable minimum window size. Plain Ctrl+C in Develop copies the remembered settings choice; Ctrl+Shift+C opens the picker. Catalog scans leave exports in Output out of the photo count, and thumbnail events update tiles listed through another Windows path spelling.

- **Windows: the AI tools can use the graphics card.** ONNX Runtime's GPU plugin needs Microsoft's DirectX Shader Compiler on Direct3D 12, which Windows does not include, so the AI tools always ran on the processor. The installers and the portable zip now bring it (`dxcompiler.dll`, `dxil.dll`, Microsoft's release 1.8.2502) beside ONNX Runtime; if it is missing, the AI tools still run on the processor and the log says why.
- **AI runs on the graphics card only where that is faster.** The one-time check of each AI model on the graphics card now also times it against the processor and keeps the faster one (on an integrated Radeon 680M, AI Subject took 14–19 s on the graphics card against 8.5 s on the processor). An AI Denoise tile that hangs on the graphics card no longer stops AI Denoise: after a time limit it goes on on the processor.
- **The first AI click no longer waits for that check.** The app used to time a model on the graphics card against the processor before its first use, so the first AI Subject click on a Radeon 680M took 110 s, 49 s of them the measurement. A model not yet checked now runs on the processor at once, and the check runs later in the background while nothing else happens: it stops the moment you edit, use an AI tool or export, never runs during an export, and is dropped when you quit. If the graphics card turns out clearly faster (at most 85 % of the processor's time, and a first run after starting the app at most 20 % slower), the model moves there; until then and everywhere else, the processor is used.

- **Dehaze adapts to the photo, as Lightroom's does** (new edits): lowering Dehaze lifted every photo towards white, so a sunset or a pastel wall under a blue sky turned milky where Camera Raw lays a grey veil below white and even darkens the brightest areas; raising Dehaze darkened bright skies of photos without near-white areas more than Camera Raw. Dehaze now estimates the photo's airlight once from the whole decoded image (the brightest areas of its dark channel, the classic haze estimate), the same for the preview, every export, crop and virtual copy, and lowering veils towards that level while raising fades its darkening relative to it. On 64 photos compared with Camera Raw at the same softening (the 48 of the slider atlas and 16 fresh ones), the largest median difference in any 10-L* tone band went from 2.6 to 1.1 L* at −50 and from 3.3 to 1.4 L* at −100; on the 16 fresh photos from 4.4 to 0.9 and from 6.4 to 2.2 L*. The rule was fitted on all 64 photos; tested on held-out halves it stays within the atlas's amber bound at −50 in 92 % of 200 random splits (version 2: 47 %). Raised Dehaze moved closer to both references (+100: 2.2 to 1.5 L*; fresh photos +50: 4.7 to 3.5) but stays further from them on the fresh photos than on the atlas, and on the atlas's 48 photos +50 moved from 0.9 to 1.2 L*. Photos edited before keep their look (recipe `dehazeVersion`).

- Add Folder names the owning library and explains how to work on refused photos, without claiming they are already in the current catalog.

- **Windows: the AI tools no longer crash the app on PCs with an old Visual C++ runtime.** Choosing Brush or another AI masking tool could close Rawtonic within seconds (access violation in `MSVCP140.dll`) where Windows only had an older Microsoft C++ runtime: ONNX Runtime needs a current one. The installers and the portable zip now bring the current runtime beside ONNX Runtime, without administrator rights or a separate install, and if an older one is still in use the AI tools say the AI runtime is unavailable instead of crashing.

- Copy Settings in the Library uses the active photo after another photo is deselected.
- **Windows: paths read as you know them, and long or busy files are handled.** Photo, folder and volume paths are no longer shown or stored as `\\?\C:\…` (a drive showed as `\\?\C:\`, and an import destination could become invalid with *The filename, directory name, or volume label syntax is incorrect*). Moves and renames deeper than 260 characters work; an XMP sidecar named `.XMP` keeps its spelling when Rawtonic updates it; a sidecar a virus scanner or the search indexer is reading is written once it lets go (or the write fails saying the file is in use, the old one untouched), and a read-only sidecar is refused instead of failing after the work; Import from Catalog no longer leaves its snapshot in the catalog's `.cache`. Your photo files were never affected.
- **Windows: picks and rejects are there again after a restart.** The library names its photos `\\?\C:\…` on Windows, the folder shown after a restart lists them as `C:\…`: the Pick and Reject counters read 0 and the flags were not shown, although they were saved. Flags are now found under either form, and a rating or label changed in such a view shows at once. Star ratings, edits and your photo files were never affected.
- **A long graphics compile is shown instead of a panel that looks dead.** On some graphics drivers (a Radeon 680M under Windows: 65–90 s) a new kind of edit, a newly opened photo or an edited photo's thumbnail waits for a shader compile. Every compile that takes longer than a moment now shows *Preparing graphics for this computer* in the activity centre and over the photo until it ends, and its start is written to the log; a thumbnail that cannot be made says why in the log.

- **A corrected capture time now travels in the XMP sidecar.** *Shift Capture Time* writes the corrected time as `exif:DateTimeOriginal` and `photoshop:DateCreated` (with fraction of a second and UTC offset when known; no offset is invented) into the photo's XMP sidecar, so Lightroom, Capture One and other libraries see it, as with Lightroom's Edit Capture Time; *Back to the time in the file* and Undo remove exactly the dates Rawtonic wrote. The other way round, a sidecar whose capture time differs from the file's — for example after Lightroom's Edit Capture Time — becomes the photo's capture-time correction when it is imported or read with *Use XMP*; equal times change nothing. Sidecars of photos without a correction are written byte for byte as before, and your photo files are never changed.
- **Faster lossless JPEG XL exports.** A synthetic 36 MP RGB image encoded in about 2.3 seconds instead of 143 seconds on the test laptop, with identical decoded pixels and a 72% larger file. Memory use remains above the export budget; lossy JPEG XL is unchanged.

- **Lens profiles correct as the Lensfun database measured them** (new lens choices and Auto lens correction): Rawtonic now applies a Lensfun lens profile the way Lensfun itself defines it. Before, the distortion was refitted and rescaled, the colour-fringe correction lost part of its shape, and vignetting took the nearest measured aperture and focus distance instead of blending between measurements. Against Lensfun's own correction on 18 lenses (13 used while building it, 5 checked afterwards), at settings both on and between the measured ones, the corrected geometry now agrees within 0.002 pixel at 24 MP (was up to 15 px with each program's own framing), colour fringes within 0.002 px (was 0.16 px) and the corner brightening within 0.15 % (was up to 49 % between measured apertures and distances). The corrected photo is framed by the largest crop it fills completely, checked at every edge pixel: no empty corners or edges (the old framing could leave a thin black sliver at the top of some wide-angle zooms), and up to 2.6 % more of the photo than a frame kept at Lensfun's scale. The vignetting correction keeps its measured strength (80 % of the profile). Saved edits keep their lens parameters and export exactly as before; the camera's own lens data is unchanged.

- **Updated lens corrections** from Lensfun, including Nikkor Z 24-70mm f/2.8 S II, GF 63mm, Tamron 70-300mm, OM System EXIF names and the corrected Nikkor AF 80-400mm profile. Already resolved lens corrections and saved diffraction settings keep their previous results.

- **Restoring a compared snapshot clears its Compare selection.** Before/After returns to the unedited photograph; a different snapshot selected as the reference stays selected.

- **Windows: the window controls sit in the toolbar.** Minimize, maximize/restore and close are now at the right end of the top bar (as on Linux) instead of in a separate "Rawtonic" strip above it, which freed a row; the empty bar drags the window and a double-click maximizes or restores it. The controls' labels are translated. Hovering over the maximize button opens Windows 11's snap layouts, as in other Windows apps. The window now always opens fully on screen: on a small or highly scaled display (a 1080p laptop at 125 %) it opened taller than the screen with the top bar out of reach; a window too large for the screen now shrinks to it, one placed on a monitor that is gone or partly off screen comes back, and it opens maximized when even its minimum size does not fit.

- **Blacks −100 darkens the shadows as far as Capture One's −100** (new edits, RAW files): the second half of the Blacks slider did little — Blacks −100 left the darkest tones grey where Capture One's Black −100 and Lightroom's Blacks −100 clearly deepen them (L* 10–30 darkened 9 / 7 L* against Capture One's 14 / 15). Lowered Blacks now moves at Capture One's pace: −50 is unchanged, the old −100 sits at about −72, and −100 deepens the black point twice as far (on the 48 photos of the slider atlas L* 10–30 within about 1 L* of Capture One's −100, the darkest tenth within 0.1; on the 24 not used for the fit within 1.2 / 0.3). Lightroom's −100, which darkens up to the midtones and crushes a quarter of the photo to black, stays deeper. Shadow texture and the share of pure black match Capture One's at the same darkening as before. Earlier edits keep their look; Blacks copied between older and newer edits (tool copy, presets, Edit all selected) keep the darkening they render. Raised Blacks, JPEG/TIFF photos and the inherited looks are unchanged.
- **Windows: the installers finish in seconds, and the first start shows its graphics preparation.** The NSIS installer and the MSI no longer compile the GPU shaders after copying the files (on a Radeon 680M that ran over two minutes behind a busy cursor, then ended without a word). The first start compiles them instead and shows *Preparing graphics for this computer* in the activity centre while it takes longer than a moment, then *Graphics prepared … in N s* under Done.
- **Windows: no more "Could not lock adapter context" at start.** OpenGL is no longer used on Windows: wgpu's OpenGL backend there stops the app when a shader compile holds the graphics context for more than a second, which our processing shader does. A start that used to fall back to OpenGL now uses DirectX 12 or Vulkan; an OpenGL choice in Preferences becomes Auto, and the window says so once. The start guard never leaves out every graphics backend after crashes (only the newest crash's), and when no backend starts the window offers *Reset graphics settings and retry*. Headless runs (`rawtonic export`, `--warm-gpu-cache`) follow the guard but are never counted as a start crash when they are ended.
- **Windows with an AMD graphics processor: the first start no longer fills the memory.** On a Radeon 680M (Vulkan) the app's memory grew to 25–30 GB right after the first shader compile, as AMD's driver compiled several pipelines at once, and the whole PC slowed down. On AMD's Windows Vulkan driver Rawtonic now compiles one pipeline at a time: about 8 GB during the first compile and 2 GB after, with the first preview as fast as before. Closing the window during *Preparing graphics* is no longer taken for a graphics crash at the next start, *Graphics prepared … in N s* is shown when it ends, and when no graphics backend starts the activity centre opens with *Reset graphics settings and retry* in view.
- **Windows with an AMD graphics processor: the first start and the first photo are ready in seconds.** On a Radeon 680M (Vulkan) preparing the graphics took 70–97 s at up to 7–8 GB of memory, and opening the first photo another 64–71 s at up to 8.8 GB. The preview now compiles only the code a photo uses (no glow, halation or grain code for a photo without them, none of the switched-off tools): the start's graphics are prepared in about 7 s at under 1 GB, the first photo shows its preview after about 1.5 s and settles after about 11 s at 1.6 GB; on a warm start the graphics are ready in a fraction of a second. Exports are unchanged, byte for byte. Not yet: on that driver, the first Contrast, Shadows, Whites or Blacks edit of a session still waits about 90 s for its preview.
- **Crash reports from Windows name each frame by module and offset** (`rawtonic-app.exe+0x…`) instead of the misleading nearest export names (`onig_*`); each release's symbols are kept privately to resolve them.

- **Super Resolution is faster on the CPU fallback.** On the laptop, inference for the same 24 MP tile set took 461 → 146 seconds (ONNX Runtime CPU, 117 tiles); complete export takes longer. CPU-only preparation downloads an additional 2.4 MB model conversion. GPU and Core ML keep their existing models; older installed packs remain usable.

- **Each arrow-key press and each slider drag is its own Undo step.** Two quick taps of an arrow key on a slider or value field, or two short drags, used to merge into one step when they came less than half a second apart; now each is undone on its own, while a held key (auto-repeat) stays one step, as a drag does.

## 0.1.0-alpha.13 — 2026-11-11

The thirteenth alpha. Exports can be enlarged 2× by an AI model (Super Resolution), a second window shows the photo live on a second display, and the import dialog shows real pixels at 100 % for a focus check, with ratings, before anything is copied; inserting a memory card opens it. For new edits, Whites, Shadows, Highlights, Blacks, Contrast, Structure, Clarity, Vibrance, Temperature and Tint, the colour mixer and lowered Dehaze were measured against Camera Raw and Capture One on a 48-photo slider atlas and refitted, AI Denoise keeps the shadows' level and colour, and Sharpening of RAW photos leaves the noise of even areas alone. Sliders step with the keys as in Lightroom, and Alt/Option-click on a group's name resets the group. Saved edits export as in alpha.12 (see *Changes to existing edits*).

### Highlights

- **Super Resolution: export photos enlarged 2× by an AI model.** Tick *Super Resolution (2×, AI)* under Photo sizing in the Export dialog (also `rawtonic export --super-resolution`): each photo is enlarged to twice its width and height before the resize, border and output sharpening, so a 24 MP photo exports at 96 MP. The model enlarges faithfully and invents no texture: on 34 crops of 17 RAWs, halved and enlarged again, it matched the originals as closely as Lightroom's Super Resolution (PSNR 32.6 dB both, differences within measurement uncertainty), at about 6 s for 24 MP on an M5 Pro's GPU (Lightroom 7 s). Originals and edits are never changed; the option lives in the export recipe, and the exported file names the step in its software field (`Rawtonic … (Super Resolution 2x)`). The model (Philip Hofmann's 2xBHI_small_compact_pretrain, CC BY 4.0) is fetched when you tick the option. Not yet as good as Lightroom: on RAWs Lightroom resolves more foliage texture and finer high-ISO grain, because it enlarges from the sensor data.
- **Second window for a second display (Window ▸ Second Window, ⇧F).** One more window shows the photo you are working on, live: every edit appears in it at the same moment as in the main window, drawn by the same GPU display path (in Develop the main window's frame is copied on the graphics card, nothing is developed twice). It has its own *Fit* and *100 %* (click the photo to switch at that point, drag to pan, Space or Z), and *Lock* (L) keeps showing that photo while the main window moves on to others, as Lightroom's locked secondary loupe does; unlocking follows the main window again. In the Library it shows the active photo. Where the window needs more pixels than the main frame has (100 %, or Fit on a larger display) or the main window shows clipping warnings, it develops its own frame once the edit rests (on a Mac mini M4 Pro a 2400 × 1350 px region at 100 % in 0.1–0.4 s). F puts it full screen on its display (Esc leaves), and its place, size, display, full screen and zoom are kept: it opens again where it was if it was open at quit, otherwise on a display other than the main window's. A Develop slider drag costs the same with it open (input to preview 10.5 / 22 ms median / 95th percentile against 10 / 22 ms closed, 0.25 ms per frame for the second window). On Linux, where the main window's previews are drawn by the web view, the second window shows the same frames the same way (the main window's own preview images during a drag, its own developed frames at 100 % and for larger displays); a drag in the main window costs the same while it is closed (input to preview 33 / 49 ms median / 95th percentile, as before) and about 4 ms more while it is open (37.5 / 52.5 ms, Ryzen 5 3600X, RTX 3080, KDE Plasma on Wayland). Wayland lets no program choose where a window goes, so there it opens where the desktop puts it, and full screen returns to the display it was on. Grid, survey and compare views in the second window are not available yet.
- **Import: check photos at 100 % and rate them before they are imported; a memory card opens the import.** In the import dialog's large view (double-click a photo, or Space) a click on the photo or Z shows it at 100 % around the pointer; drag to look around, and the zoom and spot stay while you step with the arrow keys, so a series can be checked for focus one key per photo. The 100 % view is real pixels: the camera's embedded JPEG where it has the sensor's size (most Canon, Nikon and Pentax files), otherwise the RAW itself decoded at full size (Sony, Panasonic, Olympus and Fujifilm embed only about a third to two thirds of it) — never an enlarged preview. Those full-size pixels are now made as soon as a photo is shown in the large view (and the next one), not only after Z: once a 24–40 MP RAW has been on screen about two seconds, Z or a click shows 100 % without waiting (it waited 1–2 s); a very large file such as an 81 MP high-resolution shot still takes several seconds. P includes a photo, X leaves it out, 0–5 rate it, in the grid and the large view; the rating goes with the imported copy (catalog and XMP), nothing is written to the card. Connected memory cards are listed in the dialog as one-click sources, and inserting a card opens the import with that card (Preferences › General, on by default; not during an import or tethering, and after a running export). *Eject the card after the import* ejects it once every photo came off it copied and verified. In the grid Space now opens the large view, as in the Library (it used to tick or untick the photo; P and X do that now).
- **Import window:** drag any edge or corner to resize, or maximize and restore from the header (also by double-click). The window remembers its size across restarts.
- **AI Denoise no longer darkens and tints high-ISO shadows** (new edits, AI Denoise version 4): with AI Denoise on, deep shadows of high-ISO photos came out darker and with a stronger colour cast than with it off — on 60 test pairs at ISO 3200–51200 the cast of lifted flat shadows was 1.0–1.9 with it on against 0.7–0.8 with it off, and on a camera the network was not trained on (EOS M100) 2.7–6.6 against 0.6–2.7, a visible yellow-green shift. AI Denoise now keeps each area's level and colour as the sensor recorded them and only removes the noise: the cast is the same as with AI Denoise off (0.7 on the 60 pairs, 0.7–2.1 on the EOS M100), blacks are no longer crushed (−0.2 L instead of −3.4 at ISO 12800 and above), and detail and edges are kept as before. Noise in flat areas is unchanged at the median and, at ISO 12800 and above, 14 % lower in luminance and about half in colour in the deepest shadows; on single photos whose shadows were crushed before, the noise that was hidden in black is visible again. Photos already edited with AI Denoise keep their look; to move one to the new version, reset Noise reduction and set AI Denoise again (the Library filter "Older AI Denoise version" lists such photos once their edit is saved again). The model is unchanged, and denoised results already cached are reused.
- **Raising Whites moves at Lightroom's and Capture One's pace** (new edits): the first half of the Whites slider did little — Whites +50 brightened the brightest tones only as much as Lightroom's Whites +26 or Capture One's White +23. Whites +50 now does about what their +50 does (on 48 test photos the brightest tenth brightens by 4.7 L*, Lightroom 4.9, Capture One 5.5; before 2.5), +100 stays where it was (at both editors' +100 on half the photos), and lowering Whites is unchanged; copied Whites (tool copy, Copy Settings, presets, Edit all selected) brighten the other photo as much as they did this one, whichever version either photo was edited with, without changing anything else on it. Photos edited before keep their look (recipe `toneVersion` 17).
- **Lowering Whites darkens only the bright tones** (new edits): Whites −100 under the RAW look also darkened the upper midtones (L* 60 by about 5) and left bright areas 2.5 L* darker than Capture One's White. It now leaves tones below L* 40 alone and fades in towards white, so skin, foliage and mid greys keep their brightness while skies, snow and highlights come down (on 24 test photos at −100: bright areas −5.1 L*, Capture One −5.3; L* 60 −0.9, Capture One −1.0); texture in bright areas is kept as before. Photos edited before keep their look.
- **Raising Shadows keeps the midtones and looks less over-sharpened** (new edits): at the same lifted shadow level as Capture One's Shadow, Shadows +100 under the RAW look lifted L* 40–60 by about 4 L* more and raised the shadows' fine texture 20–30 % more, so the photo looked flat and crisp in the shadows. The lift now eases off in the midtones and the shadows' texture is lifted as it is, without the extra boost (on 12 held-out test photos at +100: midtones −0.9 L* from Capture One instead of +3.3; shadow texture 1.08–1.10 × Capture One's instead of 1.27–1.34 ×; the darkest tones are lifted as before). Photos edited before keep their look.
- **Lowering Highlights no longer leaves a light rim at strong edges** (new edits): at Highlights −100 under the RAW look the bright side of strong edges (skies behind branches, windows, lit walls against shadow) stayed lighter than the area next to it, a halo about 1 L* stronger than Camera Raw's at the same recovery. The recovery now hardly lets a dark neighbour across such an edge count, so the rim is gone while clouds and other bright texture keep their detail (on 48 test photos at −100: rim 0.3 L* below Camera Raw's instead of 1.0 above, as far below Capture One's; bright-area detail between the two; −50 and raising Highlights as before). Photos edited before keep their look.
- **Lowering Blacks deepens the shadows, not just the deepest tones** (new edits): Blacks −50/−100 under the RAW look changed only tones below L* 30, so at the same darkening of the darkest areas the lower shadows (L* 20–30) stayed 5–9 L* lighter than in Capture One and Camera Raw, and the steep step up to L* 30 made the shadows' fine texture look harsher than in either. Blacks now fades out gradually up to L* 80 as Capture One's Black does, and the deepest pixels reach true black (on 48 test photos at the same darkening: tones within about 1 L* of Capture One's, shadow texture close to Capture One's instead of 0.3–0.4 above it; −50 darkens the darkest areas as much as Capture One's −50). Raising Blacks is unchanged; photos edited before keep their look.
- **Contrast keeps the lower midtones where Camera Raw and Capture One put them** (new edits): at the same overall contrast, Contrast +100 under the RAW look darkened L* 30–40 by 2.9 / 3.5 more than Camera Raw / Capture One, and Contrast −100 lifted L* 40–50 by 1.6 / 2.1 more than either. New edits (recipe `toneVersion` 15) pivot the curve lower: on the 48 photos of the slider atlas, comparing each photo with the same photo from Camera Raw and from Capture One at the same overall contrast, the median difference per 10-L* band is now either above one editor and below the other, or at most 0.52 L* from the closer editor, at ±50 and ±100 (the atlas's tone excess; before up to 2.85; at most 0.38 on the 24 photos not used to choose it). Contrast 0, the Standard look and saved edits are unchanged; dragging Contrast is as fast as before.
- **Structure moves at Lightroom's pace** (new edits): Structure was far stronger per unit than Lightroom's Texture — +50 already sharpened fine detail more than Texture +100 on 47 of the slider atlas's 48 photos (and more than Capture One's Structure +100 on all 48). The slider now follows Texture's travel: Structure ±50 and ±100 give about the fine-detail gain of Texture ±50 and ±100 (fitted on 24 photos; on the 24 others Texture +50 / +100 / −50 / −100 now equal Structure +46 / +90 / −49 / −96, where the old slider reached them at about +18 / +28 / −24 / −40). The filter is unchanged; the slider gives finer control over the range that matters. Photos edited before keep their look (recipe `structureVersion` 2).
- **Clarity and Structure draw much smaller halos at strong edges** (new edits): raising Clarity lit up the bright side of strong dark edges (branches, window frames and roofs against the sky) about 0.9 L* more than Camera Raw at the same local contrast, and Structure darkened the dark side of bright edges into a thin rim when raised (or lifted it when lowered), 0.6–0.8 L* more than the better of Camera Raw and Capture One. Both now measure their local contrast against a base that leans to the pixel's own side of an edge, so texture keeps its lift while edges keep more of their tone. On the slider atlas's 48 photos (median over the photos) raised Clarity's (+50, +100) edge halos are now at or below Camera Raw's, and Structure's at ±50 / ±100 within 0.4 L* of the better editor (Capture One's Structure leaves almost none), and Structure ±50 / ±100 keep Texture's travel. Raised Clarity gives a little less medium-scale contrast per unit than before (+100 about Capture One's +100); lowered Clarity is unchanged. Earlier edits keep their look.
- **Vibrance works like Lightroom's** (new edits): raising Vibrance now favours blues, purples and cyans — skies and water gain clearly more colour than reds, oranges and skin — and spares already saturated colours; lowering it mutes every colour but cuts saturated ones about half as much as muted ones, where it used to cut them hardest. Vibrance no longer shifts hues (blues drifted towards purple) or changes brightness. On the 48 photos of the slider atlas the blues and purples at +50/+100 now come within 0.03/0.08 of Camera Raw's chroma gain (were 0.18/0.33 short), and saturated colours at −50/−100 within 0.05/0.06 (were cut 0.22/0.24 more); skin tones are raised as before or slightly closer to Camera Raw. Photos edited before keep their look (recipe `vibranceVersion` 2).
- **The colour mixer moves colours as Camera Raw's HSL does** (new edits): each channel's Hue, Saturation and Luminance now has its own hue range and strength, fitted to Camera Raw on 8 photographs and checked on 8 others. Skin (Orange), Yellow and Green move like Camera Raw's at the same slider value; Hue turns blue skies as far as Camera Raw (it turned them up to 29° differently), and strongly coloured areas move more than pale ones. Measured on the slider atlas (16 photographs, ±100): 18 of 36 cells at Camera Raw's level within the noise floor and 12 close, against 1 and 8 before. Still different: Blue Saturation and Luminance between 225° and 240° (a deeper sky blue) and Purple Hue −100. Greys stay untouched, and photos edited before keep their mixer.
- **Temperature behaves like real white balance** (new edits): warming now brightens the photo and leans towards magenta, and cooling darkens it slightly, as in Capture One and Camera Raw (before, Temperature kept brightness fixed and warmed towards green-yellow). +100 now reaches the warmest setting those editors allow instead of going a third beyond it; −100 is unchanged. Tint now has Lightroom's range: ±100 matches its ±150 (before, about half), and moving it towards magenta brightens slightly as in both editors. Saved edits keep their look.
- **Lowering Dehaze adds haze the way Lightroom's does** (new edits): Dehaze −100 softened a photo no more than −50 did — below −50 it only brightened further — and it left the blacks dark, so at the same softening it lifted the midtones about twice as far as Camera Raw (14.6 L* more around mid-grey), washed out more colour and left a light rim on the dark side of edges. Lowering Dehaze now lays a veil as Camera Raw's negative Dehaze does: the blacks rise with the other tones, local contrast keeps falling all the way to −100 (to about half), colour fades less abruptly, and −50 / −100 do about what Lightroom's −50 / −100 do. On the 48 photos of the slider atlas, each compared with Camera Raw at the same softening, the median difference over the photos is now at most 1.1 L* at −50 and 1.6 L* at −100 in any 10-L* tone band (before 4.8 and 14.6), the dark-side rim is in the median 0.2 and 0.6 L* below Camera Raw's (before 2.2 and 3.2 above) and the median chroma within 0.05 of its (before 0.42 short at −100); these are medians, single photos differ more. The response was fitted on 24 of the photos; the other 24 were also looked at while choosing the model, so they are no independent check: on them the largest median tone difference is 1.4 L* at −50 and 2.5 L* at −100. Raising Dehaze is unchanged; photos edited before keep their look (recipe `dehazeVersion`), also when Dehaze is copied onto them.
- **Saturated colours ease into the edge of the output space** (`renderVersion` 12, new edits and never-edited photos): bright, saturated colours (warm light after a warmer white balance or more magenta Tint, red and orange flowers, sunsets) ran into the sRGB edge and were clipped there, leaving flat patches with one channel at its maximum; at Temperature +100 about 1.9 % more of the photo clipped than in Camera Raw. Colours from 95 % of the way to the output space's edge on are now eased towards it at their own hue and stay slightly inside it: a colour inside the space gives up at most a fortieth of its chroma, and a colour beyond it keeps the brightness and colourfulness the clip gave it, just short of the edge. Colours further inside are unchanged, light near-neutral highlights still clip to white, and ProPhoto output is unchanged. At Temperature and Tint +100 the clipped area is now smaller than Camera Raw's, and Vibrance and Saturation keep the colourfulness they had against it. Saved edits keep their look.
- **Sharpening no longer sharpens the noise of even areas** (new edits of RAW photos, at every Masking value including 0): what stands no more than the photo's measured noise out of its neighbourhood — judged as it looks after the photo's tone curve — is left unsharpened, while edges and texture are sharpened as before. On 14 test photos (8 at base ISO, 6 at ISO 3200–12800) the sharpened noise in the flattest quarter of each photo, at the same visible detail as Lightroom at Amount 80, falls from 1.05 to 0.88 times Lightroom's (0.84 on the 7 photos the gate was fitted on, 0.90 on the 7 left out); on the high-ISO photos it is 0.68–0.90 times Lightroom's, below Capture One's on each of the five Capture One reaches, with halos still at most Lightroom's and far below Capture One's. At the same Amount, edges on the noisiest photo (ISO 12800) come out up to 10 % softer than before; raise Amount a little to compensate. Recipe `sharpenVersion` 8; JPEGs and saved edits are unchanged.
- **Sliders step and travel as Lightroom's do** (interface only, no recipe or export change): the number fields now react to Up/Down (±1, Shift ±10; Exposure ±0.10 / Shift ±0.33 EV; Temperature ±50 / Shift ±200 K), a slider under the pointer steps with Up/Down without a click, and a focused slider steps ±5 / Shift ±20 / Alt ±1 (Exposure and Temperature as the fields; before, a field did nothing, Exposure moved 0.01 EV and Shift on Temperature 500 K). Dragging the Temperature slider from As Shot now travels as Lightroom's: from 4050 K at the 150-pt track, +30 pt reaches 6093 K and +60 pt 8314 K (before 6811 K and 21680 K); the whole 2000–50000 K range is still reachable, and a stored Kelvin means what it did. The colour mixer's Hue, Saturation and Luminance sliders drag at least as slowly as on Lightroom's 200-pt track (one unit per point) however narrow the panel is. Measured on one RAW against Lightroom Classic and Capture One in the 2026-11-10 slider use test; the Basic sliders' travel (143 pt here against Lightroom's 150 pt) is unchanged.
- **Alt/Option-click on a Develop group's name resets the group, as in Lightroom** (interface only, no recipe or export change): in Exposure, White balance, Presence, Color mixer (all 8 bands' Hue, Saturation and Luminance), Black & white (its 8 channels) and the other tools, Alt/Option-click on the group's name does what its reset button does (White balance goes back to As Shot), as one undo step named "Reset <group>" in the History; other groups are untouched, and a processing version stays put while a layer uses its slider. The group's right-click menu has a new *Reset group* entry, and the name's tooltip names the shortcut. Alt/Option-click on the rest of the header still opens that tool alone.
- **Find another source for a heal or clone spot.** A spot picks its own source area automatically, and when that one copies something unwanted (a leaf where the sky should be) there was no way to ask for a different one. Select the spot and press `/` (Lightroom's key), the wand button beside it in the spot list, or *Find another source* in its settings and in its right-click menu: the spot searches the photo again and takes the next best source, never the one it has now nor one an earlier press turned down (a candidate within one spot radius of them counts as turned down), so each press finds something new. It is one step in history (*Spot removal*); when no other source fits, the spot keeps the one it has. Saved edits keep their sources and export as before.
- **Interface shade:** choose Darkest, Dark grey or Medium grey in Preferences. Changes apply immediately; Darkest keeps the existing appearance and Viewer background keeps its own setting.
- **Grey interface shades have clearer panel depth.** Sunken controls, panels and raised rows are easier to distinguish in Dark grey and Medium grey. Pressed buttons have a clearer dark fill.

### Fixes

- **Batch edits of photos never opened look the same as on the opened photo**: after an import, Edit all selected, a paste, sync, preset, style or Auto on photos never opened in Develop gave them the oldest responses of every tool the edit did not name, so Contrast −100 on two fresh photos rendered with the legacy tone curve on the one not opened and today's on the other. A never-edited photo now starts every batch edit with the same processing versions as opening it would give, so the same edit renders the same on both. Photos edited before keep their look.
- **Copying one tool onto an older edit no longer changes its layers or other settings**: copying Structure, Clarity, Vibrance, Temperature, Tint, the colour mixer, Sharpening, Luminance noise reduction, Defringe or a tone slider (tool copy, presets and styles, Edit all selected) from a photo edited with a newer version of the tool onto an older edit used to switch the whole photo to the newer version, so the older edit's brushed and graded layers with that tool, and its other tone sliders, changed too. Now the older edit keeps its version wherever something else on it uses the tool, and the copied value is converted into it: exactly for Whites and Structure between versions that differ only in how far the slider travels, as close as that version allows for the others (measured differences in docs/RECIPE_SCHEMA.md). Photos without anything else using the tool still take the newer version, so the copy looks exactly as on the source. Copy Settings with *Processing versions* ticked still applies the source's versions as chosen.
- **A batch export no longer loses photos when another program fills the graphics card's memory for a moment**: a photo whose render ran out of graphics memory (a game, another editor or a GPU job holding it) is now exported again after the app frees its own GPU caches and waits (0.5 s, 2 s, 5 s), into the same file name, with the same pixels as an export without the shortage. A photo that still finds no memory after that is listed as failed with "Not enough graphics memory for this photo" and the rest of the batch goes on; nothing is skipped silently.
- **The Library lists AI Denoise edits made before version 4 as "Older AI Denoise version" right away** (catalog schema 26, no recipe or export change): the filter and badge only knew a photo's older AI Denoise once its edit was saved again, so photos edited with version 3 by an earlier build were missing from the list. Opening the library once now recomputes that fact for every photo and virtual copy with AI Denoise on; recipes, sidecars and photos are not touched, and a photo's AI Denoise version stays as it was.
- **Safer 100 % views during import.** Camera crop, lens corrections and decode setup use the photo bytes already read into memory, so pulling a memory card cannot invalidate a mapped file during this render. Source photos and existing sidecars stay untouched.
- **Fresh clipping-scope masks when opening a photo.** Zoomed-in scope updates now start with fresh mask-shape state and use the base-size masks from the second update when their shapes stay unchanged.
- **The colour mixer's and the B&W mix's targeted adjustment pick the colour you see and move at Lightroom's pace.** Pressing the *H*, *S*, *L* or B&W target on the photo read the colour of the photo before white balance, tone and calibration, so after a warmer or cooler white balance a skin tone or a sky could move a band next to the one it looks like. It now reads the colour the mixer itself receives at that point (B&W: the rendered colour before the conversion), shares the drag between the bands the colour lies between by where it sits between them, and moves them by one unit per point of drag in all (was half a unit to the strongest band), as Lightroom does. Exports do not change.
- **Clipping triangles stay right while you drag zoomed in.** At 100 % and closer, the histogram's clipping triangles during a slider drag came from a small averaged copy of the photo, so a few clipped pixels (a specular highlight, a dead-black speck) were averaged away and the triangle lit only when you let go. The drag now also checks, for every small block of the photo, its brightest and its darkest pixel with your edit and the layer masks at that pixel, so such pixels light the triangle during the drag too. The triangle's colour can still miss a channel that only another pixel of the same block clips until you let go; the histogram and the other scopes are unchanged. The check takes turns between the brightest and the darkest pixels from one scope update to the next and reads a layer mask you are reshaping at the scopes' resolution, so zoomed drags with layer masks stay about as quick as without it on integrated graphics (Radeon 680M: within 0.6 ms of the drag's preview time).
- **A folder moved to another drive keeps its permissions under a strict umask.** With a umask that takes away the group's or others' permission bits (say `077`, common on shared or hardened accounts), a folder copied to another drive got only its owner's access (`700` instead of `750`), so a colleague could no longer open it; folders now get their own permissions outright, as files already did.

### Changes to existing edits

- **Saved edits export exactly as in alpha.12.** Every changed response above is behind its own recipe version (`toneVersion` 12–17, `structureVersion` 2 and 3, `clarityVersion` 3, `vibranceVersion` 2, `temperatureVersion` 2, `tintVersion` 2, `hslVersion` 2, `dehazeVersion` 2, `sharpenVersion` 8, `renderVersion` 12, `aiDenoiseVersion` 4), so photos you have edited keep their look; checked by exporting saved recipes with alpha.12 and with this release as 16-bit TIFFs (20 recipes on 6 photos, 120 exports, and 49 recipes on 12 photos, 314 exports): all identical.
- **Never-edited photos develop with the current processing,** as before, so they export differently from alpha.12: never-edited RAWs with the new Sharpening response (*Sharpening no longer sharpens the noise of even areas*) and, like never-edited JPEGs and TIFFs, with the eased output-space edge (*Saturated colours ease into the edge of the output space*). The new slider responses do not change a never-edited photo; they apply once you edit it.
- **Batch edits of never-opened photos** (Edit all selected, paste, sync, presets, styles, Auto) now start from the current processing versions instead of the oldest ones, as opening the photo first would (see *Fixes*); photos edited before keep theirs.

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls or open work; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **Lowered Dehaze** lays a veil like Camera Raw's, but on photos outside the test set it still lifts mid and bright tones about 4–6 L* more than Camera Raw: Camera Raw adapts its veil to each photo, which ours does not yet.
- **Colour mixer drags** with Hue, Saturation and Luminance moved take about 2.4 ms longer per preview frame than before (5.6 ms instead of 3.2 ms on a 2304 × 1728 frame, Apple M5 Pro); our budget is 1 ms.
- **Super Resolution** runs on the graphics processor through Core ML on macOS. Where no GPU path is available it falls back to the processor, which is very slow (about 8 minutes for a 24 MP photo); its speed on Windows and Linux graphics cards is not measured yet. On RAWs Lightroom's Super Resolution resolves more foliage texture and finer high-ISO grain.
- **Second window:** grid, survey and compare views are not available in it yet; on Linux under Wayland it opens where the desktop puts it.
- **Windows** is experimental: unsigned, tested on one NVIDIA graphics card and on a build machine without a graphics card. It does not run without a Vulkan or DirectX 12 driver (Microsoft's software renderer is refused).
- The known gaps of alpha.12 and earlier not named here remain.

### Highlights

- **Search by what a photo shows (Library, catalogs and sessions).** The search box has a *Text* / *Content* switch, remembered per library. *Content* sorts the photos shown by how well each matches what you type ("a dog", "bride in a church", "Luftaufnahme"; English, German and other languages), best first, under "Sorted by match"; it is an order, not a filter, so every photo stays in the view and your filters still apply first. Photos not indexed yet come last with a note. The model (Google's SigLIP 2, the one automatic keywords use, Apache-2.0) runs on your computer and is downloaded only when you click *Download search model* (1.5 GB, 1.2 GB if automatic keywords installed its image part); after that, the open library is indexed in the background from its thumbnails on two low-priority threads, pausing while an export, a batch edit or automatic keywords run ("Indexed 4,213 of 10,000" beside the box, progress and Cancel in the activity centre). The index lives in the library's catalog and moves with it. On a Mac mini M4 Pro: about 9 minutes per 10,000 photos with thumbnails already made, a query in about 25 ms at 10,000 photos; the text model (about 0.75 GB in memory) loads on the first query and is unloaded after 5 minutes without one. Smart albums have no content rule (a ranking has no fixed set).
- **See what Sharpening does while you drag (Develop).** Hold Option (⌥; Alt on Windows and Linux) while dragging *Masking*: the photo turns into the sharpening mask, white where it sharpens, black where Masking holds it back (flat skin, sky, noise), at the zoom you are looking at. While you drag *Amount*, *Radius* or *Detail* with Option, the photo shows in grey, so sharpening is judged without colour. Pressing Option during a drag shows it, letting go of Option or of the slider brings the photo back; on these four sliders Option no longer slows the drag (move the pointer away from the slider for finer steps). The slider tooltips say so. It is a view only: your edit and every export are unchanged. The mask is the one the sharpening applies, drawn by the same code; a drag with it is as fast as without (Mac mini M4 Pro). The first time it is used after an update it can take about two seconds to appear while the graphics code for it is prepared.
- **The brightest tones of a RAW keep their gradation instead of clipping to flat white** (new edits): the RAW look reached white about one stop below where many RAWs clip, so the top stop rendered as one flat white or near-white tone — with Exposure raised (or a tone curve inverted, as for a camera scan of a negative) it showed as blotches with hard edges. Those tones now approach white gradually and near-white colours converge on white, as in Lightroom. Tones below the very top are unchanged (on 50 bright-sky photos the median change is under 0.2 L*); photos edited before keep their look. ([#615](https://github.com/rawtonic-app/rawtonic/issues/615))
- **Lowering Whites keeps the texture in clouds, snow and white fabric, as Capture One does.** Whites −100 darkened every bright pixel by its own amount, which flattened the texture of bright areas to 0.87 of the unedited photo's (24 test photos not used to choose it; Capture One 1.02, Camera Raw 0.91). New edits (recipe `toneVersion` 10) darken bright areas by the amount of their surroundings and keep each pixel's difference from them, so texture keeps its contrast: 1.03 at −100 and −50 (Capture One 1.02 / 1.01), at about the same darkening as before (−7.8 L* against −8.1). Like Capture One's White slider this leaves a slight light rim on the bright side of strong edges, such as sky around bare branches: +1.3 L* in the median (Capture One +1.0; at most +5.8 against Capture One's +7.0). Raising Whites, other looks and saved edits are unchanged. Dragging Whites below 0 renders about 0.9 ms slower per step.
- **Blacks deepens the blacks without flattening the shadows.** Lowering Blacks darkened the dark areas the way Camera Raw does, by reducing the light in them: their texture (fabric, fur, dark wood, the dark patches of a colour chart) lost a fifth of its contrast at −100 (0.78 of the unedited photo's on 24 test photos not used to choose it; Camera Raw 0.79, Capture One 1.02) and the darkening reached far into the shadows and midtones (L* 30–40 down by 25). New edits (recipe `toneVersion` 9) set a black point on the displayed lightness instead: the dark tones move down by the same amount, so they keep their texture, and tones above L* 30 stay as they are. At −100 the dark areas keep 1.13 of their texture (−50: 1.12; Capture One 1.02, Camera Raw 0.79 / 0.99) and darken about as much as before (median −8.5 L* against −9.0), and the tones that would fall below black are eased into it instead of clipped: 0.5 % more pixels end at black in the median (Capture One 2.6 %, Camera Raw 21 %). Raising Blacks, other looks and saved edits are unchanged; an edit made before keeps its Blacks until you reset the Basic tool. Dragging Blacks renders about 1 ms faster.
- **Sharpening Masking keeps noise out of skies and walls.** On noisy photos Masking let the noise of bright, even areas through as if it were texture: at Masking 50 an ISO 3200 photo of a white wall kept half of the wall's sharpening (grain), where Lightroom keeps 6 %. Masking now takes the photo's measured noise out before it decides, and judges the rest as it looks after the photo's tone curve. On 14 test photos (8 at base ISO, 6 at ISO 3200–12800, none of them tuned on alone) the flat-area sharpening kept beyond Lightroom's at the same Masking value falls from 0.11 to 0.02 (0.06 to 0.01 on the photos left out of the fit), the wall above from 51 % to 2 %, while texture keeps about as much sharpening as before (difference from Lightroom 0.05–0.06). On low-contrast photos Masking 50 keeps a little more texture than before (Canon R6 18 → 27 %, Lightroom 41 %); lower Masking keeps more, with no more flat noise than Lightroom at Lightroom's texture on 12 of the 14 photos (two low-contrast ones keep up to 4 % more). New edits (recipe `sharpenVersion` 7, RAW photos with Masking); without Masking, on JPEGs and in saved edits sharpening is unchanged.
- **Black & white mix sliders reach as far as Lightroom's.** Pulling a B&W mix slider down to −100 darkened its colours about 15 % less than in Lightroom and Capture One, which both darken more and more the further a slider goes (−100 about 2.3 times −50, Rawtonic 1.8 times): a blue sky at −100 stayed lighter than a photographer expects. New edits use a mix response fitted to Camera Raw on 15 photographs (portraits, neon at night, magenta light, blue and dusk skies, flowers, foliage): across all channels and colours the difference from Lightroom's response falls by a third (median 0.018 → 0.012 Oklab lightness on photos left out of the fit) and −100 now reaches 0.94 of Lightroom's change instead of 0.84. A conversion with every slider at 0 looks exactly as before, and edits saved with a mix keep their look.
- **Black & white Auto handles colour casts and skies.** Auto darkened the colour that fills a photo: a room lit orange by tungsten light went grey and dull, a green lawn too dark, and on skies and sunsets it moved the mix only half as far as Lightroom's Auto. Auto now lifts the colours that fill and light the frame and moves as far as Lightroom does: on 73 photos (58 cameras and the 15 B&W test photos) the grey image differs from Lightroom's Auto by 0.18 Oklab L × 100 on average instead of 0.42 (on the halves not used for fitting: 0.16 and 0.21), the tungsten room by 1.9 instead of 7.4 when left out of the fit, and no photo moved further from Lightroom's by more than 0.2. Auto writes ordinary slider values, so saved edits are unchanged.
- **Whites sets the white point.** Raising Whites brightened the whole photo instead of its whites: at +100 the midtones (L* 40–70) rose by 15–19 L* (Capture One 2–9, Camera Raw 11–16), while the brightest tones were squeezed together below white, so bright areas lost a sixth of their texture and Whites could never make them white. New edits (recipe `toneVersion` 8) lift the midtones only slightly and stretch the bright tones up to white, as both editors do, with fewer blown highlights: on 24 test photos not used to choose it, Whites +100 keeps 1.07 of the bright areas' texture (Capture One 1.11, Camera Raw 0.91, before 0.84), turns 5.3 % of the pixels white in the median (Capture One 5.4 %, Camera Raw 5.2 %), clips a colour channel of bright saturated colours less than either (4.8 % against 5.5 % and 11.2 %) and keeps more of their colour and never turns a brighter tone darker than a dimmer one (before up to 3.8 L*; Capture One 2.3, Camera Raw 7.8). At +50 it clips about as little as both editors (0.4 % of the pixels). Lowering Whites, Blacks and saved edits are unchanged; an edit made before keeps its Whites until you reset the Basic tool.
- **Night photos keep their lights when you lower Highlights.** On dark frames (night streets, candles, lamps, interiors at night) Highlights −100 darkened the lights and the upper midtones far beyond both Capture One and Camera Raw: broad lit areas went down by 47 L* in the median of 19 night frames, where Camera Raw lowers them by 33 and Capture One much less; a toy on a dark table went from −15.7 (Capture One) and −47.3 (Camera Raw) to −58.8, a murky picture. New edits now lower them between the two editors on all 8 night frames where both were measured (median −23 L*), leave the midtones of a night frame almost untouched, as Capture One does, and keep more of the lights' structure (local contrast 0.54 → 0.67 of the unedited photo; Camera Raw 0.87). Night frames made of small lights in the dark still get a stronger response. Frames that are not dark develop exactly as before, and saved edits keep their look.
- **Shadows keeps the texture of the shadows it lifts.** Raising Shadows flattened texture in dark areas (fabric, foliage, rock, fur): at +100 they kept 1.13 times their local contrast against the unedited photo, Camera Raw 1.25 and Capture One 1.37 on the same 42 test photos (lifting shadows should show more of their texture, not less). New edits now lift texture with its surroundings and keep its contrast on screen: 1.30 at +100 (also 1.30 on the 20 photos not used for tuning, where Camera Raw keeps 1.17 and Capture One 1.33) and 1.11 at +50 (Camera Raw 1.09). Dark areas are lifted as far as before in the median (+18.0 L* against +17.9), the mid-shadows about 1 L* more; on four of the 42 photos the lift changes by 1.1 to 5.2 L*: three move towards Camera Raw's and Capture One's lift, and a near-black photo of a cat is now lifted 1.2 L* more than by Camera Raw (Capture One lifts it as Rawtonic did). Edges of lifted shadows show a faint light rim, about as Camera Raw's and far fainter than Capture One's. Saved edits keep their look.
- **Relight on side-lit faces and at full Strength.** On portraits already lit from one side, new lights left a grey, plastic-looking patch on the lit cheek (the photo's own light was mistaken for skin shine and removed) and a grey, smooth mask when lighting the shadow side (the light's skin sheen painted where the photo has no detail). At Strength 100 skin burnt to flat orange on JPEGs and clipped on RAWs: up to 70 % of the face had a clipped channel. New lights keep the photo's own shine, put sheen only where the photo shows the skin, and roll strong light off before it clips: on 13 portraits (side-lit, outdoor and RAW among them) clipped skin at Strength 100 fell to at most 2.4 % (median 0). At their own values the Side, Loop, Butterfly, Edge, Soft fill and Window set-ups keep the face's exposure (median change per photo within 0.4 L*); Split, Warm evening and Blinds at sunset, whose strong keys clipped skin before, come out 2–3 L* darker on the face (median; up to 6 L*). Existing lights render as before, also beside a new light where the new one does not reach; *Use the current ranges* moves one to the new ranges.
- **Highlights keeps the structure of bright areas.** Lowering Highlights flattened clouds, lit walls and other bright texture: at −100 they kept about 0.8 of their local contrast, while Camera Raw and Capture One keep all of it. New edits now keep it too (1.01 on 42 test photos, 1.01 on 46 others), still with the faintest dark rims of the three next to bright areas. Bright areas are lowered about as far as before: 0.2 L* less in the median, up to 4.5 L* less on some photos. Saved edits keep their look.

Rawtonic cut the frame first, so the correction lacked the pixels outside it and the photo was framed up to 2 % tighter than the camera's JPEG. Never-edited Sony photos with such a frame now develop the whole sensor, corrected as the camera does, and start with the camera's frame as a crop you can widen, as Canon's in-camera aspect ratios already did. Measured against the camera's own JPEG (median, JPEG pixels): HX95 16:9 from 11.9 to 0.35, ZV-1 II from 4.6 to 0.50, RX0 II from 3.4 to 0.45, ZV-E10 from 2.0 to 0.38 (Camera Raw: 0.54–0.92); the other aspect frames tested stay as close as before. Photos you have edited keep their frame and export exactly as before.
- **Proof sheets for clients.** *File › Proof Sheet…* (also in the thumbnail menu) lays the selected photos, or every photo shown, out as numbered contact sheets: paper size (A4, A3, Letter, Legal, Tabloid or your own) and orientation, margins, columns × rows and spacing, a caption under each photo from the export naming tokens (number, file name, rating, label, title, date …; `{rating|stars}` prints the rating as ★, nothing for an unrated photo), a header and footer with `{job}`, `{date}`, `{count}` and page numbers, the Export form's watermark (when it has none yet, type its text in the dialog: it is kept in the Export form without switching the watermark on for exports) and print sharpening. The dialog shows page 1 as it will be written. Optional crop marks at the corners of every cell (off by default; PDF as vector lines, also on the JPEG pages) help when the sheet is cut. Print sharpening comes in Low, Standard (the default, as before) and High. The settings are remembered per library. The button now shows a ring that fills as the files are written. 19 NEFs, each to a web JPEG and a 16-bit print TIFF, on an M5 Pro laptop: 12.2 s → 9.9 s, as fast as the command-line export. The same export with a spinner on screen: 11.6 s → 9.9 s. With *Reduce motion* turned on in macOS they stand still.

Import from Catalog leaves out a new photo whose original is not in the other catalog and says so, instead of refusing the import.

Exported catalogs now carry what they had in common with the catalog they came from, and an import records it too, so a photo changed on one side only is named as such again, for edits, ratings and other metadata.
- **Heal fills where a spot crosses an edge.** A heal copies one other part of the photo, so a spot on a corner, a window frame or a railing carried the neighbouring object into it or broke the line. Heal now looks at the edges around each new spot or stroke: where strong edges cross it, Remove's fill continues them instead; dust on sky and other flat areas is still healed, which keeps its grain. On 167 test spots the fill halves the error at junctions (LPIPS 0.067 → 0.035) and the choice beats both always-heal and always-fill on photos it was not tuned on. The spot list names each one *Heal* or *Fill*, and the arrows beside it switch it either way; *Fill across edges* under Heal turns the choice off. A fill takes about a second, shown on the spot, and the brush keeps painting meanwhile. Clone, Remove and existing spots are unchanged.
- **Cleaner small removals.** Remove's fill of spots, strokes and wires (holes under about 200 px thick) carries the photo's grain better: at high ISO the error drops by 10 %, overall by 2 %. Removals already made keep their pixels.
- **Nothing is lost when one side wins.** *Keep current* over a photo the other catalog changed keeps that catalog's edit as a snapshot ("From …, not imported"), as *Replace* keeps the replaced one. The rating, label, flag, keywords or text either choice leaves out of the photo are listed in the import's result and kept in the catalog's `import-reports` folder. An edit whose LUT or camera profile is missing compares by where the file sits in its catalog, so importing the same work again keeps no second copy of the snapshot.
- **Three Windows downloads, and help when Windows blocks one.** A tester's PC deleted the unsigned Windows installer right after download. Each release now has an MSI installer (the recommended download; for all users, asks for administrator permission), the installer for your user only (`-setup.exe`) and a portable zip that runs from any folder without installing. Before a release, every Windows download is scanned with Microsoft Defender on a Windows build machine (evidence, not a promise for every PC). The README and the website explain what to do when the browser, Windows Security or SmartScreen holds a download back. The downloads are still unsigned; free signing through the SignPath Foundation is prepared (`docs/CODE_SIGNING.md`).
- **Scopes stay live while you drag zoomed in.** At 100 % or closer, a slider drag renders only the visible part of the photo, and the histogram, waveform, parade and vectorscope stood still until you let go. They now keep describing the whole photo during the drag, as in Lightroom and Capture One, about ten times a second, from a small full-frame render beside each frame. Radeon 680M, Exposure drags at 100 %: scope updates 0 → 10.1 a second; input to preview p50 12.0 → 12.5 ms, p95 22 → 25.5 ms. Exports are unchanged.
- **Slider drags with the scopes open are quicker.** While you dragged a slider with the waveform, RGB waveform, parade or vectorscope shown, each scope update (about ten a second) held up the next preview frames: the scope image travelled as raw pixels for the window to unpack, and the scopes were computed on every processor core. Scope images now travel as compact PNGs the window decodes on its own, a drag's scopes are computed on two background threads, and the RGB waveform no longer computes and sends an unused luma waveform beside it. On a 6-core Linux PC, input to preview with the RGB waveform open fell from 27 to 20 ms (median) and from 44 to 36 ms (slow frames), against 18 / 29–32 ms with the scopes closed; the scopes still update about ten times a second, and they show the same pixels as before.
- **Fujifilm lens distortion corrected closer to the camera on current bodies** (recipe `cameraGeometryVersion` 10, new edits and never-edited RAWs): the lens-correction data of current Fujifilm bodies (nine curve points that reach past the frame's corner: X-Trans IV and V, X-H2S, X-S20, GFX100 family) describe the sensor's whole readout width, not only the developed frame, just as in Sports Finder mode. Rawtonic now reads them that way on every frame. Against the cameras' own JPEGs on 151 Fujifilm RAWs the median distance falls from 0.73 to 0.54 px: X-T30 III 1.64 → 0.13, X-H2S 1.49 → 0.50, X-T5 0.58 → 0.31 (largest error 8.1 → 1.1 px), X-Pro3 0.47 → 0.16, GFX100RF 0.91 → 0.67; no photo got worse by more than 0.13 px. Older bodies (X-Trans I–III, GFX 50S / 50R, compacts), whose data refer to the frame, develop exactly as before, and edited photos keep their look.
- **Fujifilm Sports Finder photos framed and corrected like the camera** (recipe `cameraGeometryVersion` 9, new edits and never-edited RAWs): in Sports Finder mode (and the electronic shutter's 1.25× crop) a Fujifilm RAF keeps only the middle of the sensor, but its lens-correction data still describe the whole sensor. Rawtonic applied them to the crop as if it were the whole sensor, so a telephoto shot's pincushion correction zoomed in 1.2 % too far. The distortion correction now reads the data at the crop's place on the sensor: an X-T5 Sports Finder photo at 222 mm matches the camera's JPEG to 0.22 px median instead of 13.7. Photos without a crop mode develop exactly as before, and edited photos keep their look.
- **Compressed RAFs of the Fujifilm X-T2, X-Pro2, X-E3 and X-T20 now show the full 6000 × 4000 frame for new edits** (recipe `sourceFrameVersion` 3, new edits and never-edited RAWs): lossless and lossy compressed RAFs of these bodies were cut by 96 px on the right (5904 × 4000), because the decoder applied the uncompressed files' border to the narrower compressed data. They now develop the frame the camera records and its JPEG shows, like the same bodies' uncompressed RAFs (rawler 0.8.0's compressed-mode selection). Photos you have edited keep their frame and export exactly as before; no other file decodes differently (1969 of 1973 raw.pixls.us files unchanged).
- **Fewer colour fringes on bright edges at moderate ISO** (recipe `fringeVersion` 6, new RAW edits and never-edited RAWs): the edge-fringe correction used its narrow window on every photo noisier than about ISO 3200, so olive and purple rims stayed along bright edges on cameras such as the Canon EOS M50 Mark II. Photos of moderate noise now get the wider window too (Canon EOS M50 Mark II, EOS M3, Nikon Z 6II and Panasonic GM1S in the test set: fewer fringe pixels on all four, fewer stray colour specks on three, about as many on the M50 Mark II); very noisy photos are unchanged. Saved edits render exactly as before.
- **Windows: the installers tell you about the update check before installing.** The setup program has a Privacy page, and the MSI says it on its first dialog: the only thing Rawtonic sends on its own is the startup check for new versions (GitHub, at most once every 24 hours), and you can turn it off in Preferences → General. The portable zip shows the same notice once on its first start, and Rawtonic makes no update check before you have seen it. The publisher shown in Apps & Features and in the file properties is now "Rawtonic Project"; updating over an earlier installation keeps working.

### Fixes

- **Exports from the app no longer crash on some AMD graphics under Linux**: on Radeon GPUs with the Mesa RADV driver every export from the app ended the app with a stack overflow (the command-line export was not affected). Render and export threads now get a fixed 8 MiB stack on every platform, and the render needs far less of it (about 40 KB down to the graphics driver instead of 2 MB). On Windows the app's and the command line's main thread now get the same 8 MiB instead of Windows' 1 MiB, so a render at start can no longer end the app without a message. Exported files are byte-identical to before.
- **An edit is no longer saved onto a different image when a sync tool replaces the open photo's file.** If another program replaces the file of the photograph open in Develop with another image of the same name, saving the edit and exporting the photo (also an export queued before the file changed) are refused with "This photo's file was replaced on disk since you opened it. Reload to see the new file with your edit applied." instead of writing the edit of the old image onto the new one; your edit stays in the session and a Reload action on the "not saved" notice opens the photo again with your edit applied, which also drops the old image's cached decode. A file whose only change is its time (`touch`, a copy without times, a cloud mount rounding times) is the same photograph as before and saves as usual (checked, covered by a test).
- **A JPEG export whose source carries more than 64 KB of EXIF is a valid JPEG again.** A camera's maker note plus other values can exceed the 65,527 bytes one JPEG segment holds; Rawtonic wrote the oversized block anyway, which produced a file no program could open, and still reported the export as done. Now the largest capture-only values (maker note, user comment, print and tone data, thumbnails) are left out, largest first, until the EXIF fits; authorship and rights, make, model, lens, capture time, exposure, GPS (when kept), colour space, the output size and the AI marking are never dropped. PNG, WebP, TIFF and AVIF exports have no such limit and keep all EXIF (checked, covered by tests).
- **A JPEG export keeps its keywords, caption, rights and AI marking when the XMP is larger than 64 KB.** One JPEG segment holds 65,502 bytes of XMP; with thousands of keywords or a long caption the whole XMP used to be left out (with only a log line). Now the export follows the XMP specification for JPEG: the main packet keeps the creating tool, the AI marking (Digital Source Type), rating, label, rights and creator, then as much as fits of the other IPTC fields, title, keywords and caption, and the rest goes to extension segments that Lightroom, Bridge and ExifTool read back as one XMP (checked with ExifTool, covered by tests). Exports whose XMP fits one segment are unchanged.
- **A never-edited RAW no longer changes when you open it and save without a change**: the export and the Library thumbnail of a RAW never edited developed its default luminance noise reduction with the first response, while Develop showed it, and every save wrote it, with the current one, which also takes out isolated specks. So opening such a photo and moving a slider back and forth changed its export and thumbnail (5 of 10 test cameras, among them Sony A7 III, iPhone 12 Pro, DJI and Pentax K-1; up to 11 % of the pixels). Never-edited RAWs now export and show in the Library as Develop shows them, and a photo reset in the Library opens in Develop with the same noise reduction as a never-edited one; their thumbnails are made again once. Edited photos and JPEG/TIFF files render exactly as before.
- **Album and preset changes made in quick succession or right before quitting are all kept.** Adding photos to an album, renaming or deleting albums, and saving, importing, renaming or reordering presets now go through one place that writes the album list and the preset list one change at a time, each change applied to the list the one before it left. (Renaming or trashing photos updates their albums under the same album-list lock.) Two quick changes, or one from a menu and one from the Albums or Presets tool, used to be able to read the same file and write over one another, so one of them was lost; now both are kept. Quitting, closing the window and *Save & Relaunch* wait for the changes and for a running preset import (up to 10 seconds) and list one that could not be written or is still being imported in the quit prompt, as for preferences, instead of losing it silently. A preset list whose file cannot be read is never written over; changes to it are listed there too, while an unchanged list does not block relaunch. Album edits also keep photo renames and removals that finish while an edit is being sent; adding photos sends only the new paths, and adding to a deleted album shows an error. A later successful preset save clears an older failed preset save (not a failed import); failed album edits stay reported until you quit, relaunch or switch libraries. Adding to a deleted album does not leave a save failure at quit. A change waiting more than 30 seconds for earlier library work returns an error instead of hanging. The files stay where they are and keep their format.
- **A preference changed right before quitting is kept**: quitting (Quit, closing the window, ⌘Q, macOS log out) and *Save & Relaunch* now wait for the preferences still being saved, up to 10 seconds. One that cannot be written is listed in the quit prompt like unsaved edits, and *Save & Relaunch* then stays open with a message instead of restarting without it.
- **Ratings, keywords and edits kept in sidecars Rawtonic did not write itself**: a `.rrdata` sidecar without an `adjustments` field, or with one value of another type (a keyword or EXIF value that is a number, written by hand or by another program), was read as empty, so the next rating, colour label, keyword or Develop change wrote it back with rating 0, no keywords and, with a mistyped field, no edit. Each field is now read on its own, so such a write keeps what the others hold. Sidecars Rawtonic writes read exactly as before.
- **Renames on cards and drives without an exclusive rename never replace a file that is already there.** On volumes where the system cannot rename without replacing (exFAT and FAT on macOS, FAT on Linux, some network mounts), a rename or move checked that the new name was free and then renamed, so a file another program created under that name in between would have been replaced. Renames there now claim the new name first (with a hard link where the volume has one, else an empty placeholder file Rawtonic creates exclusively) and are refused if it is taken. The one window left is another program itself replacing that just-created empty placeholder in the instant before Rawtonic renames over it. Renames on exFAT cards keep working.
- **A crash in native code on Windows is no longer silent, and a crash while the graphics processor starts no longer switches the backend behind your back.** When Rawtonic closed on Windows because of an access violation, a stack overflow or an abort (often in a graphics driver), the next start knew nothing about it; it now offers the usual crash report ("Rawtonic quit unexpectedly last time"), with the exception code, the module and offset Windows recorded and the step of the start it happened in, reviewed before anything is sent. A crash while the GPU started used to set the processing backend to OpenGL for good, without a word; now the next start leaves only the backend that crashed out, says which one crashed and which one it uses instead, and the start after that tries your choice again once. If it crashes again the fallback stays, and Preferences → Processing shows it with *Use automatic again*; a fallback backend that crashes too is left out as well, with a new notice. Preferences → Processing also shows the backend in use. If an earlier version had switched you to OpenGL, the first start says so once. Panics before the log exists are recorded too, and the start's GPU decisions now reach the log.
- **Windows: a start that cannot go on says why instead of closing.** Without Microsoft's WebView2 runtime (the portable zip does not include it), Rawtonic now shows a message naming the missing runtime, and only if you choose *Yes* opens Microsoft's page for it in your browser; before, Tauri showed a bare "Error" box and the next start offered a crash report for it. An error that stops the start (before the window is ready) is shown in a message box with its short reason, and is now also recorded when it happens before the settings are read, so the next start offers the crash report. These boxes are in English: the translations are not loaded yet at that point.
- **A Windows crash is still reported when Windows logs it after you have restarted Rawtonic.** The crash's details come from the event Windows writes after the crash; if Rawtonic was started again before that event existed, the details were lost. Such a crash is now looked up again at the next few starts.
- **A virtual copy opens in Develop as bright as its photo**: a virtual copy of a RAW photo opened in Develop (and in the browser views) without its camera's base look (baseline exposure, the default tone curve and colour, a library camera profile), so it looked darker than the photo it was made from although every slider was the same; on Fujifilm RAWs, whose files can be recorded up to about 4 EV darker than metered, by several stops. Copies now develop exactly like their photo. Thumbnails and exports of copies were already right and are unchanged.
- **Reset photos in a project or catalog develop with the current processing, like never-edited ones**: after *Reset Adjustments* in the Library, a photo in a project or catalog came back with its library colour label written into its recipe, so the export, the Develop view and the thumbnail treated it as an old edit and rendered it with the first version of the develop pipeline, without the crop of an in-camera aspect ratio. It now gets the current processing defaults, as a never-edited photo does, also when it was reset before this update. As before, a Reset keeps the frame the photo was edited in; a photo never edited before its Reset matches a never-edited one exactly (a photo reset twice before this update keeps the oldest frame the second Reset stored). Photos in plain folders and all edited photos are unchanged.
- **The edited badge stays off after Reset in Develop**: a photo reset in Develop was saved with the initial settings of a new edit, which the Library read as an edit once the app restarted, so the badge came back (and a duplicate-photo suggestion counted it as edited). A recipe that holds only the new-edit defaults now counts as unedited, whichever processing versions it is stamped with; changing any setting (or switching the automatic CA correction off) still counts as an edit. A Reset from the Library was already fine (checked).
- **A session or catalog whose database is missing stays that library**: a folder with a `session.json` or `catalog.json` but no database (a half-created or damaged library) was treated as a plain folder by the editor. Its photos then lost their library camera profiles, and their previews went to the app cache. Now it counts as the library it is: its photos keep their camera profiles (`profiles/`) and previews (`.cache/`), and opening it still refuses to put an empty database in place of the missing one.
- **Preferences from a newer version survive, and one unreadable setting no longer resets all**: settings this version does not know are kept when it saves, and a setting it cannot read is reset on its own, with the other preferences intact (the original file is still kept aside).
- **Reload on a photo that could not be saved works from any photo**: after the file of an open photo was replaced on disk, the *Reload* button of the "not saved" notice did nothing once you had switched to another photo; it now opens the replaced photo again with your kept edit.
- **AI Denoise no longer reports a failure when you switch photos while it runs**: choosing another photo while Develop was developing the first one with AI Denoise now just cancels that work, without an error message and without the first photo's AI Denoise being marked as failed and skipped. Switching away and back runs it as usual.
- **⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z work right after you choose a value in a drop-down (Develop)**: after changing the Base curve (or any other drop-down or checkbox), the focused control swallowed the shortcut, so Undo worked only from the Edit menu or after clicking elsewhere. Drop-downs and checkboxes now pass ⌘/Ctrl shortcuts on; text fields keep their own undo.
- **Cancelling thumbnail generation now stops it**: the cancel button used to leave the queued thumbnails running; the queue is now dropped at the next photo, and a later generation starts normally.
- **Canon EOS C50 RAW stills open** (camera data from rawler 0.8.0). Not yet tested on a C50 file: if one opens wrongly, please send a problem report.
- **Canon RF 200-800mm with the RF1.4x or RF2x extender named with its real range**: exports' lens specification now says 280-1120 mm f/9-13 or 400-1600 mm f/13-18 instead of the bare lens's 200-800 mm f/6.3-9 (lens data from rawler 0.8.0).
- Preferences: a hidden, unused RAW highlight compression preference is no longer stored; it never changed a photo.

The tenth alpha. An activity centre in the top bar keeps every running task, result and failure in one place, and one Undo now takes back a batch edit on every photo. The ninth alpha. Object removal fills large holes with a new, larger AI model on macOS and Linux, and inpainting now always runs on your computer. ### Highlights

- **Remove fills large holes with a larger AI model.** Large removals (holes about 205 px thick or more) are now filled by a distilled diffusion inpainting model that sees the photo around the hole, on macOS through Core ML on the graphics card and on Linux on a GPU (CUDA when installed, else WebGPU); small holes keep the smaller, faster model. It is downloaded once (about 620 MB, with one progress bar from 0 to 100 % and Cancel) when you first remove a large object; without it, offline or when it does not run on this computer, the smaller model fills as before and a notice says so and why. Faces can lead (skin tones; the face models are downloaded only when you ask). Colour labels on grid and filmstrip tiles are larger and show their name, so colour is not the only cue. Match Light and selection-aware merge, lens and negative commands are in the command palette.

## 0.1.0-alpha.8 — 2026-10-27

The eighth alpha. Film grain is new: built like the grain of photographic film, with four presets measured on film scans, and the Develop view now shows it as the export will look. The lens correction warp runs on the graphics card, so the Develop view and the export develop exactly the same pixels. Tethering fetches the photos a Fujifilm camera kept while Rawtonic was not connected, styles replace each other instead of piling up, AI tools need about 3 GB less memory, and object removal no longer paints new objects into large holes. The update notice now downloads the right installer, and after an update Rawtonic shows what changed.

### Highlights

- **Film grain.** Grain in new edits is now built like the grain of photographic film: grains of random size in each dye layer of a negative, strongest in the midtones and printed back, with a fine, irregular texture at every Size and Roughness instead of smooth blobs. **New grain looks different from before**; photos already edited with grain keep their grain exactly (old recipes export byte-identically). A new **Colour** slider replaces the structure choice for film grain: 0 (the default) is neutral grain like black-and-white film, higher values give each colour layer its own grain like colour negative film. Amount 50 is as strong as Lightroom's Amount 50 on a grey chart. On a laptop (M5 Pro) neutral grain costs about 3.6 ms per 4 MP preview frame, Colour about 9 ms.
- **Grain looks in Develop as it will in the export.** While a photo has grain, Develop renders its preview at the screen's own resolution and shows it pixel for pixel, whatever the High-DPI setting. At Fit the grain now looks like an export at the displayed size (95 % of its strength on a grey chart; it was 58 %, and coarser); at 100 % the view shows the export's grain pixel for pixel. A smaller export (for example a 2048 px JPEG) shows the full-size grain scaled down.
- **Grain presets measured on film.** The grain tool's built-in presets are new: *B&W 400 classic*, *B&W 100 fine*, *Colour negative 400* and *Colour negative 100 subtle*, each fitted to our own measurements of openly licensed film scans (grain strength, texture spectrum and, for the colour ones, how much the colour layers' grain goes together) for the photo enlarged from a 35 mm frame. The earlier Fine, Medium, Coarse, Heavy and Speckled presets are gone; photos they were applied to keep their grain.
- **Grain from Lightroom and Capture One** imports as film grain: Lightroom's Amount, Size and Roughness carry over 1:1; Capture One's grain types and Granularity are mapped onto Size and Roughness and Impact onto Amount by measurement (texture spectrum and strength on a grey chart).
- **One lens warp for the view and the export.** For new edits and never-edited photos the lens and geometry correction (lens profiles, the camera's own correction data, Transform, manual distortion, lens vignetting) is computed on the graphics card, the same way for the Develop view, 1:1 zoom, masks, thumbnails and exports, so what you see is the export's geometry to the pixel. Transform and Upright corrections on wide frames no longer drift by up to 1.3 px across the frame. Headless exports of 27 RAWs took 5 % less time on a Mac mini (M4 Pro).
- **Faster automatic chromatic aberration estimate.** The automatic lateral CA correction measures the photo about 60 % faster with exactly the same result; a headless export of 58 RAWs took 8 % less time (Linux PC, Ryzen 5 3600X).
- **Tethering fetches the photos waiting on the camera.** A camera that keeps tethered photos until the computer takes them (Fujifilm in USB tether mode) may hold photos taken while Rawtonic was not connected; on such a camera that is their only copy. They are now downloaded and added to the library when the camera is connected or reconnected, as Capture One does when it starts, while you can keep shooting. Each is freed on the camera only once it is safely in the library, and only with "Free photos on the camera once they are in the library" on; one that cannot be downloaded stays on the camera and is reported. Photos already in the library are recognised and not added twice. Tested on a Fujifilm GFX 50R: three waiting photos were listed at connect, downloaded, added to the library and only then freed on the camera, while new shots kept arriving. Note that the GFX 50R's queue does not survive switching the camera off or pulling the cable: photos still waiting then are lost on the camera's side, so connect before switching off.
- **Styles replace each other.** Applying a style while another one is applied takes the previous one back first; clicking the applied style again takes it back; the applied style is marked in the list and named by its Amount slider. Settings you changed yourself after applying a style keep your value when the style is replaced or removed (Capture One loses them). This also works on several selected photos at once and survives restarts; each click is one undo step.
- **AI tools use less memory.** AI models now free their working memory after each run, and on macOS freed large memory blocks go back to the system. In a session with Relight, Subject and Sky masks and full-size exports on three photos (24 GB MacBook), Develop needed 6.8 GB instead of 9.9 GB and the peak fell from 12.7 to 9.7 GB; the Library afterwards 2.7 instead of 4.9 GB. AI runs on the processor (not the Core ML ones) take 4–10 % longer for it.
- **Object removal no longer invents objects.** Remove with Detect objects could fill a large hole with a new, smeared object (a yellow blot where a kiwi was taken out of a fruit bowl): the AI fill model saw too little around the hole. It now sees 2.5 times the hole's size. On 17 objects in 13 RAWs the fill's error fell (LPIPS 0.075 → 0.071, colour error ΔE 13.2 → 11.6); on 10 held-out objects one invented object is gone, one fill is a little better and one worse, the rest alike. See Known gaps.
- **The update notice downloads the right file.** "Download" now opens the installer for your computer in the browser (the Apple Silicon or Intel DMG on a Mac, the AppImage on Linux) instead of the release page with all its files; when a release has no file for your computer, it opens the release page. "What's new" shows the release's notes in the app. Both come from the same once-a-day check as before; Rawtonic still downloads nothing itself.
- **What's new after an update.** The first start of a new version shows what changed in it, from the notes that come with the app (no network access); not after a first install. "Don't show after updates" or Preferences › General turns it off.
- **Notifications look like the rest of Rawtonic** and no longer cover the update notice: they stack above it in the lower right corner, use the app's type, colours and buttons, fade instead of sliding in, close only with their close button (a click elsewhere on one used to close it, also on one with a button to act on) and are announced politely to screen readers.
- **Fixes:** exports sized by Width, Height, Long or Short Edge (Fast (Web) among them) now have exactly the requested size (a 6000 × 3376 photo at 2048 px wide came out 2047 px wide); wide-gamut exports (Display P3, Adobe RGB, ProPhoto) no longer declare sRGB in their EXIF colour space tag, which their ICC profile contradicted; double-clicking a Relight slider resets it to the value a new light starts with (it reset to unrelated values); copying only an untouched grain Colour now copies it; the Styles list says so when you have no styles of your own yet; the mask overlay button shows a dot and names the mode when the mask display is not *Always*, and M / ⌥M (Alt+M) say which display they switched to.

### Details

- **GPU lens warp** (recipe `renderVersion` 6 and 7): version 6 computes the lens and geometry warp in 32-bit floating point with each output pixel placed from its own position instead of along a running sum per row; version 7 runs that arithmetic on the graphics card for every use of the warp (preview bases, the full-resolution frame, 1:1 regions, mask sources, the edge-fringe plan, thumbnails, merges and exports), so the display's full-resolution frame and the export's input are bit-identical (GPU tests on 6 recipes covering lens models, lateral CA, vignetting, perspective with manual distortion and a crop). Positions on 351 photos with camera or lensfun lens data stay within 0.0024 px of exact (the previous 64-bit warp: 0.0020 px). The processor warp remains for frames larger than the graphics card can hold; a failing graphics card warp fails the render instead of returning different pixels. Headless exports on a Mac mini M4 Pro: 27 RAWs × 2 recipes 20.9–21.5 → 19.7–20.1 s.
- **Automatic lateral CA estimate:** the edge check reads green first (99 % of the patches fail it), red and blue are averaged only for patches that pass, and each window's values are read once for all candidate models. On 187 RAWs the estimates of versions 1, 2 and 3 are bit-identical to before, and 299 of 299 16-bit exports are byte-identical; criterion benchmark 109 → 43.6 ms per estimate.
- **AI memory:** ONNX Runtime sessions run without their CPU memory arena (all systems), and the macOS app starts with `MallocLargeCache=0` (Info.plist), so freed large blocks leave the footprint. Exports with freshly computed Relight, Subject and Sky masks and an AI Denoise export are byte-identical to before. Not measured on Linux or before macOS 27.
- **Object removal:** the fill model's pipeline crops tightly around the hole before scaling it to 512 px, so the 1.5 × context Rawtonic passed never reached it; four 2 × 2 px marks at the corners of a box 2.5 × the hole now widen its view. Only the hole is taken from the model's result; kept pixels and the AI marking of the patch (IPTC Digital Source Type) are unchanged. The factor was chosen on 17 objects (2 ×, 2.5 ×, 3 × compared) and frozen before the held-out check.
- **EXIF colour space:** sRGB exports keep ColorSpace 1; Display P3, Adobe RGB and ProPhoto exports write 65535 (Uncalibrated), as EXIF prescribes for other spaces, and a retained Interop index becomes R98 (sRGB), R03 (Adobe RGB) or is removed. Checked by reading back 84 format, depth and source combinations with ExifTool.
- **Styles:** the applied style is stored in the recipe (`appliedStyle`, see `docs/RECIPE_SCHEMA.md`) with every setting it set and the value before; the renderer ignores it, so exports do not change.
- **Tethering diagnostics** for the Fujifilm live view work: `RAWTONIC_TETHER_TRACE=FILE` logs every camera request, `rawtonic-tether liveview` measures live view around shots; without them the helper behaves as before.

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **Batch export** is about 2× slower: 20 RAWs to a web JPEG and a 16-bit TIFF (38 files) take about 17 s, in Lightroom 8 s and in Capture One 9 s on the same Mac; while you keep editing during a batch, Capture One exports 3–4× as many files in the same time. The GPU render is the slowest stage.
- **Import** of 200 RAW+JPEG pairs from an exFAT card with backup takes 27 s (Lightroom 11.5 s, Capture One 23.7 s), and the import dialog has no metadata preset for creator and copyright yet (set them after the import).
- **Culling**: the next photo after a rating key shows after 0.51 s (median; Capture One 0.10 s, Lightroom 0.35 s); there is no auto-advance after rating yet.
- **AI masks**: the first Subject mask after a fresh install takes 13.7 s (the model loads; Lightroom 7.9 s), later ones are fast. AI masks pasted onto other photos or imported from a Lightroom catalog are computed when each photo is opened in Develop: a photo with pasted AI selections cannot be exported before that, and imported Lightroom AI masks have no effect in its exports until then.
- **Highlights −100 in dark scenes** (lamps, candles, lit windows at night): Camera Raw darkens broad bright areas 1.3–3× further on 6 of 7 dark test frames; Rawtonic reaches at least Capture One's depth on every frame.
- **Moving files**: moves to network drives (SMB and other network shares) are refused; whole folders cannot be moved to exFAT drives on macOS (their photos can); a session's photos cannot be moved out of their session (move the whole session folder, or use a catalog).
- **Pen pressure** is built into the brush, but the macOS and Linux webviews report a tablet pen as a mouse, so a pen paints at constant pressure for now.
- **Contribution channels are new**: problem reports, crash reports, camera test results and usage statistics have had little use outside testing. The removal of paths, names, serial numbers and positions from report texts is best effort — check the exact text the review dialog shows before you send.
- **AI Denoise** is about 1.5× slower than Camera Raw's Denoise (about 10 s vs 6.5 s for 24 MP on the same Mac) and leaves a higher lightness error at the same noise level (2.21 vs 1.69); on Linux it takes about 35 s per 24 MP photo on the GPU.
- **Tethering** on the GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 2.4 s after the shot, in Capture One 2.1 s (on the a7 III, a7 IV and X-H2S Rawtonic takes 1.1–1.5 s, Capture One 1.2–1.6 s). Tethering as a whole is experimental; Fujifilm live view does not work yet and Canon cameras are not offered.
- **Auto straighten**: on 51 test photos its median error is lower than Lightroom's Auto (0.06° vs 0.18°), but its 90th percentile is slightly behind (0.46° vs 0.42°). Straightening several selected photos at once is not available yet.
- **Layers**: at most 32 visible layers take effect; adding or showing a 33rd is refused with a message.
- **Colour noise reduction** still leaves more colour specks than the better editor in each ISO band from 1600 to 6400 and more than Capture One at Color 100, and fine colour detail at ISO 3200 and above is below the best of them.
- **Chromatic aberration and fringes** remain above the best reference on a few cameras (e.g. Lumix GX850, TZ202, Canon EOS M50 II); the automatic axial CA correction adds 5–18 % to export time on the photos measured.
- **Sharpening**: Masking sharpens low-contrast photos less than Camera Raw; JPEGs at Amount 120–150 get 12–19 % less fine detail than in Camera Raw; Camera Raw's strongest settings (150) are out of reach on some photos.
- **Clarity**: halos 6–15 px beside narrow bright edges remain above the better editor's on 6 of 10 comparable photos.
- Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don't open yet; imports from Lightroom and Capture One don't carry every adjustment over.
- **Linux**: tested on an NVIDIA graphics card under KDE Wayland, on an AMD laptop, and with exports on Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora; other graphics cards and desktops may show problems. The very first Develop frame after an install or driver update takes about 3 s on NVIDIA.

## 0.1.0-alpha.3 — 2026-10-20

The third alpha: about a hundred changes since alpha.2. Tethered shooting arrives (experimental), local adjustments get finer masks and a faster brush, AI Denoise gets a new model, and colour noise reduction, fringe and chromatic aberration removal, highlight recovery, Auto white balance and sharpening were reworked and measured against the leading RAW editors.

### Highlights

- **Tethered shooting (experimental).** Connect a camera on USB, set its exposure, fire it from the app (button or ⌘K / Ctrl+K), focus, and see its live view; every shot lands in the session, verified and named by your template, with your next-capture adjustments, rating and keywords. A camera fault cannot crash the app (the camera library runs in its own helper process), and nothing on a memory card is deleted. Tested on Sony a7 III and a7 IV and Fujifilm X-H2S and GFX 50R on macOS. Other Sony and Fujifilm bodies may work but are untested; **Canon cameras are not offered yet**; Fujifilm live view does not work yet; on Linux the tether tool is included but has not been tried with a real camera.
- **Local adjustments.** 16-bit layer masks (smooth gradients without banding steps), a **Refine edge** slider for hair, fur and foliage, a better Auto Mask for brush strokes, and **Solo** (H or Option/Alt-click an eye) to see one layer or mask alone. Brush strokes follow the pointer more closely and stay quick on layers with many strokes; photos with 20–32 layers stay fluid while dragging sliders, and exports with many layers need about half the memory.
- **AI Denoise** uses a new, faster model for new edits (downloaded once, on first use; photos denoised before keep their model): about 10 s instead of 27–31 s for a 24 MP photo on our test Mac, and its amount now has the effect of the same number in Camera Raw's Denoise (50 = its default).
- **Auto straighten**: one click (crop tool *Auto*, Shift+R or the photo's context menu) levels the horizon or the verticals and crops so no empty corners show. It gives no answer rather than a wrong one when a photo has no clear lines.
- **Auto white balance** rebuilt: neutral in daylight and cool light, while sunsets, golden hour and lamplight keep their mood.
- **Cleaner colour**: colour noise reduction keeps colour edges and fine colour texture and removes lone coloured specks; Defringe removes only colour the edge does not explain; Remove chromatic aberration is on by default for RAWs whose camera or lens data has no correction of its own, and Panasonic cameras' own correction data are used.
- **Tones**: recovered highlights without pink, magenta or green casts; Shadows and Highlights act more like the leading editors; sharpening keeps halos in check at high amounts.
- **Library**: redo for undone renames, moves and trash (⇧⌘Z / Ctrl+Y), emptying the session Trash, a hot folder that resumes after a restart and can watch subfolders, naming tokens from each photo's EXIF, and XMP sidecars from the card come along at import.
- **Preset import** from Lightroom and Capture One lists what it could not translate, and translates Capture One's Color Editor into Rawtonic's colour editor.
- Updated Lensfun lens database (2026-10-08).
- **Linux**: smoother slider drags on NVIDIA under Wayland, and the first photo after an update or driver change shows in about 3 s instead of 12 s. The AppImage carries update information: **AppImageUpdate** or **Gear Lever** can update it when you ask them to (Rawtonic itself never checks for updates).
- Multi view's *Edit all selected* updates every photo live again; many smaller fixes (an export the graphics card fails (e.g. out of memory) is reported as failed instead of being written as a black file, exported files are readable by other users, virtual copies export with their own edits, edits are saved before files are moved, and more).

### Changes to existing edits

New behaviour is tied to recipe version keys, so photos edited with alpha.2 render as before, with these exceptions:

- **Black last row and column fixed** (no version key, on purpose: the black edge was never intended). Lens vignetting without distortion correction, a chromatic aberration correction that found nothing, or rejected guide lines left the photo's last pixel row and column black, in exports too. They now keep their pixels; pixels near the right and bottom edge can differ very slightly where local adjustments read that line.
- **Highlight recovery**: photos with clipped highlights change in those areas (no pink, magenta or green casts); photos without clipped highlights are unchanged.
- **Text watermarks** may differ by at most 2 of 255 levels in a few pixels (new font reader).

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **AI Denoise** is about 1.5× slower than Camera Raw's Denoise (about 10 s vs 6.5 s for 24 MP on the same Mac) and leaves a higher lightness error at the same noise level (2.21 vs 1.69); on Linux it takes about 35 s per 24 MP photo on the GPU.
- **Tethering** on the GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 2.4 s after the shot, in Capture One 2.1 s (on the a7 III, a7 IV and X-H2S Rawtonic takes 1.1–1.5 s, Capture One 1.2–1.6 s). Tethering as a whole is experimental.
- **Auto straighten**: on 51 test photos its median error is lower than Lightroom's Auto (0.06° vs 0.18°), but its 90th percentile is slightly behind (0.46° vs 0.42°). Straightening several selected photos at once is not available yet.
- **Layers**: at most 32 visible layers take effect; further visible layers are ignored.
- **Colour noise reduction** still leaves more lone colour specks than the leading editors at the default amount, and fine colour detail at ISO 3200 and above is below the best of them.
- **Chromatic aberration and fringes** remain above the best reference on a few cameras (e.g. Lumix GX850, TZ202, Canon EOS M50 II).
- **Sharpening**: low-contrast detail at high Amount/Detail settings is below the leading editors.
- Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don't open yet; imports from Lightroom and Capture One don't carry every adjustment over.
- **Linux**: tested on an NVIDIA graphics card under KDE Wayland and with exports on Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora; other graphics cards and desktops may show problems. The very first Develop frame after an install or driver update takes about 3 s on NVIDIA.

### Details

- Fix: an export or thumbnail the graphics card failed to render — for example when it ran out of memory because another program was using it — was written as an all-black file and reported as done. Such a render now fails, and the export queue lists the photograph with the reason. Renders without a GPU error are unchanged.
- Fix: a geometry warp that moves no pixel (lens vignetting without a lens profile's distortion, Remove chromatic aberration that found nothing, rejected guide lines) left the last row and column of the photo black, in exports too; they now keep their pixels. This changes such exports, saved edits included, without a recipe version key (user decision 2026-10-19: the black edge was never intended): also default exports of never-edited RAWs where automatic chromatic aberration removal finds nothing to correct, and the pixels near the right and bottom edge differ slightly where local filters read the former black line. The default exports of the three benchmark RAWs (Canon CR3, Nikon NEF, Fujifilm RAF) are byte-identical.
- **Tethered shooting.** The Tether tool in the session bar connects a camera on USB, reads and sets its exposure settings (ISO, shutter, aperture, exposure compensation, white balance, drive, focus and metering modes, image quality, where photos are saved; settings fixed on the body are shown as such), fires it (button, ⌘K/Ctrl+K, also with the tool closed), focuses and shows its live view. Every photo — fired here or on the camera — is copied into the session's Capture folder with the filename template and verified, gets the next-capture adjustments, rating and keywords in the same transaction as the hot folder's, is selected in the Library and opened when Develop is showing, and the captures form a tether import batch (Previous Import; database schema 9). A camera unplugged and plugged in again is connected again by itself.
