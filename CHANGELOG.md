# Changelog

## Unreleased

## 0.1.0-alpha.6 — 2026-10-25

The sixth alpha: the changes since alpha.5, many of them from the first use tests of alpha.5. A native menu bar on the Mac and a command palette, faster imports and culling, undo for ratings and flags, built-in styles and presets per tool, a range wheel and skin tones in the Colour Editor, a photograph-only full screen, Highlights that go further in night scenes, and a Storage page in Preferences.

### Highlights

- **Menus and commands.** On macOS a full menu bar (File, Edit, Photo, View, Window, Help) with Open Recent and Export with Recipe; its key equivalents follow the shortcuts you set in Preferences. A command palette (⇧⌘P / Ctrl+Shift+P, rebindable) finds and runs any command; on Linux the same menus sit in the top bar's … menu. The view keys G/E/D and the flag keys P/U/⇧X can now be rebound.
- **Import.** Importing 200 RAW+JPEG pairs from an exFAT card with a backup copy now takes about 10 s until the files are in place instead of 27 s (Lightroom 11.5 s, Capture One 23.7 s on the same laptop): the library copy and the backup are made from one read of each source, then each copy and the source read again are compared byte for byte before anything is published. The import dialog has metadata presets (creator, copyright with the capture year, description, usage terms, credit, keywords; contact details only when you tick them) that fill empty fields, or overwrite them when you say so.
- **Culling.** The next photo shows at once: Develop, the Library loupe and the full screen develop the neighbours ahead in the direction you step, show the camera's embedded JPEG (colour-managed, framed like the developed photo) until the developed frame is in, and Auto-advance after a rating or flag key can be turned on in Preferences › General (off by default). Ratings, flags and colour labels can be undone (⌘Z / Ctrl+Z), also for many photos at once.
- **Full screen and loupe.** F shows only the photograph, full screen, from the Library and Develop (the window's own full screen moved to F11 / ⌃⌘F); I shows the photograph's facts over it. The Library has a loupe (E, Space or a double click; G back to the grid). Ratings and flags there apply to the photo shown.
- **Presets.** Grain, Vignette, Color grading, Black & white, Sharpening and Noise reduction have a preset button in their header: hover to preview, click to apply, save your own. 39 built-in presets and styles with descriptive names (Preferences › General hides them). Presets with AI masks now find the subject, sky or person again on each photo they are applied to, instead of reusing the first photo's mask.
- **Colour Editor.** A range wheel to see and drag every colour range, and a Skin tone mode with Uniformity for hue, saturation and lightness. On four public portraits it evens skin out at least as much as Capture One's Skin Tone at uniformity 50 and 100, while changing less outside the skin. Capture One styles with skin tone entries import into it; the conversion is approximate and the import report says so.
- **Masks.** Every mask kind is one click away in the row above the layer list. An Object mask takes several clicks or boxes: Shift adds another object, Alt (⌥) takes a part away, ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z takes the last one back.
- **Highlights in night scenes.** New edits lower Highlights further in dark frames (lamps, candles, lit windows): on 7 of 7 dark test frames −50 and −100 now reach at least as far as Camera Raw's; brighter frames render as before (see Changes to existing edits).
- **Before/After** can split top and bottom as well as left and right (the Compare button's menu, or ⇧Y to cycle); each session remembers its choice.
- **Storage** in Preferences shows how much space caches, downloaded AI models, logs and the open library's cache take, with limits and buttons to clear each. Clearing never touches photos, sidecars, catalogs, saved masks, Output or Trash; removed thumbnails are made again when needed.
- **Faster AI masks after a fresh install.** On a Mac the first Subject mask took 13.7 s; Rawtonic now prepares the Subject and Sky models in the background while you are in Develop, so a click 25 s after opening the first photo took 1.5–3.3 s (four runs). A click within about 15 s of opening Develop still waits for part of it.
- **Library header.** Search, filters and sorting share the session bar in wide windows. Grain moved to the Detail tab after Noise reduction; Vignette, Light effects and LUT are separate tools in Adjust.
- **First start** asks on which side the Develop tools should be (also in Preferences).
- **Fixes:** unticking a People part (e.g. Lips) left its mask on the photo; the tone curve followed a drag only about 4 times a second (now every frame); targeted adjustment missed the first press on the photo; deleted photos stayed in Previous Import and albums; the session's Trash folder had no trash icon.

### Changes to existing edits

- **Highlights:** new edits get `toneVersion` 3. Photos edited before keep their version and render as before, and so do recipes that lower Highlights or raise Shadows above +50; the editor moves other recipes to 3 when it opens them, where nothing renders differently. Never-edited RAWs render as before (Highlights at 0 is unchanged).
- Exports of existing recipes are otherwise unchanged: the faster export resize and colour-noise steps and the faster lens correction produce identical files (checked byte for byte on 27 public RAWs, 54 files).
- **Photos moved to the system Trash** leave albums and Previous Import at once (catalog schema 16; libraries are upgraded when opened). Photos trashed with an earlier alpha cannot be told apart from photos whose drive is disconnected, so they stay listed as offline.
- **Keys:** F is the photograph-only full screen (window full screen F11 / ⌃⌘F); D in Develop no longer has a second meaning for the panels.

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **Batch export** is still about 2× slower than Lightroom and Capture One (alpha.4: 20 RAWs to a web JPEG and a 16-bit TIFF in about 17 s, Lightroom 8 s, Capture One 9 s). This round made it about 4–11 % faster on a Mac mini (27 RAWs, two recipes: 41 → 37–38 s in the app); the rest is develop work on the CPU. Not re-measured against Lightroom and Capture One yet.
- **Culling** has not been re-measured with real key presses against Lightroom and Capture One yet. Scripted on a Mac mini: the next photo's first pixels after about 90 ms, its full development after about 0.2 s (was 0.62 s) at a steady pace; when you step faster than about every 0.6 s, the camera JPEG shows within about 20 ms and the development follows after about 0.43 s.
- **Import with a metadata preset** takes about 1–2 s longer for 200 photos than without.
- **Highlights −100 in dark scenes** now goes further than Camera Raw's on some frames (candles, snow) and looks darker and flatter there; about −50 to −60 matches Camera Raw's −100. On an overexposed bulb Camera Raw still lowers the bright area much further.
- **Skin tone** import from Capture One styles is approximate, and the Colour Editor as a whole is not yet at the better editor's level.
- **Before/After side by side** (two panes) is not available yet; the split shows one photo.
- **Storage**: removing AI models takes effect at the next start; most languages other than English and German show the Storage page in English.
- **Menus**: single-key shortcuts are not shown in the macOS menu items (only those with ⌘).
- The known gaps of alpha.4 not named here remain (see below).

### Details

- Import metadata presets add creator, copyright (with a capture-year token), description, usage terms, credit and keywords. They fill empty fields unless explicitly set to overwrite; contact details are opt-in. Copy writes XMP after verified publication, while Add keeps metadata in the catalog pending the usual later XMP sync. Independent RAW/JPEG photographs sharing a stem retain the existing XMP ownership limit and report a warning.
  Selecting a preset also adopts readable embedded descriptions, keywords and IPTC fields so the new sidecar preserves them. Unreadable embedded XMP is ignored, as in capture-fact reading, and never blocks the photo's import.

## 0.1.0-alpha.5 — 2026-10-24

A fix release for alpha.4: its downloads were built without the address of Rawtonic's report relay, so **Report a problem**, crash reports, camera test results and usage statistics could not be sent from the app (only **Copy report** and **Open on GitHub** worked). alpha.5 sends them as alpha.4's notes describe. Nothing else changes; edits render exactly as in alpha.4.

### Details

- Optimized builds send contributions to `https://relay.rawtonic.cc` unless `RAWTONIC_RELAY_URL` names another relay at build time (empty: none); debug builds and tests have no relay unless it is set. Before, every build without the variable, including the alpha.4 packages, had none.

## 0.1.0-alpha.4 — 2026-10-24

The fourth alpha: about 130 changes since alpha.3. Red eye correction, library search with smart albums, RAW+JPEG pairs and imports that resume, moves to other drives, a copy/paste picker, named snapshots and an export queue panel arrive. Sharpening, colour noise reduction, fringes, curves and Clarity were reworked and measured against the leading RAW editors, and you can now report a problem or a crash from inside the app, without an account.

### Highlights

- **Red eye and pet eye correction.** A new mode of the spot tool: click an eye and the pupil is found and corrected (drag to size the search circle), with Pupil Size and Darken; pet eyes keep a catchlight. The correction stays on the eye through crops, turns and lens corrections. Lightroom's red eye corrections come over with catalog imports.
- **Help without an account.** *Report a problem* (Help menu on macOS, the … menu in Library and Develop) sends your description with optional system details, log, a screenshot of the window and the photo file; after a crash Rawtonic offers a crash report at the next start; the tether camera test can send its result; a RAW that does not open offers help to get its camera supported. Nothing is sent before you have seen the exact text and clicked Send, and every dialog can instead copy the report or open it on GitHub. See [Privacy](PRIVACY.md).
- **Update check and opt-in usage statistics.** Rawtonic looks for a new release at startup at most once a day (can be turned off; it never downloads or installs anything itself). Anonymous weekly usage statistics — which camera models were opened and which tools were used, without any id — are sent only if you turn them on in Preferences (off by default).
- **Library search and smart albums.** Find photos by part of any word, filter by camera, lens, focal length, aperture, ISO, shutter speed, capture year and keywords (Shift-click picks a range), and save the filter as a smart album with nested all/any/none rules and live counts.
- **Import.** A RAW and its JPEG come in as one photo; an import cut off by a quit, a crash or a card taken out finishes when the library opens again, without duplicates; CR3 files and merged DNGs get their capture details, and the rating the camera wrote is taken over. Imports and exports onto exFAT drives on macOS work (they failed with "os error 45").
- **Move photos and whole folders to another drive**, verified byte for byte before the originals go to the Trash, undoable, keeping Finder tags and other extended attributes; photos can also move to exFAT drives on macOS.
- **Copy, paste and sync** with a picker for tools and single layers, *Previous* (paste the settings of the photo open before) and Lens Correction.
- **Snapshots** have names and thumbnails, sit with History in a dock in every Develop tab, and can be compared with the current edit in Before/After (F12).
- **Export.** Photos exported with several recipes are developed and rendered once for all of them (20 RAWs to a web JPEG and a 16-bit print TIFF: about 17 s instead of 31–35 s, every file byte-identical); a queue panel shows each photo's progress and the time left, with pause and cancel per job; Develop stays fluid while a batch exports; stripped exports keep creator and copyright.
- **Curves.** A Luminance channel (contrast without extra saturation or hue shifts), curves with more than 16 points, curves in a wide working space, and targeted adjustment for the region curve; Lightroom tone curves import as Camera Raw draws them.
- **Detail.** Sharpening follows the photo's brightness and follows Camera Raw's Radius and Masking more closely, its Amount goes to 150, and JPEGs get stronger sharpening at high amounts. Colour noise reduction leaves fewer colour specks (on 55 high-ISO RAWs now fewer than Camera Raw at Color 25 and 50 and Capture One at its default), luminance noise reduction fewer single-pixel specks. Purple and green fringes from axial chromatic aberration are corrected automatically, edges between two saturated colours are cleaner, and thin dark lines no longer get coloured stripes.
- **Looks.** Clarity leaves fewer halos beside narrow bright edges; film grain looks the same on every graphics card; LUTs go to 200 % amount, import as packs from folders, and 1D and shaper .cube files are read.
- **Speed.** Multi view zooms sooner (the selected photo at 100 % 0.98 → 0.67 s, a 12-photo zoom 4.6 → 2.5 s, with a first view of each region within about 0.5–0.7 s); the first frame of a never-opened RAW on Linux comes after 0.75 s instead of 1.2 s (on a cold NVIDIA driver cache 2.9 s instead of 4.4 s); the first slider drag after opening a photo is no longer slowed by previews built in the background.
- Many fixes: Lightroom catalog imports render their gradients, brushes, colour ranges, spots and red eyes in exports of photos never opened in Develop; lens profiles from the lens database are looked up at the photo's aperture and its sensor's crop factor (Canon, Fujifilm, Olympus and Panasonic RAWs got wrong vignetting and distortion corrections); Save & Relaunch restarts the app again; Return in a new export recipe's name saves the recipe instead of starting an export.

### Changes to existing edits

New behaviour is tied to recipe version keys (`renderVersion` 2 and 3, `sharpenVersion` 3–6, `colorNoiseVersion` 8–10, `lumaNoiseVersion` 2, `fringeVersion` 3 and 4, `clarityVersion` 2, `grainVersion` 2, `maskVersion` 4 for new layers, `curves.version` 2 and 3), so photos edited with alpha.3 render as before; never-edited RAWs develop with the new defaults. Exceptions:

- **Lens profiles from the lens database** for photos without saved lens parameters (never-edited RAWs and their previews) are looked up at the photo's f-number and its body's crop factor and keep their whole lateral chromatic aberration correction: 31 of 62 public test RAWs get a different vignetting, distortion or CA correction. Saved edits keep the lens parameters they were saved with.
- **Photos imported from a Lightroom catalog and never opened** now export with their gradients, brushes, colour ranges, spots and red eye corrections (they were left out until the photo was opened in Develop; opening it changes no pixel). Lightroom AI masks still need the photo opened (see Known gaps).
- **Export metadata** (no version key): recipes that strip metadata, including Fast (Web) and your own recipes, now keep creator, copyright and usage terms. Pixels are unchanged.
- **Ratings** (no version key): photos whose metadata you have not changed take the rating the camera wrote into the file (read once when the library is opened).

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **Batch export** is about 2× slower: 20 RAWs to a web JPEG and a 16-bit TIFF (38 files) take about 17 s, in Lightroom 8 s and in Capture One 9 s on the same Mac; while you keep editing during a batch, Capture One exports 3–4× as many files in the same time. The GPU render is the slowest stage.
- **Import** of 200 RAW+JPEG pairs from an exFAT card with backup takes 27 s (Lightroom 11.5 s, Capture One 23.7 s), and the import dialog has no metadata preset for creator and copyright yet (set them after the import).
- **Culling**: the next photo after a rating key shows after 0.51 s (median; Capture One 0.10 s, Lightroom 0.35 s); there is no auto-advance after rating yet.
- **AI masks**: the first Subject mask after a fresh install takes 13.7 s (the model loads; Lightroom 7.9 s), later ones are fast. AI masks pasted onto other photos or imported from a Lightroom catalog are computed when each photo is opened in Develop: a photo with pasted AI selections cannot be exported before that, and imported Lightroom AI masks have no effect in its exports until then.
- **Highlights −100 in dark scenes** (lamps, candles, lit windows at night): Camera Raw darkens broad bright areas 1.3–3× further on 6 of 7 dark test frames; Rawtonic reaches at least Capture One's depth on every frame.
- **Moving files**: moves to network drives (SMB and other network shares) are refused; whole folders cannot be moved to exFAT drives on macOS (their photos can); a session's photos cannot be moved out of their session (move the whole session folder, or use a catalog).
- **Pen pressure** is built into the brush, but the macOS and Linux webviews report a tablet pen as a mouse, so a pen paints at constant pressure for now.
- **Contribution channels are new**: problem reports, crash reports, camera test results and usage statistics have had little use outside testing. The removal of paths, names, serial numbers and positions from report texts is best effort — check the exact text the review dialog shows before you send.
- **AI Denoise** is about 1.5× slower than Camera Raw's Denoise (about 10 s vs 6.5 s for 24 MP on the same Mac) and leaves a higher lightness error at the same noise level (2.21 vs 1.69); on Linux it takes about 35 s per 24 MP photo on the GPU.
- **Tethering** on the GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 2.4 s after the shot, in Capture One 2.1 s (on the a7 III, a7 IV and X-H2S Rawtonic takes 1.1–1.5 s, Capture One 1.2–1.6 s). Tethering as a whole is experimental; Fujifilm live view does not work yet and Canon cameras are not offered.
- **Auto straighten**: on 51 test photos its median error is lower than Lightroom's Auto (0.06° vs 0.18°), but its 90th percentile is slightly behind (0.46° vs 0.42°). Straightening several selected photos at once is not available yet.
- **Layers**: at most 32 visible layers take effect; adding or showing a 33rd is refused with a message.
- **Colour noise reduction** still leaves more colour specks than the better editor in each ISO band from 1600 to 6400 and more than Capture One at Color 100, and fine colour detail at ISO 3200 and above is below the best of them.
- **Chromatic aberration and fringes** remain above the best reference on a few cameras (e.g. Lumix GX850, TZ202, Canon EOS M50 II); the automatic axial CA correction adds 5–18 % to export time on the photos measured.
- **Sharpening**: Masking sharpens low-contrast photos less than Camera Raw; JPEGs at Amount 120–150 get 12–19 % less fine detail than in Camera Raw; Camera Raw's strongest settings (150) are out of reach on some photos.
- **Clarity**: halos 6–15 px beside narrow bright edges remain above the better editor's on 6 of 10 comparable photos.
- Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don't open yet; imports from Lightroom and Capture One don't carry every adjustment over.
- **Linux**: tested on an NVIDIA graphics card under KDE Wayland, on an AMD laptop, and with exports on Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora; other graphics cards and desktops may show problems. The very first Develop frame after an install or driver update takes about 3 s on NVIDIA.

### Details

- **Report a problem** (Help menu on macOS, the … menu in Library and Develop): your description plus, each switchable, system details (graphics card), the last 200 lines of the log (editable), the camera make, model and file type of the open photo, a screenshot of the window (macOS, off unless ticked) and the photo's file itself (off unless ticked; the dialog warns that it keeps all its metadata and may show people). **Crash report on the next start**: when Rawtonic stops on an error in its own code or a fatal signal, it keeps a crash record in its settings folder and asks once at the next start whether to send it ("Never" deletes the records and stops recording; Preferences turns it back on). Every send first shows the exact text ("Review what will be sent"), goes through Rawtonic's own relay without an account, and gives a report id to delete it; reports are deleted after 90 days. Every dialog can instead copy the report or open GitHub's public issue form with the text shown first (no screenshot or file). Paths, user and computer names, serial numbers, e-mail and IP addresses and GPS positions are removed from the text on a best-effort basis ([Privacy](PRIVACY.md), ADR 0017).
- Anonymous weekly usage statistics, opt-in (Preferences › General, off by default): while on, Rawtonic counts per ISO week which camera models were opened (names from the decoder's camera list only), which tools from a fixed list were used (yes/no), and the app version, system and graphics card vendor; each finished week is sent once at a later start, without any id. *Show what is sent* lists every pending week in plain words or as the exact text; switching off deletes what was kept.
- Moving a session's photos to a folder outside the session (Move to Folder, paste after cut, on the same drive or to another) is refused before anything moves, with a message saying so: the session keeps only photos inside its folder, so they went offline there while their files showed in the new folder as loose photos without their ratings and edits. Move the whole session folder to the drive, or add the drive's folder to a catalog, whose photos may live anywhere. Photos still move within their session, into a session, and anywhere from a catalog.
- The tether panel's **Camera test** runs a guided test with your body (settings read and changed back, autofocus, single shots and a burst with timings, a shot on the body, cable out and back in, release from the camera's transfer queue) and builds an anonymous report (model, firmware, system, app version, results; no serial number, file names or folders), which *Send result…* sends through the review dialog, or copies, or opens as a GitHub camera report. The panel shows each camera's support level from the compatibility list, also before connecting. A RAW that fails to decode, or shows only its embedded preview, offers a guide to sharing a sample on raw.pixls.us (opened in the browser; Rawtonic uploads nothing there) or a problem report with that file; formats no decoder reads are not offered camera help.
- Export recipes that strip metadata, including Fast (Web) and existing user recipes, now retain creator, copyright and usage terms. Contact details (email, phone, website and postal address) remain omitted unless explicitly enabled in Export > Metadata (CLI: `--keep-creator-contact`). GPS, camera and serial details remain stripped. Recipe files are not migrated or rewritten; export pixels are unchanged. Collages keep their previous policy: no source metadata, with AI marking when needed.
- Built-in LUT labels use descriptive names in all interface languages; saved LUT paths and colour data are unchanged. On macOS, plain Delete/Backspace opens the Library delete dialog, and choosing a crop ratio returns focus to the photograph.
- Multi view zooms sooner: the selected photo's 100 % view 0.98 → 0.67 s and a locked 100 % zoom of 12 photos 4.6 → 2.5 s on a Mac mini M4 Pro (decodes it already holds are reused while they match the current settings). A zoom right after entering shows each cell's region from the reduced copy it was developed from within about 0.5–0.7 s, until the full-resolution region replaces it (still decode-bound, about 5 s); the zoom's own full decodes are kept for the background previews instead of being decoded again.
- Export: Return in *New recipe name* saves the recipe (it started an export of the selection), and the Subfolder field no longer capitalises what you type.
- Photos imported from a Lightroom catalog render their gradients, brushes and colour ranges, their spots and their red eye corrections without being opened: the import records each photo's orientation, and exports, the CLI and thumbnails place them as the editor does (opening the photo changes no pixel). Lightroom's Red Eye and Pet Eye corrections are imported. AI masks still wait for the photo to be opened, and the import says how many photos have them.
- Photos can be moved to exFAT drives on macOS (card readers, many external SSDs), which were refused: macOS cannot rename a file there without risking replacing another, so each file is created under its final name only if that name is free, its creation recorded before a byte is written, then copied, synced and read back there. The originals go to the Trash only after every copy is verified. Such a file is visible while it is copied, so Rawtonic never deletes it again: if the move fails, is stopped or is interrupted by a crash, the copy stays, complete or not, beside the untouched original, and the message and the operation's note name it; a notice lists such copies with Show in Finder and Move to Trash (drag it out of the Trash to get it back; offered only for the copies that move made, and not when the original is gone). Remove it before moving that photo there again. Another program writing into that new file during the copy can lose what it wrote: a write into a part already copied makes the check fail (the copy stays, the original is untouched), while a write ahead of the copy is overwritten without trace and the move completes normally (ADR 0016). Whole folders cannot be moved to such a drive (their photos can).
- Stronger sharpening of JPEGs and other non-RAW photos above Amount 40 (recipe `sharpenVersion` 6, new edits): measured against Camera Raw on the same JPEG pixels, fine detail on held-out frames went from 10 / 22 / 31 % below Camera Raw's to 5 / 12 / 19 % below at Amount 80 / 120 / 150, with halos still below Camera Raw's. RAW photos and amounts up to 40 render as with version 5; 450 of 450 full-resolution exports of older recipes byte-identical.
- The Sharpening Amount slider goes to 150 for sharpening responses 2 and up (Camera Raw's range; layers keep −100…100), and Lightroom's Sharpness imports 1:1 up to 150.
- Sharpening Masking follows Camera Raw's ramp (recipe `sharpenVersion` 5, new edits): less sharpening on flat noise, more on moderate texture; the texture error against Camera Raw fell from 0.123 to 0.098 on held-out frames. Without Masking it renders as version 4; 272 of 272 full-resolution exports of older recipes byte-identical.
- **Red eye and pet eye correction**: a mode of the spot tool (Remove, Heal, Clone, Red eye). A click searches the circle around it for the pupil, a drag sizes the circle; the pupil is found by classic image processing (no AI model) and fitted as an ellipse, and slivers and red surfaces are refused. Pupil Size and Darken per eye; pet eyes keep a catchlight. Corrections are stored per photo (`adjustments.redEyes`) in the photo as decoded, so turns, flips, straightening, lens and transform corrections keep them on the eye, and preview and export share them. They are not copied with settings (like spots).
- Rawtonic checks GitHub for a new release at startup at most once per day, including pre-releases on alpha/beta builds. Turn this off in Preferences, choose whether to include pre-releases, or use **Check now**. A small notice links to the release page; Rawtonic never downloads or installs updates itself. See [Privacy](PRIVACY.md) for what the check sends.
- Copy, paste and sync offer Lens Correction again: a lens choice pasted onto the open photo applies at once, and its lens data are looked up for that photo (also an offline one, from its recorded camera facts) as their own part of the paste's undo step; an export started before the lookup finishes resolves it itself.
- Clarity leaves fewer halos beside narrow bright edges such as slats, balusters, window frames and branches against the sky (recipe `clarityVersion` 2, new edits): the detail band is measured against an edge-aware base. At Clarity +100 the dark ring 1–6 px from such edges is 19–68 % of version 1's on 6 held-out photos and lower on all 11 measured, at or below the better of Camera Raw and Capture One on 3 of the 6; texture gain stays within a few percent of version 1. The Clarity version travels with Clarity through presets, paste and *Edit all selected*. Saved edits render as before.
- LUTs: Amount goes to 200 % (above 100 % the LUT's change is extended, kept inside the colour cube); importing a folder or several files makes a pack, grouped in the LUT picker; .cube files with 1D tables, a 1D shaper before the 3D table, or other input ranges are read (a .cube the earlier reader took is read exactly as before). Files that cannot be imported are listed with the reason in your interface language. Imports run off the main thread.
- The first frames after opening a photo come sooner on integrated graphics: background previews of the neighbouring photos wait while the open photo is decoded or rendered and give way to slider and brush drags within one tile of their work. On an AMD Radeon 680M laptop (Linux), the first frame after a first start 4.06–4.70 → 3.42–3.50 s, and input to preview on the first drag after opening (median) 33–41 → 11–14 ms. Exports byte-identical.
- A batch export no longer freezes the Develop view: exports render on a GPU processor of their own, wait between tiles while a Develop frame runs, and their CPU work runs at lower priority. Measured during a batch export on a laptop: 42–58 screen updates per Exposure drag, the last 10–52 ms after release (Lightroom 40–47 and up to 310 ms, Capture One 11–18 and up to 2.1 s). The export processor is released when memory runs short. Slider drags with several photos selected in multi view commit at intervals instead of on every move. Exports byte-identical.
- Moves to another drive keep extended attributes: on macOS Finder tags and comments, resource forks and the other attributes a program may set, on Linux the user attributes (`user.*`), for photos and for moved folders and everything in them. They are copied with each file, read back from the copy and compared, and the original goes to the Trash only while its attributes still match its copy's. A drive that cannot keep them (FAT or exFAT on Linux) refuses the move before anything is copied, naming the file and its attributes; the original stays where it is (ADR 0016).
- Colour noise reduction leaves fewer colour specks at its default (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 10, new edits and never-edited RAWs): each pixel's filtered colour is now checked against its neighbours' plain filtered colours only, no longer against the side of a colour edge its own noisy colour picked, with a margin of 1.25 instead of 1.5 times the noise. At Color 25 the coarser scales keep a little colour noise, and in flat areas the filter kept single pixels' noisy colour within a range that included their own choice; those pixels no longer stand out. On 55 high-ISO RAWs, colour specks fell from 7.5 to 5.0 per million pixels at Color 25 (Capture One's default and Camera Raw 5.4), from 4.2 to 3.5 at Color 50 (Camera Raw 4.7) and from 4.1 to 3.1 at Color 100 (Camera Raw 4.6, Capture One at 100 1.4), with more specks on at most 2 of the 55 files; colour edges stayed within 0.12 % of version 9 in every ISO band, the error against clean ground-truth frames (RawNIND) fell or stayed in every cell, and fine colour detail fell by about 1 % at ISO 1600–2500 and 6400. Per ISO band still above the better editor from ISO 1600 to 6400 at every Color (below both from 12800 up), and above Capture One at Color 100 overall. Saved edits render exactly as before; at Color 0 version 10 renders as version 9.
- Folders can be moved with everything in them: **Move Folder…** in a folder's menu in Folders picks where to. On the same drive it is a rename; to another drive the Library asks first, naming the folder, its photos, files and size, the drive and its free space. The whole folder, files the library does not know included, is copied next to its new place, every file synced, read back from the drive and compared byte for byte, and then the folder appears under its name at once, never replacing anything. ## 0.1.0-alpha.3 — 2026-10-20

The third alpha: about a hundred changes since alpha.2. Tethered shooting arrives (experimental), local adjustments get finer masks and a faster brush, AI Denoise gets a new model, and colour noise reduction, fringe and chromatic aberration removal, highlight recovery, Auto white balance and sharpening were reworked and measured against the leading RAW editors.

### Highlights

- **Tethered shooting (experimental).** Connect a camera on USB, set its exposure, fire it from the app (button or ⌘K / Ctrl+K), focus, and see its live view; every shot lands in the session, verified and named by your template, with your next-capture adjustments, rating and keywords. - **Cleaner colour**: colour noise reduction keeps colour edges and fine colour texture and removes lone coloured specks; Defringe removes only colour the edge does not explain; Remove chromatic aberration is on by default for RAWs whose camera or lens data has no correction of its own, and Panasonic cameras' own correction data are used.

- **Linux**: smoother slider drags on NVIDIA under Wayland, and the first photo after an update or driver change shows in about 3 s instead of 12 s. The AppImage carries update information: **AppImageUpdate** or **Gear Lever** can update it when you ask them to (Rawtonic itself never checks for updates).
- Multi view's *Edit all selected* updates every photo live again; many smaller fixes (an export the graphics card fails (e.g. out of memory) is reported as failed instead of being written as a black file, exported files are readable by other users, virtual copies export with their own edits, edits are saved before files are moved, and more).

### Changes to existing edits

New behaviour is tied to recipe version keys, so photos edited with alpha.2 render as before, with these exceptions:

- **Black last row and column fixed** (no version key, on purpose: the black edge was never intended). Lens vignetting without distortion correction, a chromatic aberration correction that found nothing, or rejected guide lines left the photo's last pixel row and column black, in exports too. They now keep their pixels; pixels near the right and bottom edge can differ very slightly where local adjustments read that line.
- **Highlight recovery**: photos with clipped highlights change in those areas (no pink, magenta or green casts); photos without clipped highlights are unchanged.
- **Text watermarks** may differ by at most 2 of 255 levels in a few pixels (new font reader).

### Known gaps

These are measured shortfalls against the better of the leading professional RAW editors; they stay open until they are closed by measurement.

- **AI Denoise** is about 1.5× slower than Camera Raw's Denoise (about 10 s vs 6.5 s for 24 MP on the same Mac) and leaves a higher lightness error at the same noise level (2.21 vs 1.69); on Linux it takes about 35 s per 24 MP photo on the GPU.
- **Tethering** on the GFX 50R: the new photo is on screen 2.4 s after the shot, in Capture One 2.1 s (on the a7 III, a7 IV and X-H2S Rawtonic takes 1.1–1.5 s, Capture One 1.2–1.6 s). Tethering as a whole is experimental.
- **Auto straighten**: on 51 test photos its median error is lower than Lightroom's Auto (0.06° vs 0.18°), but its 90th percentile is slightly behind (0.46° vs 0.42°). Straightening several selected photos at once is not available yet.
- **Layers**: at most 32 visible layers take effect; further visible layers are ignored.
- **Colour noise reduction** still leaves more lone colour specks than the leading editors at the default amount, and fine colour detail at ISO 3200 and above is below the best of them.
- **Chromatic aberration and fringes** remain above the best reference on a few cameras (e.g. Lumix GX850, TZ202, Canon EOS M50 II).
- **Sharpening**: low-contrast detail at high Amount/Detail settings is below the leading editors.
- Nikon High Efficiency NEFs don't open yet; imports from Lightroom and Capture One don't carry every adjustment over.
- **Linux**: tested on an NVIDIA graphics card under KDE Wayland and with exports on Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora; other graphics cards and desktops may show problems. The very first Develop frame after an install or driver update takes about 3 s on NVIDIA.
