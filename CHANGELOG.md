# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Fix: after Remove chromatic aberration was switched on or the lens data changed, the crop and transform view could measure the lateral CA on its reduced copy and show a different correction from the export; it now always uses the export's full-resolution measurement. Saving an edit of an offline Panasonic photo whose preview predates the camera's CA data no longer drops that correction from the recipe (such previews are made again).
- Remove chromatic aberration may replace the camera's or a lens profile's own lateral CA data in new RAW edits where its full-resolution measurement leaves clearly fewer colour fringes (recipe `autoCaVersion` 3); otherwise the lens data render exactly as before. Switching it off keeps the camera data, switching it on replaces them, a changed CA amount keeps them at that amount. On 55 public RAW files the bright-edge fringe colour fell on 11 beyond the measure's noise (e.g. Fujifilm X100V 17.3 → 9.9, Sony NEX-7 5.0 → 2.4) and rose on none; 42 render identically. Saved edits render byte-identically.
- Panasonic cameras' own lateral CA data (RW2) are applied in Camera lens mode, as for Fujifilm and Sony; bodies with CA data but no distortion data (e.g. TZ81) now get Camera mode. Lateral CA in full-resolution exports was lower on all 12 files measured (e.g. S1 0.98/1.46 → 0.31/0.42 ‰). Recipes saved before keep their parameters (recipe `cameraCaVersion`).
- Colour noise reduction pulls lone off-colour pixels to their neighbours' side of colour edges (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 5, new edits and never-edited RAWs): on 55 high-ISO RAWs isolated colour specks fell from 126 to 76 per million pixels at Color 25 (35.7 at Color 50), with colour edges and texture unchanged. Saved edits with versions 1–4 render byte-identically.
- Remove chromatic aberration is on by default for new RAW edits whose camera or lens profile has no lateral CA data of its own (recipe `autoCaVersion` 2; for camera or profile CA data see `autoCaVersion` 3 above). The measurement is refined at full resolution — analysed on a reduced copy it overestimated the CA, by about twice on an 80 MP high-resolution frame, which got red/blue fringes instead of losing them — and a correction is applied only at the strength that measurably lowers the colour fringes at the photo's edges, else not at all. On 37 public RAW files, the share of bright edges with fringe colour fell on 10 (Nikon D800 17.4 → 6.3 %, Olympus E-M5 III high-res 12.2 → 9.3 %, Canon EOS M50 II 4.7 → 2.5 %, OM-1 II 11.0 → 9.7 %), 25 render identically and none got worse beyond the measure's noise. Switching it on explicitly still replaces the camera's data. Saved edits render byte-identically.
- Dark, noisy colours keep their hue after colour noise reduction (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 4, new edits and never-edited RAWs): the filter's coarse scales are now built from linear mean colours, so a colour channel that is weak against the noise is no longer pulled towards zero (lifted black fur no longer turns blue; a flat synthetic patch keeps its red ÷ green ratio within 5 %, before −19 %). Measured on 55 high-ISO RAWs against Capture One and Camera Raw, colour edges and the colour texture that matches clean ground-truth frames are unchanged, colour noise is slightly lower and isolated colour specks fall from 58 to 46 per million pixels. Saved edits with versions 1–3 render byte-identically.
- Colour noise reduction keeps more real colour texture where a photo's coarse colour scales are quiet (recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 3, new edits and never-edited RAWs): moss, lichen and small flowers of 8–30 px no longer turn grey at ISO 1600–6400. Measured against clean ground-truth frames of the same scenes (RawNIND), ISO 3200–5000 colour texture is now as close to the truth as the better of Capture One and Camera Raw (error ratio 1.01 / 0.97 at 2–4 / 4–8 px, before 1.07 / 1.11), colour detail kept 0.90 → 1.03 of the better editor at Color 25; colour noise stays within a quarter of an 8-bit step of both. ISO 12800 and up keep their stronger smoothing (still below Capture One's colour texture there). Saved edits with version 1 or 2 render byte-identically.
- Recovered highlights without pink, magenta or green casts: where two colours clip they are rebuilt from the colour still recorded, chrominance is no longer taken from the dark side of edges, and fully clipped areas become neutral at the brightness of the rebuilt colours around them (no grey wedge or lilac band along the clipping contour), held back next to recorded colours (no green rims). Seen with Highlights −100 on public RAWs: the share of pink in formerly clipped areas fell from 2.4 % to 0.1 % (Nikon D800 sun), 4.1 % to 0 % (Sony ZV-1 sky); Capture One and Camera Raw show none. Photos without clipped highlights render byte-identically; others change in their clipped areas (preview proxies are made again).
- Shadows and Highlights in new edits (recipe `toneVersion` 2): Shadows above +50 lifts the quarter-tones further — at +100 the lift at 5 / 3 stops below white is +2.3 / +1.0 EV (before +1.8 / +0.7), the better of Camera Raw's and Capture One's (+2.1 / +1.0 and +2.4 / +1.0) without Capture One's flattening of the quarter-tones; +50 and below are unchanged. Lowering Highlights acts like the two editors at moderate values (−50 at 1 stop below white −0.37 EV, before −0.60; Capture One −0.33, Camera Raw −0.40), keeps whites brighter and the upper midtones untouched, acts at the same tones on every camera (before −0.5 … −1.5 EV at −100 by camera), brings out more structure in bright areas, and at −100 still reaches at least Camera Raw's depth. Saved edits keep their response.
- Exports in Display P3, Adobe RGB (1998) and ProPhoto RGB, chosen in the export dialog and saved in recipes (`--color-space` for `rawtonic export` and the headless export). JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP and AVIF carry the matching ICC profile, JPEG XL declares the space.
- One wide-gamut rendering for every output (ADR 0010): colours beyond sRGB are kept out to the ProPhoto gamut and clipped only by the output, as in Camera Raw; the look's saturated colours were refitted against Camera Raw's ProPhoto renders (281 camera models; sRGB exports 0.937 → 0.908 ΔE from Camera Raw). sRGB exports change accordingly.
- Develop view in Display P3 on macOS, and soft proofing of an output colour space with a gamut warning (Proof in the histogram bar, Shift+S).
- AVIF exports carry XMP (rating, caption, keywords, and the marking of AI-generated areas).
- Saturated highlights, blues and the lightness of colours refitted against Camera Raw (sRGB 0.901 → 0.887 ΔE; yellow highlights 2.21 → 1.88).
- JPEG, PNG, TIFF and WebP sources in Display P3, Adobe RGB, ProPhoto or another embedded profile are read in their colour space (they were read as sRGB).
- Fix: Vibrance turned some saturated reds and oranges grey (in exports and in the Develop view).
- Adding photographs is all or nothing: a session's new files, Add (catalog) and imports from another program's catalog read each photograph's editor sidecar or XMP first and store the photograph with its edit and metadata in one transaction. A crash or full disk no longer leaves a photograph registered without them; the next try adds it complete. A photograph whose sidecar cannot be read is reported and left out instead of stopping the session from opening. `rawtonic add` brings sidecar edits and XMP metadata too.
- Libraries record import batches (database schema 4): each ingest, Add or import remembers the photographs it added, the groundwork for Previous Import.
- **Synchronize Folder…** (a folder's menu in Folders, and the library menu): finds photographs renamed, moved between folders, added or deleted outside Rawtonic, and lists them for review before anything changes. A move counts as verified only when the file's full SHA-256 equals the one on record; moves matched by size and sampled content only, copies of photographs in the library and removals start unticked, and files that could be several photographs need a choice. Photographs on a disconnected drive are never treated as moved or removed. Applying checks every change again and skips what changed since the scan; moved photographs keep their edits, ratings, keywords, versions and albums, and an XMP sidecar that moved along stays the library's. Files on disk are never moved, changed or deleted; a removal only removes the library entry. `rawtonic sync-folder LIBRARY [FOLDER] [--apply]` does the same without the app (`--include-probable`, `--include-copies`, `--remove-missing`).
- Every photograph gets its content hash (SHA-256, database schema 5) on record: ingest stores the hash it verified the copy with; others are hashed in the background after a library opens, one file at a time with pauses (`rawtonic checksums LIBRARY` hashes them at once).
- **Previous Import** in Library › Albums shows the photographs the latest ingest, Add or Synchronize Folder added.
- Colour noise reduction measured per photo and per scale: the chroma is split into five scales, the colour noise of each is measured in the photo itself (its smoothest areas), and each scale keeps only what stands out of its own noise, with luminance and colour edges protected. On the synthetic test set (known clean image, ISO 800–12800, Bayer and X-Trans) at Color 25 it leaves 21 % less colour noise than the previous filter, keeps fine coloured lines and dots far better (mean ΔE00 4.5 against 5.4) and keeps colour texture (3.5× more), with less colour spread across edges. New edits and photos without a saved colour noise setting (also in headless exports) use it; saved edits keep the previous filter (recipe key `colorNoiseVersion`), so their exports do not change. Dragging the Color slider is as fast as before (7–8 ms per frame at 2304 × 1536); a 24 MP export takes about 0.09 s more. Photos larger than the graphics card's texture limit render it in windows exactly as a whole render.
- The multi-scale colour noise reduction (not yet released) cleans more without bleeding: next to brightness edges it keeps only the colour that follows the brightness, it no longer mistakes round noise blotches for colour edges, Color 100 and Smoothness now act on the coarse blotches too, and the finest scale stays crisp next to colour edges. On 26 public RAW files at ISO 1600–204800 (Color 25), against the better of Camera Raw and Capture One: 1–4 px mottling 0.79× → 0.66× (the previous cross-bilateral filter 0.61×), 4–16 px blotches 1.10× → 1.02×; at Color 100 1–4 px 1.14× → 0.86×, 4–16 px 1.42× → 1.30×. Synthetic held-out test: colour spread across edges 0.32 → 0.23, colour noise −13 %. Saved edits with the earlier filter render unchanged.
- Colour noise reduction keeps colour edges as crisp as Camera Raw and Capture One (not yet released filter, recipe `colorNoiseVersion` 2): the decode-time chroma clean-up, which spread colour edges over about 4.5 px before any noise reduction, is skipped for it (saved edits with the earlier filter keep it and render unchanged); rebuilding its scales keeps each colour on its side of an edge (edge-aware expansion, with a guard that treats a lone far-off pixel as noise); Color above 25 also smooths colour variation larger than the scales reach. On 26 public RAW files at ISO 1600–204800, against the better of Camera Raw and Capture One (median, all files): colour-edge sharpness 0.81× → 1.06× at Color 25 and 0.88× → 0.99× at Color 100, colour detail kept 1.03× → 1.80×, 4–16 px blotches at Color 100 1.30× → 0.97×; colour noise stays below both (0.85× at Color 25). Dragging Color costs as before (8–9 ms per frame at 2304 × 1536); photos above 16 MP rebuild the scales on the CPU in about 25 ms instead of 8 ms.
- Colour noise reduction at Color 50 and 100 keeps colour edges and colour detail like at 25 (its edge guards and ranges no longer widen with the amount, which only removed colour noise far below one 8-bit step): against the better of Camera Raw and Capture One, edge sharpness at Color 100 0.99× → 1.10×, colour detail at ISO 12800+ 0.93× → 1.25×; Color 25 is unchanged.
- Luminance noise reduction leaves more even grain at its low settings (new edits and never-edited RAWs): it follows the photo's own luminance noise by brightness (read noise in the shadows) and, below Luminance 50, keeps a uniform share of fine grain instead of smoothing flat areas to plastic while texture and edges stay grainy. Against Capture One's default on 26 public RAW files, at the RAW default (Luminance 15): unevenness of the grain 1.35× → 1.21× (ISO 3200–5000: 1.85× → 1.40×), fine texture kept 1.08× → 1.12×, luminance noise still below Capture One's (0.84×). Luminance 50 and above render as before; saved edits with the earlier colour noise filter are unchanged.
- Light flare renders on photos larger than the graphics card's texture limit (it failed with an error), exactly as a whole render. Its glow map now reads the photo with its own bilinear filtering instead of the graphics card's, so it is the same on every graphics card; exports with Light flare change by at most 1 level in 8-bit (0.0006–0.18 % of values in the measured renders) and by at most 68 of 65 535 in 16-bit. Exports without it do not change.
- A batch export that failed for some photographs lists each of them with the reason in the export queue, translated where the reason is known (offline RAW, texture limit, a file that could not be written).
- Hot folder captures form an import batch (database schema 6), so Previous Import shows them; after another import the next capture starts a new one. A capture's next-capture adjustments (preset or the previous photograph's settings) are stored with it in one transaction: a capture is never registered without them, and the retry stores both.

- Rename the app from Re-touch to **Rawtonic** (rawtonic.cc). Bundle ID `cc.rawtonic.editor`; settings, presets, LUTs, albums and the remembered library are carried over once from `io.retouch.editor` and the former files stay untouched. CLI `rawtonic`, crates `rawtonic-*`, environment variables `RAWTONIC_*`.
- One export dialog: the Studio export now has WebP, JPEG XL and AVIF, resizing by long/short edge, width, height, megapixels or percentage with an optional enlarge, watermark, border, pad to aspect ratio, a subfolder for every destination, kept folder structure, capture-time file dates, mask export and an estimated size, all saved in recipes. The inherited export panel is removed; older recipes open with the previous defaults.
- XMP sidecars use the namespace `https://rawtonic.cc/ns/1.0/`; sidecars written as Re-touch are still read and converted on the next save. Sidecars no longer grow with every save (each rewrite used to leave an empty description behind).
- Ingest names come from each photograph's EXIF: capture date and time (DateTimeOriginal with sub-seconds and time zone, then DateTimeDigitized, the camera's file date, a CRW's own time stamp, and only then the file's modification time), camera make/model, serial number, lens, ISO, focal length, aperture and shutter speed, plus the file number, card folder, and rating, label, title, creator and copyright. The catalog's date folders use the same date. The ingest dialog's example shows the first chosen photograph's real name and where its date came from. Older templates keep working. `rawtonic name` shows the names without copying.
- Ingest copies a photograph's XMP sidecar from the card (`NAME.xmp` or `NAME.EXT.xmp`) beside the copy and its backup under the new name, verified and never replacing a file, and the library takes over its rating, label, keywords and IPTC fields.
