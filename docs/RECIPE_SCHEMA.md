# Recipe schema
Managed storage uses a versioned envelope, never unversioned loose parameters:
```json
{"schema_version":1,"process_version":"rapidraw-1.6.4","adjustments":{},"layers":[],"snapshot":null}
```
The inherited adjustment schema is defined by `src/utils/adjustments.ts` defaults and UI slider bounds; the adapter preserves fields losslessly. `adjustments` contains the native renderer recipe, including inherited masks/crop/retouch vectors. `layers` is reserved for the future independent composite model and must remain empty until implemented, rather than being silently ignored. Each history step is immutable in SQLite, with a version head pointer; undo/redo changes the head. Metadata is shared between versions.
Known fields: exposure -5..5 EV, contrast/highlights/shadows/whites/blacks -100..100, saturation/vibrance -100..100; exact inherited defaults must be checked before conversion. These are target semantic ranges, not a statement that upstream scale matches. Do not translate without an explicit process migration.
Forward versions are rejected for editing. Unknown adjustment fields are retained. Raster mask paths must be relative to the session and accompanied by hashes before portable package export. Validation and a complete machine-readable schema are outstanding.
`adjustments.sectionVisibility` switches parts of the recipe off without losing their values. Inherited master keys: `basic` (exposure, contrast, brightness, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks), `color` (white balance, saturation, vibrance, hue, colour grading, colour mixer, calibration), `curves`, `details` (sharpening, noise reduction, clarity, structure, dehaze), `effects`. Per-tool keys (2026-10-03, Lightroom's panel eye) narrow these further: `whiteBalance` (temperature, tint), `presence` (clarity, structure, dehaze, vibrance, saturation), `colorMixer` (hsl), `sharpening` (sharpness, sharpnessThreshold), `noiseReduction` (luma and colour noise reduction). A parameter renders only if its master and its tool key are not `false`; a missing key means on.

`adjustments.guidedPerspective` holds the guided perspective: `lines` (up to two `vertical` and two `horizontal` guides, coordinates 0–1 of the uncropped photo), `enabled` (two or more lines), `upright` (2026-10-04: `off`, `auto`, `level`, `vertical`, `full` or `guided`; missing on recipes from before Upright) and `autoCrop`. With `upright` set to anything but `off`, the correction zooms until no empty corners remain (Lightroom's Constrain crop) unless `autoCrop` is `false`; recipes without `upright` never zoom.

`adjustments.baseCurve` (2026-10-04): `"standard"` (missing key), `"highContrast"`, `"extraShadow"`, `"linear"`. RAW files only. Other names render with the standard curve. The curve choices are applied after the base look as a change of display luminance and chroma by display level: High contrast darkens the shadows and brightens the upper midtones around mid grey, Extra shadow lifts the shadows by up to 0.8 EV, Linear response undoes the default tone curve (Rawtonic's own shapes since 2026-10-11). Part of the copy/paste group with Exposure and the tone mapper.

`adjustments.grainStructure` (2026-10-12): `"even"` (missing key, the original grain), `"soft"`, `"clumped"`, `"speckled"`, `"gritty"`. Unknown names render as `"even"`, so recipes without the key export byte-identically to before (the renderer, CLI and headless export never default to anything else). The editor writes the key: grain turned on in a recipe without one gets `"clumped"` (new edits, and grain turned on again after resetting the grain tool, which removes the key); a recipe loaded with grain but without the key gets `"even"` written, so its grain keeps rendering Even when copied, pasted, synced, saved as a preset or turned off and on; presets and pasted settings from before the choice with grain but no key are applied as `"even"`. Lightroom presets, XMP and catalog imports with grain get `"clumped"`. Amount, Size and Roughness work for every structure and each is scaled to Even's strength at Roughness 50 (Gritty 1.4×). Even and Clumped are equally strong in every tone (Clumped: Roughness gathers fine grain into clumps); Soft (rounded grain with irregular clusters), Speckled (bright specks over a fine grain) and Gritty (coarse, hard-edged grain) are strongest around display level 0.45 and fade towards black and white. Capture One styles with grain map `FilmGrainType` onto it (Fine or none → even, Silver Rich/Soft/Cubic → soft, Tabular → speckled, Harsh → gritty). Part of the grain copy/paste group.

`adjustments.grainVersion` (2026-10-23, `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `GRAIN_VERSION`, shader `apply_portable_grain`): which grain `grainAmount`, `grainSize`, `grainRoughness` and `grainStructure` render. `2`: the portable grain — the same gradient noise, structures, strength and tone masks as `1`, but the lattice cell and the position in it are computed exactly in integers (12 fractional bits, the frequency by integer long division) and the gradients come from an integer hash (PCG), so every GPU renders the same grain pattern (within float rounding of the interpolation); statistically the same grain, measured 2026-10-23 on a Nikon D7200 NEF export (grain = render − the render without grain, luma in 8-bit levels, mid-tones): standard deviation version 2 / version 1 = 1.000–1.001 at Amount 25/50/100 (Size 25, Roughness 50), Size 0/50/100, Roughness 0/100 and the Soft, Clumped, Speckled and Gritty structures (e.g. Amount 50: 7.66 vs 7.65), spectral centroid within ±0.002 cycles/px (0.068–0.383), share of power below 0.05 cycles/px, skewness and kurtosis equal within ±0.02 (Speckled +0.64 / +1.40 vs +0.64 / +1.38); a noise-only comparison of both versions over all structures × Size 0/25/50/100 × Roughness 0/50/100 on a 1024² window gave std ratios 0.985–1.010 (within that window's sampling noise). Unverified: other cameras and image sizes beyond one 24 MP photo, and the running app side by side. Size is clamped to slider 5–200. `1`: the earlier grain, whose float hash turned one-ulp differences between GPUs into a different pattern (an AMD and an NVIDIA GPU differed on ~90 % of the pixels). Without the key a recipe renders with `1`; the editor writes `2` when grain is turned on in a recipe without the key, writes `1` into a loaded recipe, preset or pasted settings with grain but without the key (in Copy/Sync it is a processing version: the destination keeps its own unless the processing versions are chosen), removes the key with the Effects tool's reset, and copies it with the grain values (the source's, else `1` for a source with grain, `2` for one without); Lightroom and Capture One imports with grain get `2`. A value above the newest renders as the newest.

RAW detail defaults for recipes without the key (2026-10-04): `sharpness` 40, `colorNoiseReduction` 25, `lumaNoiseReduction` 15; JPEG/TIFF 0.

`adjustments.colorNoiseVersion` (2026-10-13, `crates/engine/src/color_noise.rs`): which colour noise reduction `colorNoiseReduction`, `colorNoiseDetail` and `colorNoiseSmoothness` drive. `2`: the multi-scale filter (chroma pyramid of five scales, each thresholded by the colour noise measured in the photo at that scale and brightness; luminance and colour edges protected; its output changed on 2026-10-14 before any release, with the brightness noise profile and refitted guards, Detail and Smoothness, and again the same day with luminance-following and coherent edge guards, a finest-level edge guard and stronger Color 100, and with edge-aware expansions, the coarsest level smoothed above Color 25 and the base image without the decode-time chroma clean-up). `3` (2026-10-16): the same multi-scale filter keeping more 8–30 px colour texture where those scales of the photo are quiet: from 1/8 of the resolution on, a scale's measured colour noise is taken as at most the finer scale's ÷ 1.7, and the thresholds of the 1/8 and 1/16 scales fall with their noise (2.5 + 2.75 · log2(noise / 0.012) multiples of it, between 2.5 and version 2's 8 / 7; `Tuning::for_version`). `4` (2026-10-17): the same filter as `3` with each scale built from the linear mean colour of the pixels it covers instead of the mean of their square-root chroma, so dark noisy colours keep their hue once the noise is removed (averaged square roots pulled a channel that is weak against its noise towards zero: lifted black fur turned blue; `Tuning::linear_means`); luminance and its noise measurement are unchanged, so Color 0 renders as `3`. `5` (2026-10-17): the same filter as `4`; at full resolution a pixel whose chroma is more than one unit of the colour noise from the median of its 3 × 3 neighbourhood takes that median to decide which side of a colour edge its edge-aware expansion follows (`Tuning::guide`, `Guide::Switch`; its own chroma otherwise, and its detail stays its own), so fewer isolated off-colour pixels remain along colour edges; Color 0 renders as `4`. `6` (2026-10-17): the same filter as `5`; the full-resolution detail is measured against the same edge-aware expansion of the coarser scale as the reconstruction (`Tuning::finest_detail_aware`), as the coarser scales already were; against the plain expansion a pixel next to a colour edge overshot past its own colour (halos, saturated rims on the dark side of an edge) or snapped to the other side's colour where its noisy chroma picked it (blotches along edges); colour transitions stay as wide as the photo has them (0.1–0.45 px wider than `5`); Color 0 renders as `5`. `7` (2026-10-18): the same filter as `6`; at full resolution a pixel whose chroma is more than two units of the colour noise from the median of its 3 × 3 neighbourhood while its square-root luminance is within one unit of the noise of theirs (an isolated off-colour pixel, not a coloured point brighter or darker than its surroundings) takes the median chroma as its detail (`Tuning::speck_swap`), so fewer isolated colour specks remain; Color 0 renders as `6`. `8` (2026-10-20): the same filter as `7`, judging the pixel's luminance after the luminance noise reduction instead of before it (`Tuning::speck_luma_after_nr`): a pixel brighter or darker than its neighbours only by noise that the luminance noise reduction takes back is an isolated off-colour pixel, a point it keeps standing out keeps its colour; with Luminance 0 and at Color 0 it renders as `7`. `9` (2026-10-20): the same filter as `8`; each pixel's filtered chroma is kept within the range, per component, of the plain reconstruction of the coarser scale at the pixel and its 8 neighbours (and of its own edge-aware reconstruction), widened by 1.5 units of the colour noise (`Tuning::range_clamp`): a pixel whose noisy chroma the detail kept along luminance and colour edges no longer stands out of its neighbourhood as a colour speck; the clamp weakens with the filter below Color 25 and above Detail 50, as the speck swap does, and fades out for chroma 4–8 units of the noise beyond the range, so a coloured point that far keeps its colour; Color 0 renders as `8`. `10` (2026-10-24): the same filter as `9`; the range leaves out the pixel's own edge-aware reconstruction (only the plain reconstruction at the pixel and its 8 neighbours) and is widened by 1.25 units of the colour noise (`Tuning::range_plain`, `RANGE_CLAMP_PLAIN`): where the coarser scale still holds some colour noise (mostly at Color 25), a pixel whose reconstruction or kept detail took its own noisy colour no longer stands out of its neighbourhood as a colour speck; Color 0 renders as `9`. `2` to `9` recipes render exactly as before; all nine share the base image. `1`: the earlier cross-bilateral filter in the renderer. Without the key a recipe keeps what it rendered with before: `1` when it sets `colorNoiseReduction`, the newest (`10`) when a RAW recipe leaves it to the default (never-edited photos, headless exports without a recipe). The editor writes `10` into new edits, keeps a saved recipe's filter (also `2` to `9`) when loading it, and copies the key with the noise reduction group. Layers keep their colour amount: with version 2 to 10 a layer below the photo's amount blends towards the unfiltered colour, a layer above it gets the photo's filter, and a layer on a photo with Color 0 uses the cross-bilateral filter. With `2` to `10` the base image is developed without the decode-time chroma clean-up (preference `rawPreprocessingColorNr`; the multi-scale filter measures and removes the colour noise itself), with `1` as before (`BaseImageOptions::for_color_noise`); decoded-image cache keys and preview proxies record which base they hold, and the editor develops the open photo again when its filter changes. With `2` to `10` the luminance noise reduction also follows the photo's luminance noise by brightness (measured with the colour pyramid, also at Color 0) and, below Luminance 50, leaves a more even share of grain (2026-10-14); `1` recipes render their luminance exactly as before.

`adjustments.toneVersion` (2026-10-16, `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `TONE_VERSION`, shader `cr_shadows_reach` and the `HL_V2_*` constants of `apply_highlights_adjustment`): which response the tone sliders have under the RAW look. `2`: Shadows above +50 lifts the quarter-tones further (display −8 … −1.5 EV, faded in quadratically from +50, so +50 and below render exactly as with `1`; at +100 it reaches the better of Capture One's and Camera Raw's Shadows +100 there), and lowering Highlights is refitted: it works in units of the look's exposure (the same display levels on every camera), −50 is about 0.4 of −100's change (Highlights −50 near Capture One's and Camera Raw's −50, −100 at least Camera Raw's reach), less of the upper midtones, more bright local detail; raising Highlights is unchanged. `1`: the earlier response. Without the key a recipe renders with `1` (old edits keep their look; the CLI and headless exports follow the recipe). The editor writes `2` into new edits and into loaded recipes without the key when nothing in them renders differently (no Shadows above +50 and no lowered Highlights on the photo or a layer); otherwise it keeps the recipe's version. The key is photo-wide (layers' Shadows and Highlights follow it), copied with the tone group and with Shadows in a tool copy, and reset with the Basic tool on the photo.

`adjustments.curves.version` (and a layer's `curves.version`; 2026-10-21, `crates/engine/src/point_curve.rs` `LONG_CURVE_VERSION`, `WIDE_CURVE_VERSION`, shader `wide_curve_input`): how the point curves (`luma` = the RGB curve, `red`, `green`, `blue`) render. `3`: in the wide working space, as Camera Raw's do — the colour in ProPhoto primaries (Bradford D65 → D50) encoded with the sRGB curve, each channel through the RGB and its own curve, and back; nothing is clipped to sRGB or carried past the curves (the ProPhoto cube holds the whole working gamut). Only when a curve changes something: neutral curves skip the conversion and render as without the key. Measured against Camera Raw on two photos: 0.06 levels for a red channel curve (9.6 on display values), 0.66 for the RGB curve on saturated colours (1.46). `2`: on display values clipped to sRGB, with the part beyond sRGB carried at the curve's luminance ratio; curves of the photo with more than 16 points render whole (layers: their first 16). Missing (`1`): on display values, the first 16 points. The editor writes `3` into new edits and Lightroom imports write it; an edit of curves that already change something keeps their version (at least `2`), so moving a point of an older curve does not shift its colours; reset curves start at `3`. Curves of different versions compare equal where they render alike. Part of the curves, so it travels with them through copy, paste, presets and sync.

`adjustments.curves.luminance` (and a layer's; 2026-10-21, `crates/engine/src/point_curve.rs` `luminance_points`, shader `apply_luminance_curve`): the Luminance curve, points like the other channels (0–255, at most 16 render). After the RGB and channel curves, the colour's luminance (Rec. 709 Y of linear sRGB) goes through the curve on its sRGB encoding (the other channels' axis, so grey moves as under the RGB curve) and the linear colour is scaled by the ratio, at most 4× with the rest of the change added as grey (so a lifted black end lifts black, which scaling cannot): chromaticity, and with it saturation and hue, stays wherever the ratio is below 4. Missing or neutral: nothing (the shader skips it). Independent of `curves.version`. Capture One's `GradationCurveY` imports into it.

`adjustments.cameraCaVersion` (2026-10-17, `crates/engine/src/embedded_lens.rs` `CA_VERSION`): which cameras' own lateral CA data the camera lens mode (`lensCorrectionMode` `camera`) reads when the parameters are filled in from the RAW (saving, pasting, `apply_default`). `1`: also Panasonic's (RW2 IFD0 tag 0x011b, `embedded_lens::panasonic_chromatic`), which brings camera mode to Panasonic bodies that store CA data without distortion data (TZ81). Missing: Fujifilm's and Sony's only, as before, so a recipe saved again keeps its parameters. The rendering itself reads only `lensDistortionParams`. The editor writes `1` into new RAW edits and when the camera's correction is applied; headless exports of never-edited RAWs with camera data get it through `embedded_lens::apply_default`. Copied with the lens correction group.

`adjustments.diffractionCorrection` / `adjustments.diffractionVersion` (`crates/engine/src/diffraction.rs` `VERSION`): sharpening by the f-number and the pixel pitch, RAW only. The pitch comes from the file's FocalPlaneXResolution, else from the body's crop factor in the bundled Lensfun database. `diffractionVersion` `2` (2026-10-18, written when the correction is switched on, copied with it): the whole database. Missing (`1`): without the camera entries the 2026-10-18 database update added (`lens_correction::CAMERAS_ADDED_2026_10_18`: EOS R8 Mark II, PowerShot A710 IS, Z5IIC, OM System PEN, Lumix DC-L10, DC-TZ300/ZS300), so saved recipes render as with the earlier database; on those bodies they get no correction, as before. Offline photos keep both values in their source facts (`diffraction_sigma_v1` when it differs).

`adjustments.lensProfileFor` (2026-10-18, `src-tauri/src/file_management/edits.rs` `LENS_PROFILE_FOR`): what the recipe's `lensDistortionParams` were resolved for — the lens mode and lens (chosen or detected; for the camera's own data its `cameraCaVersion`) and the parameters themselves, as a string, or `"unresolved"` when no lookup was possible yet (no database or EXIF). A save keeps the parameters only while this matches; a new lens choice, a cleared or changed parameter set or `"unresolved"` is looked up again, so a Lensfun database update (renamed, re-measured or newly covered lens) reaches a saved edit only through a new lens choice or Auto lens correction. Missing (recipes saved before it): the parameters saved with the recipe count as resolved for its saved lens choice; the editor adds the key when it loads such a recipe. A recipe marked `"unresolved"` is resolved from the photo before any render (`rawtonic_export::exif_processing::prepare_recipe_lens`: export, thumbnails, editor, face retouch, collage, multi-view) and when loaded into the editor; an export fails with a clear error when that still cannot be done (lens database missing). Rendering otherwise ignores it. Not copied or pasted.

`adjustments.autoCaVersion` (2026-10-17, `crates/engine/src/lateral_ca.rs` `VERSION`): the automatic lateral chromatic aberration correction (Remove chromatic aberration, `autoChromaticAberration`). `2`: the red and blue scale found on a reduced copy is refined at the photo's full resolution, and the refined and the reduced copy's model are tried at ¼, ½, ¾ and full strength; the one that leaves the least colour unexplained by the local luminance at the measured edges wins, and nothing is applied unless it lowers that colour by 3 % (`estimate_v2`). With `2` the correction is also on when `autoChromaticAberration` is not set, unless the lens correction carries a lateral CA correction of its own (camera data with `lensTcaEnabled`, or a profile's TCA); `true` replaces that correction, `false` turns it off. `3` (2026-10-17): the same estimate, and where the lens correction has its own lateral CA correction at its default amount (`lensTcaAmount` 100, `lensTcaEnabled`) and `autoChromaticAberration` is not set, it competes with it instead of leaving it: the lens data's correction, no correction, version 2's candidates and the lens data's correction refined at full resolution are scored by the same check, and the best replaces the lens data's correction only when the evidence is clear (`estimate_versus`): at least 20 % less colour on all measured patches, on each half of them and on those beyond 0.6 of the radius, and a clearly smaller red/blue shift left by the patch-shift measurement; otherwise the lens data's correction renders as with `2`, byte for byte. `false` keeps the lens data's correction (the user's choice), `true` replaces it. The editor shows the switch on in that case; turning it off stores `false`. Missing (= `1`): the earlier estimate, only when `autoChromaticAberration` is `true`. The editor writes `3` into new RAW edits (also a recipe without `lensCorrectionMode`, as for the lens defaults); saved recipes keep rendering as before. Headless exports of never-edited RAWs get it through `embedded_lens::apply_default`. A geometry key, copied with the lens correction group.

`adjustments.fringeVersion` (2026-10-16, `crates/engine/src/edge_fringe.rs` `VERSION`): the edge-fringe pass. `1`: after the lens correction (so after any lateral CA correction) and before the renderer's noise reduction and sharpening, colour along strong luminance edges that neither side of the edge explains — demosaic false colour on thin lines, rests of lateral CA, the pink and cyan fringes of branches against a bright sky — is replaced by a local model of colour on luminance (5 × 5 guided filter in square-root RGB at full resolution, only near strong edges, a pixel's chroma never raised); two-colour edges and thin coloured lines keep their colour. Reduced previews below 0.35 of the full resolution and thumbnails skip it. `2` (2026-10-17): the same in 7 × 7 windows, which also clear 1–2 px lateral-CA rests and fringes on 2–3 px lines that the colour noise reduction otherwise breaks into blotches along the edge; on noisy photos (luminance noise measured on the photo from half the edge test's start, about ISO 3200 and up) it keeps the 5 × 5 windows and renders as `1`. `3` (2026-10-20): as `2`, and on the same low-noise photos a second stage for edges between two saturated colours, where the demosaic leaves red and blue a pixel off green (a light or tinted seam between cyan and magenta of equal luminance, a tinted line along a yellow object's edge against grey): in each 7 × 7 window that holds a strong edge and two colours (little colour variance off its principal colour line, in square-root RGB) the pixels move onto the window's principal colour line in linear RGB (the line a blur between the two colours follows), averaged over the windows that hold them; windows of three or more colours are left alone, and so is a pixel whose channels lie well outside the ranges of its window's two colours (a small patch of a third colour on the edge; misregistration only mixes each channel between the two); noisy photos render as `1`. `4` (2026-10-20, `edge_fringe/axial.rs`): first, on every photo (noisy ones too), chromatic aberration across strong edges, mostly axial (purple or blue fringes on dark branches against the sky, a green or yellow rim on the dark side of a bright edge, up to about 8 px wide): each pixel near an edge of more than about two stops looks along the luminance gradient for the edge's dark and bright sides and takes the colour of their linear-light mix at its own luminance (its luminance is kept, its colour never raised). A pixel keeps its colour where a channel lies well beyond both sides' values (a third colour), where the luminance between the sides rises in two steps (a band of another colour along the edge, a printed stroke, a light leaf before the sky), or where its colour differs from the mix in a hue outside the purple-to-blue and yellow-to-green families of axial aberration by more than about twice the photo's noise (an orange glow). Then as `3`. Missing: no pass, as before; recipes with `1` keep the 5 × 5 windows, recipes with `2` and `3` render as before. The editor writes `4` into new RAW edits (also a recipe without `lensCorrectionMode`, as for the lens defaults); saved recipes keep rendering without it. Headless exports of never-edited RAWs get it through `embedded_lens::apply_default`. Copied with the chromatic aberration group.

`adjustments.defringeVersion` (2026-10-17, `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `DEFRINGE_VERSION`, shader `apply_defringe2`): the fringe detector of Defringe (`defringePurpleAmount`, `defringeGreenAmount` and their hue ranges, and the layers' `defringe`). `2`: colour in the selected ranges is a fringe where the edge across it does not explain it — pairs of samples on opposite sides of the pixel (four directions, 3, 7 and 16 detail-scaled pixels) stand for the edge's two sides, the edge predicts their linear-light mix at the pixel's luminance (below the darker side, a thin dark line, that side's colour fades as a darker copy of it), and only pairs with a much brighter, lit side count; a patch of one colour and brightness and the edge of a patch of the pixel's own hue are kept. A fringe moves to the most neutral prediction along its own hue and, where that prediction lies outside the selected ranges, turns to its hue (a lavender sky rim back to the sky's blue, a thin branch against the sky dark slate); a turn that would leave the colour in range is dropped. Full strength from about amount 7, as `1`. `1`: every colour in range near a luminance edge loses chroma. Without the key a recipe renders with `1`; the editor writes `2` into new edits (RAW and other), keeps a saved recipe's detector when Defringe or a layer's Defringe is in use (else it may take `2`, which renders the same), resets to `2` with the tool's reset, and copies the key with the Defringe amounts. A value above the newest renders as the newest.

`adjustments.sharpenVersion` (2026-10-19, `3` 2026-10-21, `4` 2026-10-23; `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `SHARPEN_VERSION`, shader `apply_sharpen_v2`): the response of the sharpening (`sharpness` with `sharpenRadius`, `sharpenDetail`, `sharpenMasking`, `sharpnessThreshold`, `sharpnessFalloff`, and the layers' `sharpness`, which add to the amount). `4`: response `3` with Radius and Masking after Camera Raw's: below Radius 1 the Threshold gate reads the unsharp difference rescaled to Radius 1's kernel (its high-pass share) and the gain rises by that ratio^0.15; Masking measures the spread over a 3 px Gaussian (detail-scaled; every pixel within the sharpening kernel, beyond it every other point of the even grid, weighted for the pixels it stands for) and ramps in from zero spread up to 1.75 × 1.5 times the earlier threshold. From Radius 1 up without Masking `4` renders as `3`. `3`: response `2` weighted by lightness, as Camera Raw's sharpening is: it fades out towards the shadows (none below a perceptual luminance of 0.03, full from 0.27) and rises up to twice in the highlights (from 0.4 to 0.65), at every amount including 40. `2`: the unsharp mask is limited to a halo ceiling that barely grows with the amount (amount^0.05), its small-signal gain grows as amount^1.75 from Detail 25 up (0.9 at Detail 0), the Detail tilt stops growing at Amount 56 and the amount adds a tilt of its own below Detail 100; fitted to Camera Raw's band gains and halos at Amount 40–150, Detail 0–100. `1`: the earlier response, whose halos grew linearly with the amount. `1` and `2` are the same at Amount 40 with Radius 1, Detail 25 and Masking 0; all three are the same for negative amounts and without sharpening. Without the key a recipe renders with `1`; the editor writes `4` into new edits (RAW and other), gives a saved recipe without the key `4` only where it renders the same (no sharpening, and no layer with a sharpening amount) or where it is new (empty, as Library Reset saves it; the backend's `apply_render_default` gives such a recipe, and a photo without any, `4` too, so never-edited RAWs render with it in exports and thumbnails), resets to `4` with the Sharpening tool's reset, and copies the key with the sharpening controls; Lightroom XMP with sharpening imports as `4`. A value above the newest renders as the newest.

`adjustments.lumaNoiseVersion` (2026-10-19, `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `LUMA_NOISE_VERSION`, shader `luma_speck`): the luminance noise reduction (`lumaNoiseReduction` with `lumaNoiseDetail`, `lumaNoiseContrast`, and the layers' `lumaNoiseReduction`, which add to the amount). `2`: also takes isolated luminance specks out of noisy photos — a pixel's luminance is kept within the range of its eight neighbours plus half its noise (at the same chromaticity), and the sharpening (and diffraction correction) does not lift a pixel that stands out of that range by noise alone; a pixel more than 4–6 noise levels out of its neighbours' range (a point light, a star) is left as it is. It fades in with the luminance amount up to 15 and with the photo's measured noise level between 0.002 and 0.004, and is skipped at or below 0.002, where photos render exactly as `1` (most base-ISO photos; just above it the change is below one 8-bit step). `1`: the earlier filter. Without luminance noise reduction both are the same. Without the key a recipe renders with `1`; the editor writes `2` for new edits and upgrades a saved recipe without luminance noise reduction (photo or layer) to it.

`adjustments.renderVersion` (2026-10-20, `crates/engine/src/image_processing.rs` `RENDER_VERSION`): fixes in the render pipeline that change the pixels of saved edits without a tool of their own. `2`: lens and geometry warps and straightening keep negative (wide-gamut) channels where the source has them (a Catmull-Rom sample may go down to its lowest tap when that is negative, else stops at 0 as before); the large blur of Clarity, Dehaze and Glow (photo and layers) has its whole radius at render-tile edges on photos with a short side above 3456 pixels (it stopped at the tiles' 128-pixel overlap, leaving seams every 2048 pixels); the Classic and AgX looks' Color Grading and Calibration are off when their tool switches are (the other looks always were). `3` (2026-10-23, `crates/rawdecode/src/hot_pixels.rs` `DeadTest`, `crates/engine/src/image_loader.rs` `DecodeVariant`): a RAW photosite counts as dead only when it is also darker than 0.6 × each adjacent sample of the other colours (white-balanced), as the hot test already asked of bright ones; before, the same-colour test alone lifted the red and blue samples on thin dark lines (slats, mesh, wires) to the samples beside them, which showed as magenta and green stripes on X-Trans files. `adjustments.lutPath` / `adjustments.lutIntensity` (2026-10-24, `crates/engine/src/lut.rs`): the LUT and its amount, 0–200 (default 100; values above 200 render as 200). Up to 100 the LUT's colour is mixed with the colour without it, as before. Above 100 (new; earlier editors never wrote it) the LUT's change is extrapolated in the LUT's own output space (display values, sRGB-encoded): `lut + (amount/100 − 1)·(lut − base)`, the extra part scaled down as a whole, keeping its direction, so that no channel leaves the [0, 1] cube (ratio r of the room left to the nearest face over the extra step, softened to r / (1 + r⁴)^¼; a channel already beyond the cube gets no step outward; `lut::apply_amount`, shader `lut_amount`). .cube files are read per Adobe's Cube LUT Specification 1.0 and the shaper form Resolve writes: 3D, 1D, or a 1D shaper before a 3D table, with `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX` and `LUT_1D_INPUT_RANGE`/`LUT_3D_INPUT_RANGE`. A file the earlier reader took (a 3D table) is read exactly as before, so saved edits render unchanged; that reader ignores `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX`, so importing a 3D table whose domain is not 0–1 is refused (reported) rather than reading it two ways. 1D tables and shapers (which did not load before) are baked into a 65³ table over the unit domain, checked against the file's own reading at 8192 colours; a file it misses by more than 1/255 (a curve too steep for the grid, such as a square root near black) is refused.

Mask geometry (2026-10-10, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation.rs` `PhotoGeometry`): AI mask components (`ai-subject`, `ai-foreground`, `ai-sky`, `ai-people`, `ai-depth`, `quick-eraser`) store `maskDataBase64` in the photo's own frame (lens- and transform-corrected, before orientation, flips, straightening and crop) and are placed by the photo's geometry when drawn, so they stay on the content when the photo is turned, flipped, straightened or cropped later; their `rotation`, `flipHorizontal`, `flipVertical` and `orientationSteps` only record the geometry they were made in. `warp` (masks made since 2026-10-10) holds the lens and transform adjustments (`GEOMETRY_KEYS`) the bitmap was computed in; when they differ from the photo's now, the mask is moved through the source into the current warp. Colour range `samples` and the `targetX`/`targetY` point of `color` and `luminance` components are full-resolution points (and areas) of the photo as shown when they were taken, before the crop; a sample's own `rotation`, `flipHorizontal`, `flipVertical` and `orientationSteps` give that geometry (older samples use the component's).

Drawn mask frames (2026-10-10, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation.rs` `ShapeFrame`): `radial` and `linear` components and every stroke (`lines[]`) of `brush` and `flow` components (and the brush-shaped `clone`, `heal`, `liquify`, `retouch`) keep their coordinates in the photo as shown when they were drawn (full-resolution pixels after orientation, flips and straightening, before the crop). Their optional `frame` records that geometry: `rotation`, `flipHorizontal`, `flipVertical`, `orientationSteps`, and `width` × `height`, the photo's own size (full resolution, before orientation). When the photo's geometry differs from its frame, the shape is drawn where that spot of the photo is shown now (points moved through the photo's own frame; a radial's `rotation` turned with it; a linear's start and end swapped when one side is mirrored relative to the other), so drawn masks stay on the content when the photo is turned, flipped or straightened later. Without `frame` (recipes before 2026-10-10) a shape is drawn as the photo is shown now, as before. Each brush stroke has its own frame, so strokes painted after a turn and those painted before each keep their spot. The editor writes the frame for every new shape and stroke and for a shape whose handles are dragged (converted to the current geometry); when an edit changes the orientation, flips or straightening, shapes without a frame that the edit left alone get the frame they were shown in until then — in the editor (`keepDrawnMasksOnPhoto`, src/utils/maskGeometry.ts) and for pasted, synced and preset settings and auto settings applied to photos in the backend (`keep_drawn_masks_on_photo`; there the masks left alone are those whose parameters the edit did not change). Imported Lightroom masks record the photo's own frame (Lightroom keeps them in the unrotated photo).

Frame sizes (2026-10-11): `width` and `height` may be absent (frames stamped by the backend, which does not decode the photo); such a frame is in the size of the photo it is on. A frame whose size differs from the photo's own size by a pixel or more (a mask copied, synced or saved in a preset from a photo of another size) places the shape proportionally, as Lightroom keeps its masks relative to the photo: every point at the same fraction of the photo's own width and height (pixel index i of n at (i + 0.5) / n), a radial's first radius along its carried axis and its second so that the ellipse's area scales with the photo's, a linear's `range` and `rangeEnd` as distances from the carried central line, a stroke's `brushSize` by the square root of the area ratio. Copying settings, syncing them and saving a preset give the drawn masks they take along the frame of their photo (`frameDrawnMasks`; its size when it is the open photo). Frames recorded with the same size, and shapes without a frame, render as before.

Masks read from the photo's own frame (AI masks, colour and luminance ranges, the luma range, colour samples, Auto Mask strokes) take, on a straightened photo, the photo's pixel nearest the point the straightened photo is resampled at (since 2026-10-11; they rounded that point down before, half a pixel off on average).

Auto Mask version (2026-10-18, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation.rs` `BrushLine`, `AutoMaskModel`): a brush stroke with `autoMask: true` may carry `autoMaskVersion`. Absent (every stroke before 2026-10-18, and strokes imported from Lightroom presets) is version 1: each dab keeps colours by their CIE Lab distance to the colour under its centre (fitted to Camera Raw), and such strokes render byte-identically to before. `2` is the edge-aware mask the editor writes for new strokes: per dab, the colours under it split into clusters, the one nearest the centre's colour is the subject, the others are backgrounds unless they are the subject's soft-edge mix, and a pixel keeps the subject's share of its linear-light mix with the nearest background; the stroke's coverage is then multiplied by the tip-weighted mean of its dabs' shares. `3` (2026-10-18, new strokes; `stroke_models_v3`) models the stroke's dabs together: each dab's subject is its cluster nearest the medoid of the subjects the dabs within three radii along the stroke pick by their centre's colour (a centre on a gap or highlight no longer turns the subject round); the dabs within six radii vote on which side of the stroke their backgrounds lie, and where that side is trusted a background cluster lying behind the centre counts as the subject's own and pixels behind the centre are kept whatever their colour (dark gaps in fur, shaded leaves). Strokes with `2` keep version 2, byte-identically. Versions 2 and 3 need the photo like version 1 does. A stroke changes version only by the layer menu's **Upgrade to current Auto Mask** (2026-10-18, `src/utils/autoMaskUpgrade.ts`, decision D6): one history step that writes the current version on the layer's older Auto Mask strokes and leaves everything else as it was. Layer solo ("show only this layer / mask") is a view and never written to a recipe.

Dab version (2026-10-18, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation.rs` `BrushLine`, `DabTip::dab_version_2`): a brush stroke may carry `dabVersion`. Absent (every stroke before it) is version 1: dabs a fixed step apart along the path (Capture One's tip: r(0.2 + 0.8√s) for softness s, 0.77 r at hardness 50), the last one at the last whole step, so the stroke ends up to a step short of its last point; such strokes render byte-identically to before. `2`, which the editor writes for new strokes, adds a dab at the last point that counts for the share of a step the path covered since the dab before it (as an exponent on what the dab leaves uncovered), so a stroke reaches where it was released and a stroke being drawn reaches the pointer. A Capture One tip without Auto Mask also lays its dabs at most 0.4 of its falloff (outer − inner radius) apart, at least 0.1 of its outer radius and never farther than version 1, each counting for its spacing's share of a version 1 dab: the same mean coverage across the stroke as version 1, without its scallops along the edge. Lightroom tips (a dab at every point) and strokes drawn as capsules (no hardness, full flow, no Auto Mask) render as in version 1; Auto Mask strokes keep version 1's spacing and gain only the end dab.

Pen pressure (2026-10-20, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation.rs` `PenPressure`, `src/utils/penPressure.ts`): a brush stroke drawn with a pressure-sensitive pen while the brush's Pen pressure Size or Flow is on stores each point's pressure as `p` (0–1, three decimals) and carries `pressureSize: true` and/or `pressureFlow: true`. A dab's radius (Size) and/or flow (Flow) is the stroke's times the pressure at the dab, interpolated linearly between points; dabs are spaced by the step of their own radius (a pressure peak between dabs raises the last dab within half a step of it, else adds a dab weighted by its share of a step), and a stroke that would otherwise be drawn as a capsule is drawn with dabs of its feathered tip. Lightroom tips scale the dab at each point; Auto Mask fits each dab's model at its own radius. Absent keys and absent `p` mean full pressure: mouse and touch strokes never carry them (user decision D3: a mouse paints at constant pressure, strength comes from Flow), and every stroke without them renders byte-identically to before. Lightroom imports keep per-dab `r`/`f`/`h` changes by splitting a stroke into lines of equal settings (exact for Lightroom's one-dab-per-point tip), not through `p`.

Mask version and Refine edge (2026-10-18, `crates/engine/src/masks.rs` `MaskDefinition`, `crates/engine/src/mask_generation/precise.rs`): a layer (`masks[]`) may carry `maskVersion` and `refineEdge`. Absent `maskVersion` (every layer before 2026-10-18) is the 8-bit mask, byte-identical to before. `2`, which the editor writes for new layers, computes the layer's coverage in floating point and keeps it at 16 bits: radial and linear gradients without rounding their centre or coverage to bytes, sub-mask and layer opacity without truncation, and the grow and feather of `ai-subject`, `ai-foreground`, `ai-sky` and `ai-people` components in proportion to the photo's short side (not the cropped mask's, so the amounts mean the same at any crop or preview size), with a fractional grow. A version 2 layer whose every step is exact at 8 bits (brush, flow, colour and luminance ranges, depth ranges and AI masks without grow or feather, at full opacity, without Refine edge) keeps the 8-bit mask. `refineEdge` (version 2 only) is a radius in pixels of the full-resolution photo: within it of the layer's 50% line the coverage becomes the photo's own mix of the colours on either side — the averaged colours of the nearest pixels farther than the radius inside (foreground) and outside (background) the line, the pixel's linear-light share between them — where those colours differ and the pixel's colour lies near their line; elsewhere the mask is kept, fading back to it at the radius. `maskVersion` `3` (new layers from 2026-10-18 to 2026-10-20; the same mask otherwise) changes Refine edge on a mask thinner than the radius (its deepest point nearer the 50% line than the radius) where the foreground colour is itself a mix: for a pixel whose nearest sure background and the sure background on the far side of the mask (looked for up to four radii past it) have the foreground colour on their linear-light line, the side the foreground is mostly made of (by at least 0.1 of the mix) becomes the foreground and the other the background — a thin stroke inside a soft, defocused edge, with the subject's interior beyond it. Version 2 layers keep version 2's Refine edge. `maskVersion` `4` (new layers from 2026-10-20; otherwise version 3) grows and feathers `ai-subject`, `ai-foreground`, `ai-sky` and `ai-people` components on the mask grid widened by the photo around it as far as the grow and the feather reach (never past the photo's edges), then cuts it back: coverage just outside the crop grows and feathers into it, so a cropped layer shows the uncropped photo's mask; versions 2 and 3 grow and feather the cropped grid alone. Without the photo's size the grid is not widened. Refine edge needs the photo. The develop pass reads a 16-bit layer from a half-float mask array (steps of at most 1/2048 near full coverage); masks without a 16-bit layer stay in the byte array.

## Relight

`relightLights` holds up to four non-generative projected lights. Each has an
`id`, `enabled`, `type` (`sun` or `spot`), sRGB hex `color`, `intensity` (0–100),
`x`/`y` (pattern center, −100…200 percent of the developed frame), `size` (5–200), `rotation`
(−180…180 degrees), `elevation` (5–90 degrees), `scale` (5–300), `softness`,
`diffusion`, `shadows` (all 0–100), `pattern` (`none`, `blinds`, `window`,
`strip`, `foliage`, `grid`, `palm`, `branches`, `checkerboard`, `dots`,
`horizontal`, `diagonal`, `weave`, `noise`, `crosshatch`) and `invert`.
Optional `sourceX`/`sourceY` (−100…200 percent) position the light independently
of the pattern; optional `patternRotation` (−180…180 degrees) rotates only the
pattern. Legacy recipes fall back to `x`/`y` for spot position and `rotation`
for pattern rotation, preserving their rendered output. Missing lights are identity.
`sectionVisibility.relight=false` bypasses all lighting without losing settings.
`relightOriginalLight` (0–100, default 100) scales the existing illumination
when at least one light has nonzero intensity. Up to analysis version 4 it
does not reconstruct albedo or remove the original light direction; version 5
(below) removes the original light's shading and skin shine on the subject.

Optional `relightDepth` contains `{data, geometry}`: a 16-bit grayscale PNG data
URL at inference resolution, and the geometry parameters at analysis time
(engine `relight::geometry`). Analysis uses the displayed GPU input frame, after lens/perspective correction,
orientation, flips, straightening and crop. Geometry records non-neutral crop
and straightening too; older maps remain valid only in their original neutral
frame. A geometry mismatch disables depth until the user analyzes again.
`relightDepthAmount` (0–100, default 60) controls normal strength; zero uses
projected lighting. Surface gradients are computed once using an edge-aware local slope fit on 16-bit
depth. A local curvature heuristic reduces unreliable normals near short
silhouette transitions, without changing height or exact linear planes.
The result is packed in a GPU texture and reused for light edits. Depth is specific to
the photograph and excluded from copy/paste and presets; light settings are
copyable. Analysis version 2 additionally stores `faces`: up to eight arrays of
468 `[x, y, z, confidence]` points (x/y normalized to the displayed frame; z is
camera-facing relief in short-side image units). They refine normals and local
height without rerunning inference on light edits. `version: 2` opts into the
refined light response; legacy maps remain compatible. Each light may store
`distance` (10–300, default 60), the spot source's image-relative distance.
Facial geometry remains photo-specific and is excluded from copy/paste.
No model inference or network request is needed to reopen or export
a recipe containing a depth map. The feature does not synthesize photo pixels
and does not add an AI patch.

Analysis version 3 keeps the same depth and facial geometry and defaults
`relightSkinReflections` (0–100) to 25; older analyses default to zero.
An explicit value overrides either default. This global, copyable setting adds
view-dependent dielectric reflections only within confident facial coverage,
using cached normals, a fixed skin-like roughness and the light's colour.
It does not recover skin/material parameters or remove existing highlights.
Zero, missing faces, disabled depth and stale geometry suppress the reflection.

Analysis version 4 additionally stores `camera` with normalized `fx, fy, cx, cy`,
`inverseMin`, `inverseMax` and `reference` (currently 1). A PNG code `v` decodes
as inverse distance `inverseMin + (inverseMax-inverseMin)*v/65535`. Distance
is relative, normalized by the analysis median, not metres. Version 4 requires
valid finite camera metadata; unknown newer versions are not decoded as old
height maps. The cache key includes the camera. Gradients, perspective positions,
face relief, view direction, gobo projection, spot falloff and local shadows all
use this camera. Facial relief scales with the face's local scene distance.
Surface amount zero flattens this geometry onto its reference plane, retaining
the same light transport. Scene normals are regularized without smoothing across
large depth jumps; only confident facial geometry casts local shadows.

Analysis version 5 (new analyses since 2026-10-15) keeps version 4's fields
and smooths the scene slopes over a wide, depth-aware window when they are
loaded (monocular depth blotches backgrounds and skies). For a subject of at
least 5 % of the frame (BiRefNet matte) it adds `subject`, an RGBA PNG data
URL (8 bits per channel; 16-bit ones from earlier builds read the same) at
up to 1024 px per side and twice that wide: the left half holds
the subject normal's x and y (renderer frame: x right, y down, z towards the
camera), the matte and the normal's weight; the right half the share of the
photo that is the original light's skin shine and how far a new light reaches
the background (the inverse square of its distance relative to the
subject's). The subject, as a card at its median depth, casts its shadow on
that background (only the matte occludes). People
(faces, or skin in the whole-photo parse for profiles) take DAViD's normals,
objects the smoothed scene normals. `sourceLight` (nine numbers: ambient,
light, direction x/y/z, four zeros) is the photo's own lighting fitted on the
people's skin or the object's dominant material, scaled so the subject's lit
side has shading 1; a new light is divided by this shading, so it lights the
material (`I·L/S`) and Original light 0 % leaves only the new light, and the
skin shine is reduced with the original light. `subjectDepth` is the subject's
median inverse depth. Outside the matte the scene is lit evenly (no normal
response). Recipes of versions 2–4 render as before (byte-identical exports).

Since 2026-10-13: absent light fields render with a new light's values
(intensity 25, rotation 135, shadows 100, colour `#fff1db`; engine
`relight::DEFAULT_*`, parity-tested against `createRelightLight`). Earlier
recipes with fields missing therefore render with these values. The editor
writes `sourceX`/`sourceY`/`patternRotation`/`distance` explicitly when it loads
a recipe (same rendering). Optional light fields: `number` (stable display
number), `temperature` (Kelvin the colour came from; `color` stays
authoritative), `maskId` (limit the light to that mask layer; a hidden or empty
layer switches the light off, a deleted one no longer limits it), and
`pattern: "custom"` with `customPattern: {id, data, name}` — a 512×512 grayscale
PNG data URL made from the chosen image's central square (white transmits
light), copied with the light. A custom light without its bitmap is
unpatterned. `relightDepth.cacheKey` names an analysis: previews send its
bitmap and landmarks once and then only the name, as with AI patches; files
without a key are named by a hash of the bitmap when loaded.
